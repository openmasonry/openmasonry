# The Constitution of OpenMasonry

**Version 0.1 "Genesis".** This text is a *governed file*. Its SHA-256 is pinned in the genesis entry; from then on the Tyler refuses any change that is not pinned by a passed amendment (Art. VII). Articles or clauses marked *(procedural)* bind members but are not checked by the verifier (Whitepaper §8.5). The protocol rules referred to here are implemented in [`src/openmasonry/`](src/openmasonry/) and explained in the [Whitepaper](docs/whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md). Where this text and the code disagree, the code governs what the ledger accepts, and an amendment that removes the disagreement must be opened within 90 days.

---

## Preamble

We, who come freely, form an open fraternity of builders. We keep the forms that taught generations to improve themselves (the degree, the sponsor, the ballot, the lodge, the symbol) and refuse the conditions that turn fraternities into networks of hidden power: secrecy of government, discretion without rules, and power without end. We bind ourselves to the Seven Eternal Landmarks ([`canon/eternal.toml`](canon/eternal.toml)), which no ballot may change.

## Article I. Name, nature and independence

1. The fraternity is called **OpenMasonry**, the Open Craft.
2. It is a membership association with no shareholders. It issues no token, share or coin, and charges no fee for membership, degree, vote, office or access (Landmark III).
3. It is independent of, and not affiliated with, any grand lodge, Supreme Council or other Masonic body. It uses historical degree names as reference only and reproduces no ritual of any other body.
4. Its canonical record is the ledger in `ledger/entries/`. A copy that verifies from genesis and holds the same head is as good a record as the original; which chain is canonical is settled by Art. XI.5. The rooms of study (Art. XV) are not part of the record and carry no authority. No entry of the record is ever rewritten or erased; a wrong is repaired only by a new public entry (Landmark VI, Art. XVI).

## Article II. Separation of powers

1. **Legislative** power belongs to all active Master Builders (3°+), exercised by amendment ballot.
2. **Executive** functions belong to Lodge officers, maintainers (8°+), the Watch (30°+) and the witness nodes named in the ledger, each within the duty its degree unlocks.
3. **Judicial** power belongs to Arbiters (7°+), juries drawn by lot, and the Court of Review (31°+).
4. **Guardianship** belongs to the Supreme Council (33°), limited to Art. VIII.
5. No body may exercise the function of another. No person may sit in judgment of a matter in which they are a party, a sponsor of a party, or have voted as an elector. *(Procedural, except that the verifier excludes the accused from a sanction ballot and the candidate from their own advancement ballot and examination.)*
6. The **Custodian** (Art. XVI) is none of these four powers and no part of their permanent structure. The office is an interim guardianship: it makes no rule, holds no executive office of a degree, judges no one and decides no ballot. It may only repair by the corrections of Art. XVI, it is held in trust for the guardianship of section 4, and it ends as Art. XVI.11 provides.
7. An **office**, in the sense of Landmark V, is a seat a person holds by election, lot or naming: the Lodge offices (Art. IX.2), the Stewards of the rooms (Art. XV.7), the seats of the Supreme Council (Art. VIII) and the Custodian (Art. XVI.10). Each has a term. Any other seat a Lodge or a House fills by election or naming lasts one year (Art. IX.2). A duty a degree unlocks is not an office, and neither is a seat drawn by lot for one matter (an examiner, a juror), which ends with that matter. A witness is a key, named by the genesis or by a witness ballot, and removable by a witness ballot at any time (Art. XI.2).

## Article III. Membership

1. Any adult may apply. An adult is a person who is 18 years old or has reached the age of majority where they live, whichever is higher. The only conditions of admission the fraternity sets are adulthood, sponsorship, the signed Declaration, and the ballot (Landmark I). The ledger also requires the technical means of signing (a GitHub account and a key listed on it) and the waits of Art. III.4, X.4 and X.7; these are means and delays, not conditions on the person.
2. Admission takes five steps, in this order:
   - **Application.** The person introduces themselves in public, in their own words. An application is not a ledger entry, asks for no personal data and gives no right.
   - **Invitation.** A Master Builder who is ready to answer for the person signs an `invite` entry that names the person's GitHub login and the numeric id of that account, and thereby becomes the person's first sponsor (Art. IV). An invitation lapses if no petition follows within 30 days.
   - **Petition.** The invited person signs a `petition` entry with their own key, committing to the SHA-256 of the Declaration in [`canon/eternal.toml`](canon/eternal.toml) (`bin/om declaration`). A petition is valid only under a live invitation for that login. A petition not admitted within 180 days lapses.
   - **Second sponsor.** A second Master Builder sponsors the petition, where Art. IV.1 requires two.
   - **Ballot.** A sponsor opens the admission ballot (Art. VI).

   No one who is not a member writes to the ledger, except by a petition under a live invitation.
3. Membership is held by one natural person. Holding more than one membership, or operating an identity for another person, is fraud. *(Procedural, except that one GitHub account holds one membership.)*
4. A member may leave at any time by a signed `depart` entry. No suspension, correction or halt of the ledger prevents a departure (Landmark VII). A recovery of the departed member's key may be asked within 30 days of any departure, and later only as a recovery that follows one in time for it, as below; one asked before the departure goes on. A recovery is in time for a departure when it is asked before the departure (the latest of that membership, not yet applied, and alive at the departure or asked, stopped or voided at most 90 days before it) or within 30 days of it, or when it follows a recovery in time for that departure, whatever ended that recovery, within 150 days of that recovery's request or of the old key's stop of it; so one follow-up may follow another. A recovery in time for a departure reverses it once it is completed, since the ledger cannot tell the member from whoever else holds the key (Art. X.6), and a Council seat the departure ended comes back only into a vacancy; the member may then depart again with the new key. A departed member may be invited again after 90 days, and not while a sanction ballot on that record is open.

## Article IV. Sponsorship

1. Every candidate has two sponsors, both Master Builders: the one who invited them, and a second who sponsors their petition. During the Founding Phase the number required equals the number of active Masters, up to two, so the inviter alone suffices while only one Master exists; and, while fewer than seven Master Builders are present (neither dormant nor silent, Art. XII.5: throughout the Founding Phase, and later while fewer answer), one sponsor suffices once a petition has been live 30 days, so that no Master holds a door shut by never answering (the other Masters still vote on the admission). Only a sponsor of the candidate may open the admission ballot. While a succession is in force (Art. XII.5) the successors may invite and sponsor as Masters do.
2. A Master may hold no more than four sponsorships at a time, counting the candidates they sponsored who were admitted in the last 365 days and have not departed, their invitations that are live or lapsed less than 90 days ago (a lapsed invitation stops counting once another Master invites the same person), and the pending petitions they sponsor that have not lapsed or been refused. A Master under a sponsorship ban (Art. IV.4) can neither invite nor sponsor, and no Master may invite themselves.
3. A sponsor answers for the candidate's identity, adulthood and good faith, not for their opinions.
4. If a member is expelled for fraud (sanction measure `expel_for_fraud`) within 730 days of admission, each of their sponsors loses the right to sponsor for 730 days. A second occurrence is a grave offense (Art. X).

## Article V. Degrees

1. The degrees, their requirements and their duties are defined by [`canon/degrees.toml`](canon/degrees.toml), a governed file.
2. A member advances one degree at a time, up to the 32nd, after completing the waiting period, publishing the required masterwork, passing an examination by members drawn by lot, and a ballot of peers.
3. The 33rd degree is never applied for; it is conferred only by the lot of the Supreme Council (Art. VIII).
4. Degrees grant duties and eligibility, never additional votes (Landmark IV). The 3rd degree confers the legislative vote and the right to sponsor (Art. II.1, IV.1).
5. Examiners must judge the work against the degree's stated art and virtue, publish a short reasoned verdict, and declare any conflict of interest. A conflicted examiner must recuse, and the next member in the lot's order replaces them; where no one is left to replace them, the examination ends without penalty and a new one may be opened at once. *(Procedural, except that the verifier seats the replacement or ends the examination.)*
6. Only the candidate may open their own examination. An examination fails when half or more of its examiners report a fail, or when the advancement ballot it supported is lost on the votes; after a failure, a new examination for the same degree may be opened 90 days after the failed one was opened. An examination without a majority verdict 60 days after it was opened has timed out: it ends without penalty, a new one may be opened at once, and the examiners who never reported are left out of the next lot whenever anyone else can be drawn. An examination whose lot was not drawn within 7 days of its beacon round (the source of randomness was unavailable) ends the same way. A halt does not count toward these days (Art. XVI.3.d).
7. The examiners are drawn from the members of the degree sought and of every higher degree, then from all Master Builders, sponsors last (and, in a succession, the successors: Art. XII.5); where none of these holds as many members as the degree asks for, every member any of them holds is drawn together, and the number of examiners is lowered to them, in every phase. After an examination timed out, the next lot of the same candidate and degree seats every member any of them holds, never fewer than the degree asks for, so that every examiner who never answers is called. A silent Master Builder (Art. XII.5) is not drawn from among all Master Builders.

## Article VI. Ballots

1. Ballots follow the commit–reveal procedure and the decision function that the protocol core enforces (Art. VII.1), described in Whitepaper §6 and specified in SPEC §7.
2. Retaliation against any member for how they voted, examined or judged is a grave offense.
3. Vote buying, vote selling and coercion are grave offenses.
4. Every ballot has seven days to commit and three days to reveal, except an amendment ballot, whose opener may set two to thirty days to commit, and no fewer than seven while any of its electors is dormant, and one to fourteen days to reveal. The reveal window opens six hours and fifteen minutes after the commit window closes. A ballot may be closed (by a witness or a member) once its reveal window has ended, or as soon as every elector has revealed. A ballot whose votes do not reach its quorum lapses: it decides nothing and penalizes no one.
5. Only one ballot of a kind may be open on the same subject at a time. For an amendment the subject is the path and the text proposed, so a pending amendment never holds a path against another text.
6. Every eligible member keeps the vote, a dormant one included (a member is dormant once more than 365 days have passed since their last signed entry, a key replacement not counted); the quorum and the turnout of a ballot count only the electors who are not dormant when it opens, and, for an admission and an advancement, not silent either (Art. XII.5). Where a ballot falls to a wider electorate because too few of its electors are present, its own electors keep the vote: no fallback ever removes a vote (Landmark V). So when fewer than three are present on its first rung, an advancement is decided on the Masters' rung, and every member who had the vote on the first rung keeps it.
7. A ballot that lapses for want of quorum may be re-run once, within 30 days of its close, on the same subject and with at least seven days to commit. The re-run leaves out of its quorum and turnout the electors of the lapsed ballot who revealed no vote in it (and the dormant); every elector still holds the vote in it. A re-run is never re-run, and neither is a ballot that gave less than seven days to commit: if it lapses, it is opened again as a new ballot, which waits for every elector.

## Article VII. Amendments

1. A governed path changes only when a passed amendment ballot pins the SHA-256 of its new text, and the change is merged with exactly that text. For 30 days after the ballot passes, the previously ratified text also remains lawful, so that the change can be merged. The governed paths are the six pinned in the genesis entry and any path a passed amendment pins; a pin a passed amendment added may be removed by another amendment (it pins sixty-four zeros), a genesis pin never. An amendment fails if, when it closes, the path is no longer pinned to what it was when the ballot opened. Every degree table the ledger ever pinned stays archived as `canon/history/<sha256>.toml`, and nothing archived there is ever changed or deleted. The six are: this Constitution (`CONSTITUTION.md`); the degree canon (`canon/degrees.toml`), each new version of which is also archived as `canon/history/<sha256>.toml`; the Seven Eternal Landmarks and the Declaration (`canon/eternal.toml`), which section 4 puts beyond amendment; the Custodian's charter (`docs/governance/CUSTODIAN.md`); the rules of the rooms (`docs/governance/ROOMS.md`); and the protocol core, pinned as `@protocol`. The protocol core is the code that decides what the ledger accepts and the workflows by which the Tyler runs it on every proposed change; its files are listed one by one in a manifest that is itself one of them, so the list is part of what is pinned. Code outside the core (the command line, the members' and agents' tools, the witness node, other automation) is not governed: it changes by review under the Custodian's charter, and it must never decide whether an entry or a governed file is lawful.
2. Amendments pass by two-thirds of votes cast with the ordinary quorum and a turnout of at least one third of the active Master Builders who are not dormant (Art. VI.6). No halt pauses an amendment ballot, and no cap or waiting period of the ledger holds one back, except that one member has at most three amendment ballots open at a time.
3. An amendment that alters the decision function, the electorates, the sponsorship rules or this Article requires two passed ballots at least 90 days apart. The first is a first reading: it is taken on the proposed text placed at a path of its own, `first-reading/` followed by the governed path (for the protocol core, `first-reading/protocol.patch`, the patch that makes the change), so that its passing pins that path and leaves the governed path as it was. The second, which passes at least 90 days after the first, pins the governed path to exactly the text the first reading ratified, and only from it do the 30 days of section 1 run. *(Procedural: the verifier applies every passed amendment to the path it names and does not tell a first reading from any other amendment; members keep this order.)*
4. The Seven Eternal Landmarks cannot be amended. The ledger rejects any amendment that targets them. Changing them means founding a different fraternity under a different name.

## Article VIII. The Supreme Council

*Sections 1, 2 and 4 are enforced by the verifier as far as seats are concerned; the powers of 3 and the threshold of 5 are procedural.*

1. The Supreme Council has 33 seats, filled by verifiable lot among active members who have held the 32nd degree for at least 730 days and have never sat on the Council. Any active member may call the lot when seats are vacant; the eligible members are fixed at the call.
2. A seat lasts three years (1,095 days) and cannot be held twice.
3. By two-thirds of its seated members, the Council may:
   a. suspend a passed decision for up to 30 days and refer it to the Court of Review;
   b. convene an extraordinary assembly of all Master Builders;
   c. certify or decertify official mirrors, subject to reversal by amendment ballot. Witness nodes are added and removed only by witness ballot (Art. XI.2).
4. The Council may not admit, promote, sanction, spend, legislate or appoint. Its members vote in ordinary ballots like any other member.
5. Until the Council has at least 11 seated members, its powers under 3.a are exercised by the Court of Review, and under 3.b and 3.c by an amendment ballot.

## Article IX. Lodges and offices

*(Procedural until federation.)*

1. A Lodge is a group of at least seven members with a regular rhythm of study. Until lodges are federated, all members belong to Open Lodge No. 1.
2. Each Lodge elects for one-year terms, renewable once consecutively: a Worshipful Master, a Senior and a Junior Warden (all 3°+), a Secretary, a Treasurer and a Steward of Light.
3. Officers publish an accountability report at the end of their term.
4. Lodges may adopt bylaws that do not conflict with this Constitution.

## Article X. Justice, sanctions and mercy

1. Sanctions are graduated: **admonishment**, **suspension** (one year, after which it ends by itself), **expulsion**. Each is decided by a sanction ballot of Arbiters (7°+), or of all Masters where fewer than three Arbiters exist; a sanction ballot may also reinstate a suspended or expelled member. No sanction on a Master Builder, a reinstatement aside, is decided by a single elector: such a ballot is not opened where its electorate has fewer than two members, and it decides nothing unless at least two votes are cast.
2. Grave offenses are: fraud of identity or sponsorship; a second sponsorship of a member expelled for fraud (Art. IV.4); retaliation; vote buying or coercion; harassment or discrimination; deliberate falsification of records; abuse of a correction (Art. XVI.12); and binding or pressing anyone to silence about a room (Art. XV.4).
3. The accused has the right to be informed, to answer in writing, and to be heard before the ballot opens. *(Procedural.)*
4. An expelled member may be invited again one year (365 days) after the expulsion, and if admitted starts again at the 1st degree. A Prince of Mercy (26°+) must grant the request to return a hearing within 60 days *(procedural)*.
5. Disputes that are not offenses are resolved by an Arbiter or a jury of three drawn by lot. *(Procedural.)*
6. Losing a key is not an offense, and no one is sanctioned for it. A member replaces their own key by a signed `rekey` entry: the current key signs it and the new key proves itself. It may be done once in 30 days, also under suspension, a freeze or a halt, and a replaced key is never registered again; a key recovery, too, goes on during a halt. A member whose key is lost or in other hands recovers the membership through their peers, not through any office. After a Custodian has frozen the old key (Art. XVI.3), the member asks with a new key; two Master Builders, or as many other Master Builders as exist, co-sign that the person asking is the member; and 21 days of notice run from the request. A recovery never needs a Custodian: without a freeze (no custodian key is live, or no Custodian answers) the member asks all the same, three Master Builders co-sign, or as many others as exist, and none where the member is the only Master Builder; while another Master Builder who is not a sponsor of the member revealed a vote or reported in an examination within the last 365 days, or reached the 3rd degree within them, the co-signers are not all sponsors of the member; 30 days of notice run from the last co-signature needed, and the old key is frozen from that moment; and any other Master Builder may oppose the recovery by a signed entry, which is void when the oppositions reach one fifth of the other active Master Builders, rounded up, and never fewer than one. In either case whoever holds the old key may stop the recovery by a signed entry while it is open. The recovery that follows one the old key stopped cannot be stopped by it, and needs three co-signers, or as many other Master Builders as exist. Wherever the co-signers are fewer than the number asked for because fewer Master Builders exist, the notice is 45 days. A request that has been open 30 days, where the co-signatures given by then, counted as one at least where another Master Builder exists, are fewer than it needs, and, for a recovery asked without a freeze, whose notice had not begun by then, needs from then on only those already given, and never fewer than one where another Master Builder exists, and they may then all be sponsors of the member; its notice is 45 days. After a freeze, those 45 days start on that thirtieth day, and the old key stays frozen meanwhile; without a freeze, they start on that thirtieth day if the co-signatures then given are enough, and otherwise at the co-signature that makes them enough. Only the other Master Builders who revealed a vote or reported in an examination within the last 365 days, or reached the 3rd degree within them, count among the co-signers a recovery may need; one who answered nothing keeps the power to oppose it. Where none answered, the recovery rests on the member's GitHub account, the 45 days of notice, the old key's objection and the opposition of any Master Builder. A recovery lapses 60 days after its notice began, or after the request where no notice began, and a login asks once in 90 days, and not before 90 days after the old key stopped a recovery of it. A recovery reverses a departure when it is in time for it (Art. III.4), once it is completed. No correction and no ballot replaces a key. If the old key stops a recovery, the matter is also for the members, by a sanction ballot. Replacing a key one still holds needs no one.
7. An admission undone by correction (Art. XVI.4) leaves the record `voided`. That is not a sanction: it states no offense, bans no sponsor and records no fraud, and the person may be invited again after 90 days, and not while a sanction ballot on that record is open, like a member who departed. A correction never finds fraud. Where fraud is to be found and its consequences applied (Art. IV.4), that is for a sanction ballot: for 90 days after the undo, a sanction ballot with the measure `expel_for_fraud`, and no other, may still be opened on the voided record (Art. XVI.4.b).

## Article XI. Witnesses, the Tyler and infrastructure

1. The Tyler (continuous integration) verifies the full ledger on every proposed change, using the verifier of the base branch and never the proposed code, and refuses invalid entries, edits or deletions of past entries, and unratified changes to governed paths. To judge, it runs the protocol core (Art. VII.1) and no other code of the repository.
2. Witness nodes replay the ledger, close expired ballots, perform pending lots and sign checkpoints. A checkpoint covers every entry before it and states the digest of the whole state those entries produce; the ledger refuses a checkpoint whose digest is not the state's, so two verifiers that disagree on any stored fact part at the next checkpoint, in public. Witness keys are named in the genesis entry, or added and removed by a witness ballot of all Master Builders decided by the amendment rule. A witness key may be frozen for 30 days by correction (Art. XVI.3); while it is frozen it signs nothing, and members may still close ballots and perform lots.
3. The fraternity shall maintain mirrors on at least three independent hosts and anchor checkpoints publicly (OpenTimestamps). *(Procedural.)*
4. Anyone may run an unofficial witness or mirror to audit the ledger; only witnesses named in the ledger may sign entries.
5. The canonical location of the ledger is the one named by the ratified `src/openmasonry/network.toml`, a file of the protocol core, and not what any host says. Every clone and every witness remembers the last head of the canonical ledger it verified, and signs nothing on a ledger that no longer holds that head, that starts from another genesis, or whose last entry is dated ahead of the clock. The procedures for a lost key, a lost host and a flaw in the protocol are in [`docs/operations/RECOVERY.md`](docs/operations/RECOVERY.md). *(The memory of a clone is procedural: it is kept by the tools, not by the verifier.)*
6. The Tyler is a convenience; the verifier is the law. A merge made without the Tyler is lawful when the resulting record passes the pinned verifier, and each such merge is recorded in public (`docs/operations/RECOVERY.md`). A governed file found unratified in the canonical repository is an alarm, not a lock: it never stops a departure, a key replacement, an amendment or any other lawful entry. A change of the protocol core is merged only when the new core replays the record already kept to the same head and the same state. *(Procedural: the `candidate` check reports it; the gate does not enforce it.)* A rule that changes applies from a fixed future time or entry number written in the text the ballot pinned, never to what is already kept.

## Article XII. Founding Phase and the Founder's Sunset

1. The Founding Phase lasts while fewer than seven active Master Builders exist.
2. Founders hold the 3rd degree and no other rank; their honorary title of Founding Architect confers no power. As a member, no founder has any power another Master Builder lacks, save one fallback: any founder, whether or not that founder held the office before, may take up the office of Custodian while no custodian key is live, the Sunset has not come and fewer than three active Master Builders who are not founders exist (Art. XVI.10). The Custodian office is an office in the sense of Landmark V (Art. II.7), with a term, and not status in the sense of Landmark III: a founder's claim of it confers no degree, no vote and no weight. Until the Sunset a founder whom the genesis or a founder's claim names as Custodian also holds that office, with the powers of the Corrections article (Art. XVI) and no others. When a founder is examined, examiners are drawn from outside the founding cohort whenever enough other members exist.
3. The **Founder's Sunset** is the first entry after which the ledger shows seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses. From that entry on no custodian key signs anything, whether the genesis named it or a founder's claim (Art. XVI.11). At the Sunset the administration of the canonical repository and of the rooms shall pass to owners who are the witness operators. *(The end of the keys is enforced by the verifier; the transfer of administration is procedural.)*
4. Until then, the Custodian administers the canonical repository and the rooms, and acts only under the Custodian's charter (`docs/governance/CUSTODIAN.md`). The office gives no rank, no degree and no vote. In the ledger it has the corrections of Art. XVI and nothing else, and every moderation act it performs outside the ledger is recorded in the ledger as a `note` (Art. XVI.3), so that the ledger itself is the public moderation log. *(Procedural, except as Art. XVI is enforced.)*
5. **Succession.** The game never stops for want of a Master Builder. While no active Master Builder exists (a dormant Master, Art. VI.6, does not count), any active member below the 3rd degree may sign a succession claim. For the succession claim and for the lot of examiners from among all Master Builders, a Master Builder is also dormant while silent, and so is any member in the electorate of an admission or of an advancement, who keeps the vote (Art. VI.6): called to at least two examinations or ballots that ended in the last 180 days, they answered none of them. Two calls count as two only when they end at least 7 days apart. Only a ballot that gives at least 7 days to commit calls its electors, and only a revealed vote answers it (a sealed vote never revealed does not); a shorter ballot calls no one. An examination calls its examiners, and a report answers it. While a succession is in force, the calls of every member are recorded, and a successor who is silent is no successor: where every member of the highest degree present is silent, the next degree down stands in. A silence ends with one revealed vote or one report; recording a work does not end it. Any entry signed before 90 days have passed by a Master Builder who, after it, is active and neither dormant nor silent cancels the claim. Otherwise, from the 90th day until three active Master Builders who are neither dormant nor silent exist, the active members of the highest degree present, while that degree is below the 3rd, stand in for Master Builders in four things only: they are the last rung of the lot of examiners, they elect an advancement where no Master Builder is present, and they may invite and sponsor. They have no vote on an amendment, a witness, a sanction or a Custodian, and no objection; they never count for the Sunset, the Founding Phase or the number of sponsors an admission needs; and every dormant Master Builder keeps the vote.

## Article XIII. Data and privacy

1. The ledger holds logins and account ids, public keys, hashes, links and decisions. Personal content lives outside it; the ledger keeps only its hash, so authors can erase their content.
2. Members shall not record sensitive personal data in ledger entries.
3. The privacy of members is a trust. The fraternity keeps no secret but those Landmark II permits: personal privacy, private keys, a ballot while it is open, and a flaw not yet repaired. A room of study may be closed, but it is never secret (Art. XV).
4. The public reason of a correction, and a `note`, name no person beyond a login and hold no personal data: they are public forever and cannot be erased. *(Procedural.)*
5. The Custodian's private tools read only the sources published in [`docs/governance/CONSOLE.md`](docs/governance/CONSOLE.md). They never read what is written in a room, they keep facts about a person for at most 90 days, and any member may ask what they hold about their own login. No correction and no sanction rests on a fact that only those tools hold. The Console is a working tool of the Custodian and not a body: it decides nothing, and whatever of its output binds anyone is published. *(Procedural.)*

## Article XIV. Perpetuity

OpenMasonry cannot be dissolved by any ballot. Its members may leave and its code and history may be forked (Landmark VII). What was built in public remains in public.

## Article XV. Rooms

*(Procedural: the rooms lie outside the ledger, and the verifier checks nothing in this Article.)*

1. Each degree has a study room. A room is a place of study and not a body: no ballot, election, examination, hearing, appointment, spending or rule is made there, and nothing said there has force.
2. The teaching of every degree, and everything a member is examined on, is public. A room holds only the conversation, questions and drafts of those who study. Nothing of lasting value may exist only in a room.
3. A room is read by the members of its degree and of every higher degree who are not suspended, expelled or departed and whose admission was not undone. Access follows the ledger and is applied by a published program; no one is let in or kept out by choice. The one exception is a room hold of 14 days, which is a correction (Art. XVI.3): public, with a ground and a reason, and open to objection; a member is held again only 90 days after their previous hold began, unless other Custodians withdrew that hold. The roll of each room, as the ledger derives it, is public. On every run the program writes, and its workflow publishes to a public place the Observatory reads, the access the host actually grants (the readers of each room, the owners of the organization and every installed application) and the log of its runs, which names no person.
4. No one is bound to silence about a room. The one duty is privacy: no one publishes another person's personal data or unpublished work without consent. Reporting an offense, or an attempt to decide in private, is never a breach.
5. No one is discussed in a room they cannot read. No ballot, candidate, examination, case or office is canvassed in a room. Examiners and electors judge only what is public. Rooms are not for commerce, patronage or recruitment, and are never presented as holding secrets.
6. An offense in a room is judged under Art. X. Anyone present may put what was said before the electors and the accused; the ledger records its hash, the charge and the reasoned outcome, without the personal data of the person harmed. Nothing is deleted while a case is open.
7. Each room may have up to three Stewards, drawn by lot among those with access who volunteer, for one year. They keep order and have no other power. *(Procedural.)*
8. A member who leaves may take and erase their own contributions. The rules of the rooms are [`docs/governance/ROOMS.md`](docs/governance/ROOMS.md), a governed file.

## Article XVI. Corrections (interim guardianship)

*Enforced by the verifier, except where a section is marked procedural.*

1. **Nothing is rewritten.** No entry of the ledger is ever rewritten, reordered or deleted (Landmark VI). A wrong is repaired only by a **correction**: a new signed entry of a closed kind, made by a Custodian (section 10). Every correction states a ground of section 2 and pins the SHA-256 of a public reason, which is archived in the repository as `ledger/reasons/<sha256>.md`. The Tyler refuses a correction that comes without its reason, and a copy of the repository in which a reason is altered is unlawful.
2. **Grounds.** The grounds are closed: a key compromised or lost (`key_compromised`); an entry accepted through a defect of the protocol (`protocol_defect`); fraud of identity (`identity_fraud`); an attack in progress (`attack_in_progress`). Disagreement with an outcome is never a ground. A note (section 3.f) repairs nothing and states none of the four: its ground is `moderation`, which no other act states. *(The verifier checks that the ground of the act is stated. Whether the reason bears it out is judged by the members, through objection and removal.)*
3. **Immediate acts.** An immediate act takes effect at its entry, ends by itself, and needs one custodian key. There are five (a to e), and the note (f), which is signed by one key like them and has no effect:
   a. **Freeze a member's key** for 30 days, on the ground `key_compromised` only. The key then signs nothing but what section 8 keeps. A member's key is frozen again only 90 days after the previous freeze of that member began, whatever key the member then holds, unless other Custodians withdrew that freeze (section 4). A key is frozen only while fewer keys other than that key are frozen than one tenth, rounded down, of the active members who are not dormant (a limit never below one), those held by a key recovery (Art. X.6) included; the limit holds back freezes only, never a key recovery.
   b. **Freeze a witness key** for 30 days. The last witness that is not frozen is frozen only on the ground `attack_in_progress`.
   c. **Stay one ballot** for 14 days, once in the life of that ballot, and only before its reveal window opens. The time of the stay is taken out of the ballot's clock, so no window is shortened.
   d. **Halt the ledger** for 14 days, on the ground `attack_in_progress` only, and at most once in 90 days. During a halt no other ballot is decided and no lot is drawn; the ledger accepts only departures, key replacements, the entries of a key recovery, amendment ballots, the ballot that removes a Custodian, the entries of Custodians, objections and witness checkpoints. The clocks of the other ballots, of examinations, invitations and petitions stop, so no right runs out while the ledger is halted; an amendment ballot and the ballot that removes a Custodian run on.
   e. **Hold a member out of the rooms** for 14 days (Art. XV.3). A member is held again only 90 days after their previous hold began, unless other Custodians withdrew that hold.
   f. **Note.** The public record of a moderation act done outside the ledger, on the ground `moderation`. It changes nothing in the ledger, it is kept apart from the other corrections, and a Custodian signs at most ten in a day.
4. **Noticed acts.** Two acts touch a ballot, and they take effect only after notice:
   a. **Void an open ballot**, by a correction posted before its reveal window opens. The ballot ends with no effect and no one is penalized: a petition stays live, and an examination is free to support another ballot.
   b. **Undo** an admission or an advancement, by a correction posted within 90 days of the close of its ballot. The undo applies only if everything the ballot decided still stands exactly as the ballot left it, all of it or nothing. Nothing else is touched and nothing cascades: votes, reports and sponsorships given in between stand. An undone admission leaves the record `voided` (Art. X.7) and takes the person out of the examinations, sponsorships and invitations still open; no electorate changes, and a vote the person sealed is still revealed and counted. The person may be invited again after 90 days, and within 90 days of the undo may still be expelled for fraud by a sanction ballot (Art. IV.4). The undo of an admission is not applied while a sanction ballot on that member is open. An undone advancement takes back one degree and its examination, with no penalty. The addition of a witness is not undone by correction: a witness is removed by a witness ballot (Art. XI.2).

   A noticed act is public for **14 days of notice** before it can take effect. It is then applied by a further signed entry within the **7 days** that follow, or never, and every condition is checked again on the day it is applied. A ballot is the target of each noticed act at most once, unless other Custodians withdrew the earlier one. While the voiding of a ballot is pending, that ballot is paused: no vote is committed or revealed and the ballot is not closed, and the time of the pause is taken out of the ballot's clock. Where the genesis names more than one Custodian, a noticed act needs as many custodian keys as the genesis set. A correction that is in force or pending, immediate or noticed, is withdrawn by the Custodian who posted it, or by as many Custodians together as a noticed act needs; a freeze or a room hold withdrawn by Custodians other than the one who posted it leaves no wait behind, and a void or an undo so withdrawn may be posted once more; a halt and a stay are spent whoever withdraws them.
5. **What a correction never does.** A correction never admits, advances, appoints, reinstates or sanctions anyone, and never changes a vote, a key, a governed file, a Landmark or this Article. No correction stays, voids or undoes an amendment ballot, or the ballot that removes a Custodian. A correction may undo, freeze or pause; it never grants (Landmark VI).
6. **Recusal.** A Custodian never stays, voids or undoes a ballot whose subject, opener or sponsor is the Custodian's own member, and never holds their own member out of the rooms. No Custodian stays, voids or undoes a ballot that adds a witness or that advances a member to the 3rd degree: the office is recused from the ballots that bring its own end (section 11.b).
7. **Limits.** Beyond the bounds of each act, all Custodians together make at most five corrections in any 30 days, notes aside. Of those five each Custodian has an equal share: five divided by the number of Custodians whose key is live, rounded up.
8. **What no correction prevents.** Neither a freeze nor a halt keeps a member from departing (Landmark VII), from replacing their own key, from stopping a recovery of their own key, or from taking part in an amendment ballot or in the ballot that removes a Custodian; and a halt keeps no one from asking for a key recovery or co-signing one. A member whose key is frozen votes in every ballot: a freeze keeps no one from committing or revealing a vote.
9. **Objections.** Any active Master Builder may object to a correction by a signed entry, within 90 days of it, also during a halt. Three may not: the member of a Custodian who signed the correction, the member the correction is aimed at, and a member whose key is frozen. A correction is void for good when its objections reach one fifth of the eligible Masters, rounded up, and never fewer than one; the eligible Masters are the active Master Builders who are not dormant, other than the first two. Objections to a noticed act that was applied are accepted for 90 days from its application. An immediate act ends at that moment. A noticed act that is voided before it is applied is never applied. An act voided by objection is not posted again on the same target for 90 days. An objection made after a noticed act was applied is recorded and reverses nothing: the remedy of the members is then a new ballot, or the removal of the Custodian. A note cannot be objected to.
10. **Custodians.** Custodians are named by the genesis entry, three at most, and by a founder's claim. Each is named with a name that is no member's, a custodian key, a separate revoke key, and the login of the founder who holds them (the Custodian's member); both keys prove themselves when they are named. The office is no degree and carries no vote. The custodian key signs corrections and the sign of life, and nothing else. The revoke key has one power: to remove its own custodian key at once. **The founder's fallback:** whenever no custodian key is live, the Sunset has not come and fewer than three active Master Builders who are not founders exist, any founder, whether or not that founder held the office before, may take up the office, as often as needed, by a claim signed with their member key and sent from their own GitHub account, carrying the two new keys and their proofs. One claim is pending at a time; its name is a login nobody holds, and no one is invited under it while the claim is pending; its two keys were never registered. The claim is public for 30 days of notice; the objection of any one active Master Builder who is not a founder, made before the claim is applied, voids it, and no founder then claims the office again for 30 days; it is then applied by the founder within the next 30 days, every condition checked again that day, and a noticed act needs that one key. No founder against whose custodian key a removal ballot passed ever takes up the office again. With three or more active Master Builders who are not founders, the office is filled only by amendment. No other entry, ballot or lot creates a Custodian or gives one a new key.
11. **How the power ends.** A custodian key signs nothing, for good, from the first of these:
    a. **Silence.** 180 days pass without a signed sign of life from that key. A correction is not a sign of life.
    b. **The Sunset.** The ledger shows seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses (Art. XII.3).
    c. **Revocation** by its own revoke key.
    d. **Removal.** The active Master Builders remove the key by a ballot that any of them may open, the Custodian's member excluded from opening it and from voting. It passes by a simple majority of the votes cast, with the ordinary quorum. Its windows are fixed at seven days to commit and three to reveal; they run through any halt, and no correction touches it.
    e. **The term.** Seven years (2,555 days) pass from the genesis, or, for a key a founder's claim named, from the day the claim was applied, whether or not the Sunset has come.

    When a key ends, every act it had in force ends with it, and no noticed act it posted is ever applied. Where no Custodian exists, no correction exists.
12. **Abuse.** A correction made on a false ground, to gain advantage in a ballot, or against a person rather than against a harm, is an abuse of this power and a grave offense (Art. X.2). *(Procedural.)*
13. **An honest warning.** While few Master Builders exist, corrections are checked above all by publicity and the right to fork (Landmark VII), and only by as many members as can object. While the Custodian's member is the only Master Builder, no one can object to a correction or remove the Custodian; and where no Master Builder who is not a founder exists, no one can stop a founder's claim of the office.
14. **After the Sunset.** The intended path is a college of Custodians drawn by lot among the Master Builders, for a term. It exists only if the members add it by amendment; until they do, section 11 holds.
15. **In trust.** This power is interim. It is held in trust for the guardianship of Art. VIII and gives way to it: it founds no permanent body, it passes to no heir, and nothing in it limits the Supreme Council or the Court of Review. *(Procedural.)*
