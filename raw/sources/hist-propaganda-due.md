# Clip: Propaganda Due (P2), Italy

## Sources clipped
1. Wikipedia, "Propaganda Due": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Due (orientation; accessed November 2026).
2. Italian Parliament, *Commissione parlamentare d'inchiesta sulla loggia massonica P2* (Anselmi Commission), final report 1984, known via the article above; not consulted directly.
3. Italian Law no. 17 of January 25, 1982 ("Norme di attuazione dell'articolo 18 della Costituzione in materia di associazioni segrete e scioglimento della associazione denominata loggia P2").

## Facts
- Origins: *Propaganda Massonica*, founded in Turin in **1877** under the Grand Orient of Italy (GOI), a lodge for prominent men whose membership was kept confidential; banned with all Masonry under Fascism (1925); renumbered "Propaganda Due" after WWII. The 1960s–70s lodge run by Licio Gelli is described as only "tangentially related" to the original.
- **Licio Gelli** (1919–2015): initiated 1963; in 1970 appointed to represent Grand Master Lino Salvini over the lodge; Venerable Master from 1975.
- The GOI **withdrew the lodge's warrant in 1976** (Wikipedia; some accounts speak of a 1974 "demolition" decision and of Gelli's expulsion in 1981; **dates vary by source**). Gelli kept running it as an irregular, covert body until 1984.
- **March 1981:** investigating judges searching Gelli's properties near Arezzo (Castiglion Fibocchi) found a list of **962 names**: ministers and parliamentarians, the chiefs of all the intelligence services, generals and admirals, judges, bankers, industrialists and journalists. The revelation brought down the Forlani government (May 1981).
- **Anselmi Commission** (chaired by Tina Anselmi, set up late 1981, reported 1984): concluded P2 was a secret organization that had infiltrated state institutions to condition political life, a "parallel" power.
- **Law 17/1982** dissolved P2 and banned secret associations under art. 18 of the Constitution.
- *Piano di rinascita democratica* ("Plan of Democratic Rebirth"), documents found in 1982, outlined capturing parties, press, unions and the judiciary.
- Linked to the collapse of Banco Ambrosiano (1982) and the death of its chairman Roberto Calvi in London (first ruled suicide, later prosecuted as murder); courts in the 2020s identified Gelli among those behind the financing of the 1980 Bologna station massacre.
- Contested interpretations: whether listed people were all genuine members, and how far the GOI leadership tolerated the lodge, remain debated.

## Why it matters (note)
P2 is the canonical case of a closed network using the forms of a lodge (confidential membership, personal loyalty to one leader, no external audit) to become a vehicle of capture. It had already been disowned by its own Grand Orient before the scandal.
