## Facts
- **Prince Hall:** abolitionist and community leader in Boston; death 4 (or 7) December 1807. Birth year and place are **uncertain** (c. 1735 is often given; claims of birth in Barbados are unproven).
- **1775:** Hall and fourteen other free Black men, after being refused by Boston's white lodge, were initiated (traditionally on March 6, 1775) by **Lodge No. 441 (Irish Constitution)**, a military lodge attached to the British 38th Regiment of Foot. When the regiment left, they received a limited permit to meet as **African Lodge No. 1** (to march and bury their dead, not to make Masons).
- **1784:** the (Premier/"Moderns") Grand Lodge of England issued a warrant dated **September 29, 1784** for **African Lodge No. 459**, with Hall as Master. The warrant is commonly said to have reached Boston only in **1787** (secondary sources).
- **1791:** Hall is styled Provincial Grand Master; some sources date an "African Grand Lodge" to 1791, others to **1808** (organized by lodges of Boston, Philadelphia and Providence after Hall's death); **dates differ** between sources.
- **1813:** after the English union, African Lodge was dropped from the UGLE roll (no contact for years) but not formally erased. **1827:** the African Grand Lodge declared its independence from England. It later became the Prince Hall Grand Lodge of Massachusetts.
- **Recognition:** mainstream US Grand Lodges long refused to recognize Prince Hall bodies (racial segregation, plus arguments over "exclusive territorial jurisdiction" and the legitimacy of the 1784 warrant).
  - **October 14, 1989:** Grand Lodge of Connecticut is the first mainstream US Grand Lodge to formally recognize its Prince Hall counterpart.
  - **December 14, 1994:** UGLE recognizes the Prince Hall Grand Lodge of Massachusetts (a brief 1946 recognition by another body was withdrawn the same year, per one source).
  - By 2026 (Wikipedia): 47 of 51 mainstream US Grand Lodges recognize Prince Hall Grand Lodges; Mississippi, Louisiana, South Carolina and West Virginia do not.
- **Size:** Wikipedia cites "300,000+" members and 4,500+ lodges; the counting basis is unclear, so treat it as approximate.
