# Clip: Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), eight design principles

- **URLs:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom · https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Elinor_Ostrom’s_Eight_Commons_Governance_Design_Principles (cited in the whitepaper; 403 at capture)
- **Author:** Elinor Ostrom (1933–2012); Cambridge University Press, 1990
- **Captured:** November 2026 (modern work: summary/paraphrase only)

## The eight principles (paraphrase)
1. **Clear boundaries**: who may use the resource, and the limits of the resource itself, are defined.
2. **Rules fitted to local conditions**: restrictions on time, place, technology and quantity tied to local labor, materials and money.
3. **Collective choice**: most of those affected can take part in changing the rules.
4. **Monitoring**: monitors are accountable to the users or are users themselves.
5. **Graduated sanctions**: penalties proportional to the seriousness and context of the offense.
6. **Conflict resolution**: fast, cheap local forums.
7. **Minimal recognition of the right to organize**: external authorities do not challenge the institutions created by the users.
8. **Nested enterprises**: in larger systems, nested layers of organization.

## Context
- Based on field studies of long-lived common-pool resource institutions (Swiss pastures, Japanese forests, Spanish and Philippine irrigation, etc.).
- Ostrom was the first woman to receive the Nobel Prize in Economics (2009, with Oliver Williamson), "for her analysis of economic governance, especially the commons".
