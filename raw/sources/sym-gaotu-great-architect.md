## Key facts (summary)
- The Christian image of God as architect: Thomas Aquinas compares God to an architect in the *Summa*; John Calvin repeatedly calls God the "Architect of the Universe" in the *Institutes* (1536).
- The entry suggests that Anderson, a Presbyterian (Calvinist) minister, took the term from Calvin when drafting the *Book of Constitutions* (1723); there it serves as an allegorical name for the Deity.
- In Freemasonry the term works as a common denominator that lets members of different creeds share the same lodge; each projects onto it his or her own conception.
- Readings listed: Masonic (neutral/ecumenical), Christian (creator), Gnostic (demiurge), Hermetic (divine potential in each person), Rosicrucian (Heindel). The Swedish Rite, which requires a Christian profession, speaks of a "triune" Great Architect.
- Complement (source sym-regularity-ugle-1929): the Grand Orient of France dropped the requirement in 1877, giving rise to the "regular" vs. "liberal/adogmatic" split.
