## Old Charges
- The Regius Poem (c. 1390, 794 lines) is the oldest; the Cooke Manuscript (c. 1425). They contain a legendary history of the craft and rules of conduct for masons (cf. whitepaper §2).

## Anderson's Constitutions (1723), public domain
Six Charges: I. Concerning God and Religion; II. Of the Civil Magistrate supreme and subordinate; III. Of Lodges; IV. Of Masters, Wardens, Fellows and Apprentices; V. Of the Management of the Craft in working; VI. Of Behaviour.
- Charge I (1723 text): "A Mason is oblig'd by his Tenure, to obey the moral Law; and if he rightly understands the Art, he will never be a stupid Atheist nor an irreligious Libertine." Further on it obliges Masons only "to that Religion in which all Men agree".
- Authorship debated: some name Desaguliers and Payne as authors of the Charges, with Anderson as the hired editor. Anderson altered the Charges in 1738; Entick (1756) restored the 1723 text.

## Landmarks
- General Regulation XXXIX (1723) orders "the old Land-Marks" preserved without defining them. Biblical echo: Proverbs 22:28 ("Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set").
- **Mackey (1858)**, "The Foundations of Masonic Law", *American Quarterly Review of Freemasonry* vol. II, pp. 230–269: 25 landmarks. Abbreviated titles: 1 modes of recognition; 2 three symbolic degrees; 3 legend of the 3rd degree; 4 government by a Grand Master; 5 the Grand Master presides; 6 dispensation to confer degrees; 7 dispensation for new lodges; 8 making Masons "at sight"; 9 meeting in lodges; 10 lodge governed by a Master and two Wardens; 11 lodge tiled (guarded) when in session; 12 right of representation; 13 right of appeal; 14 right of visitation; 15 examination of unknown visitors; 16 no lodge interferes with another; 17 subjection to the laws of the jurisdiction; 18 qualifications of the candidate (a man, of mature age, without disability, freeborn); 19 belief in God; 20 belief in a future life; 21 Book of the Law in every lodge; 22 equality of Masons; 23 secrecy; 24 speculative science founded on an operative art; 25 the landmarks cannot be altered.
- Mackey's criterion: ancient, universal customs whose origin is lost in history ("immemorial").
- **Roscoe Pound (1911)** reduced them to 7 (belief in a Supreme Being, immortality, the book of the law, legend of the 3rd degree, secrecy, operative symbolism, being a free, adult man).
- American Grand Lodge lists range from 7 (West Virginia) to 54 (Kentucky). A 1954 bulletin counted 13 Grand Lodges that had formally adopted Mackey's list and 8 "by custom".
- Commission on Information for Recognition (Conference of Grand Masters, 1950s): three standards: monotheism, the Volume of the Sacred Law as lodge furniture, a ban on discussing religion and politics.
