## Facts and excerpts (public domain)
**Ballot (ch. III).** Haywood calls the ballot question "the most irritating of all subjects relating to lodge government and discipline". Most American jurisdictions require unanimity; English lodges required three black balls to reject; **French lodges rejected the candidate when one fifth of the votes were negative**. Secrecy of the vote protects the voter from pressure and spares the candidate. Abuses (voting on rumor or a personal grudge) are acknowledged. Conclusion: "the present system is as good a method of balloting as can be devised."

**The Lodge (ch. VII).** In operative Masonry the lodge was "the building, usually temporary, and often little more than a shed": headquarters, mess hall, tool store. The name passed from the building to the men. Symbolically it is "a mystical representation of the world".

**Apprentice's tools (ch. XXIII).** The 24-inch gauge symbolizes well-apportioned time (the traditional division of the day into three periods of eight hours: service to God and to those in need, work, rest). The common gavel "is a tool for shaping and not for breaking": it knocks the rough corners off the stone = vices and excesses.

**Fellow Craft's tools (ch. XXX).** Plumb = uprightness (citing the 1821 Webb Monitor: "walk uprightly in our several stations before God and man"); level = equality (citing Pike: "Masonry was the first apostle of equality"); square = upright conduct, with parallels in several cultures (the Chinese Great Learning, a 1427 gravestone, the Limerick bridge of 1517) and linked to Pike's "Doctrine of the Equilibrium".

**Ashlars (ch. XXXI).** Rough ashlar: "a stone, as taken from the quarry, in its rude and natural state" (citing the Monitor). Perfect ashlar: the same stone ready for use; "perfect" = complete. Readings: summum bonum (J. W. Lawrence); an upright, trustworthy man (old Lectures); a "power of self-recovery" (Emerson); Pike: rough ashlar = the people, perfect ashlar = a well-ordered State; Royce: the "beloved community".

**Pillars (ch. XXXIV).** Biblical sources disagree (Kings: 18 cubits; Chronicles: 35). Jachin ("He shall establish") and Boaz ("In Him is strength"), read by Mackey as strength and stability. Other readings: Providence (the pillar of cloud and of fire, Preston), Government and Religion (Caldecott), Space and Time (Covey-Crump), the tropics. "There were no globes on these Pillars, though the chapiters themselves were spherical". Hermeneutic principle: "a symbol that says but one thing is hardly a symbol at all".

**Liberal arts (ch. XXXIX).** Trivium (grammar, rhetoric, logic) + quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy). Medieval operative lodges absorbed them; in Preston's revision (18th century) they moved from the 1st to the 2nd degree, which became a "short course".

**Trowel (ch. XLVI).** Assigned to the Master: the Apprentice rough-dresses, the Fellow squares, the Master sets and joins. "the trowel and the cement spread on by the trowel, is the kindly, pervasive, irresistible spirit and power of Brotherhood."
