## Grand Orient de France (GODF)
- Formed in **1773** by a reorganization of the earlier Grande Loge de France (traditionally dated June 24, 1738; French lodges exist from the 1720s). Oldest and largest "adogmatic" grand body in continental Europe.
- **1877 Convent:** on a motion (vœu IX) carried by pastor **Frédéric Desmons**, the GODF made "absolute freedom of conscience" the first article of its Constitution and removed the obligation to believe in God and the immortality of the soul; references to the Grand Architect of the Universe were later removed from rituals and documents. How "strenuously" Desmons pushed is debated (Masonic Mouse).
- **1878:** UGLE resolved it viewed the step "with profound regret" and ceased recognition; this is the root of the split between "regular" (Anglo-American) and "liberal/adogmatic" (continental) Freemasonry, later codified in UGLE's 1929 Basic Principles.
- Active in Third Republic politics: support for the 1901 law on associations and the 1905 separation of Church and State. **Affaire des fiches (1904):** GODF lodges were found to have supplied files on the religious opinions of army officers to the War Ministry for use in promotions, a major scandal.
- Dissolved by Vichy in 1940; members persecuted.
- **2010:** convent left lodges free to initiate women (contested by internal procedures in 2011–12); the GODF does not call itself mixed.
- Size: ~52,000–55,000 members in 1,400+ lodges (figures differ within the same article).

## Maria Deraismes and Le Droit Humain
- **Maria Deraismes** (1828–1894), writer and campaigner for women's rights and secularism, was initiated on **January 14, 1882** by the lodge *Les Libres Penseurs* at Le Pecq, which declared itself autonomous from its grand body in order to do so (most accounts name the Grande Loge Symbolique Écossaise; one secondary summary names the Grand Orient; **unresolved here**); the grand body refused to recognize the initiation.
- Dr **Georges Martin** (1844–1916), physician and senator, supported her admission and proposed a mixed order.
- **April 4, 1893:** Deraismes and Martin initiate a group of women in Paris and found the *Grande Loge Symbolique Écossaise Mixte de France "Le Droit Humain"*, the first mixed grand body (the Co-Freemasonry article gives March 14, 1893; the British Federation page says "1883", apparently an error; **dates differ**).
- Deraismes became the first Grand Mistress; she died in 1894. The higher degrees were added around 1900–1901 and a Supreme Council formed to govern the Order (the British Federation page says 1900; other sources 1901).
- **September 26, 1902:** Lodge Human Duty No. 6 consecrated in London with **Annie Besant**; British Federation founded. The Order spread internationally (headquarters in Paris; federations worldwide).
- Principle: admits men and women equally, regardless of ethnic or social origin or religious, political or philosophical views. Not recognized by UGLE-aligned "regular" Freemasonry.
