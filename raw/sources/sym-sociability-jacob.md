## Jacob (1991): summary
- Thesis: the hundreds of lodges founded in 18th-century Europe were among the main enclaves in which modern civil society took shape.
- The lodge brought together philosophers, merchants, officials and professionals and created self-government in miniature: constitutions, laws, elections, representatives. Chapter "Creating Constitutional Societies"; it ends with the Grand Lodge of Paris and the reform of national government.
- Reception: some critics liken it to Habermas's studies of the "public sphere"; Masonic readers note a political emphasis at the expense of esoteric content.

## Context (general knowledge, not taken from these pages)
The concept of "sociability" as a historical object was popularized by the French historian Maurice Agulhon (*Pénitents et francs-maçons de l'ancienne Provence*, 1968).
