## Supreme Council (SJ)
- Founded in Charleston, South Carolina, in **May 1801** (traditionally May 31) at Shepheard's Tavern, by the "Eleven Gentlemen of Charleston", including John Mitchell (first Grand Commander) and Frederick Dalcho.
- Calls itself the "Mother Supreme Council of the World", the first Supreme Council of the 33-degree Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite.
- Its founding documents invoke the "Grand Constitutions of 1786" attributed to Frederick II of Prussia; that attribution is **widely doubted** by historians (general knowledge, not from the pages above).
- 1813: a Supreme Council for the Northern Jurisdiction is formed in New York; in 1823 the SJ ceded 15 northern states to it. The SJ today covers 35 US states.
- Nearly collapsed after the anti-Masonic wave following the Morgan Affair (1826): four members by 1844. Moved its residence to Washington, D.C. in 1870; headquarters House of the Temple (1911–1915), where Pike is entombed.
- Degree bodies: Lodge of Perfection (4–14), Chapter of Rose Croix (15–18), Council of Kadosh (19–30), Consistory (31–32); 33° honorary, conferred by the Council, which has at most 33 active members led by a Sovereign Grand Commander (21 holders since 1801).

## Albert Pike
- Dec 29, 1809 (Boston) – Apr 2, 1891 (Washington). Lawyer in Arkansas; represented Native American nations in claims against the federal government; Mexican–American War captain; Confederate brigadier general (1861), commanded the Indian Territory, negotiated Confederate treaties with tribes; his forces fought at Pea Ridge (1862); resigned the same year.
- Elected Sovereign Grand Commander of the SJ in **1859** (date given as January 2 or March 26, 1859 by different sources) and served until his death in 1891 (32 years).
- Rewrote the Rite's degree rituals and wrote *Morals and Dogma* (1871/72), a ~860-page commentary on the 32 degrees, long given to new members.
- Controversies: opposed Black suffrage and made racist statements; an 1868 editorial proposed a secret "Order of Southern Brotherhood"; some historians call him a Ku Klux Klan leader, but his 1997 biographer (Walter Lee Brown) found no reliable evidence of membership; **unresolved**. A forged "Luciferian" letter attributed to him originates in the Léo Taxil hoax (1890s), which Taxil himself publicly confessed in 1897.
- His statue in Washington, D.C. was toppled and burned by protesters in June 2020; Wikipedia states it was reinstalled in October 2025 (not independently verified here).
