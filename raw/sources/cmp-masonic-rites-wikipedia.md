## York Rite (American Rite)
- Not one body but a grouping of autonomous bodies. **Royal Arch Chapter:** Mark Master, (Virtual) Past Master, Most Excellent Master, Royal Arch Mason. **Cryptic Council (Royal & Select Masters):** Royal Master, Select Master, Super Excellent Master. **Commandery of Knights Templar:** Order of the Red Cross, (Passing Order of St Paul), Order of Malta, Order of the Temple.
- Royal Arch membership is required for the other bodies; the Knights Templar admit only Christian Masons. National body for the Templars: Grand Encampment of the United States.
- Name from the legend of an assembly of masons at York.

## French Rite
- Derived from the ritual of the "Moderns" Grand Lodge of London (exposed in *Masonry Dissected*, 1730); codified by the Grand Orient de France 1781–1786; four "Orders of Wisdom" integrated in 1786 (Élu, Écossais, Chevalier d'Orient, Rose-Croix); the name "French Rite" spread after the GODF's *Régulateur du Maçon* (1801).
- **3 Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees + 4 Orders = 7 grades**; a 5th Order (administrative/study) is cited by some bodies, giving lists of up to 9 grades.
- The GODF removed the obligation of belief in God and the immortality of the soul in 1877 (liberty of conscience); it works the rite in about 80% of its lodges.

## Swedish Rite
- Worked mainly in the Scandinavian countries.
- Christian: members profess the Christian faith.
- A single graded system of 10 degrees, with an 11th honorary degree in some jurisdictions.

## Rectified Scottish Rite
- Shaped by Jean-Baptiste Willermoz at the Convent of the Gauls (Lyon, 1778) and the Convent of Wilhelmsbad (1782).
- Christian and chivalric: the Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees lead to an inner order of Knights Beneficent of the Holy City.
