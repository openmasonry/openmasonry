# Clip: Albert G. Mackey, *The Symbolism of Freemasonry* (1869; 1882 ed.)

- **URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11937 (text: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11937/pg11937.txt)
- **Author:** Albert Gallatin Mackey (1807–1881)
- **Date:** preface dated Feb. 22, 1869; title page 1882. Public domain.
- **Captured:** November 2026

## Structure
31 chapters, from "Preliminary" and "The Noachidae" to "The Lost Word". Relevant: VII (operative/speculative union at the Temple), XII (Symbolism of Solomon's Temple), XIII (Form of the Lodge), XIV (Officers), XV (Point within a Circle), XVI (Covering of the Lodge), XXII (Symbolism of Light), XXIII (Corner-stone), XXIX (Symbolism of Labour).

## Excerpts (public domain)
- Preface: "the plumb is a symbol of rectitude of conduct."
- Ch. VII: "It is at this epoch, then, that I place the first union of speculative and operative Masonry." (Solomon's Temple as the legendary landmark)
- Ch. XII: the Temple "is the link that binds the operative and speculative divisions of the order."
- Ch. XIII: "The form of a masonic lodge is said to be a parallelogram, or oblong square"; the rectangle represents the world as the ancients knew it; the lodge is a symbol of the world.
- Ch. XIV: the three principal officers refer "to the sun at its rising, its setting, and its meridian height".
- Ch. XV: on the point within a circle, an ancient reading: "the point indicating the sun, and the circle the universe".
- Ch. XVI: "The lodge, as a representative of the world, is of course supposed to have no other roof than the heavens".
- Ch. XXII: light "was there, as it is now, in Masonry, made the symbol of truth and knowledge."

## Note
Mackey is an interpreter of 19th-century North American tradition; historians today regard his solar and "ancient" readings as speculative. Use as interpretation, not as history.
