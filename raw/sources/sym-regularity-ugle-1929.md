# Clip: Regularity and recognition: UGLE Basic Principles (1929)

- **URLs:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Masonic_jurisdiction · https://www.skirret.com/papers/gl_recognition.html · https://freemasonrymatters.co.uk/john-hamill-discusses-some-of-freemasonrys-most-cherished-principles/
- **Authors:** United Grand Lodge of England (1929 text); secondary summaries
- **Date:** Sept. 4, 1929 (published together with the Grand Lodges of Ireland and Scotland); accessed November 2026

## Key facts (summary; primary text not recovered in full)
- In 1929 the UGLE codified basic principles (usually counted as eight) for recognizing a Grand Lodge as "regular": legitimate origin; belief in the Great Architect/Supreme Being; obligation taken on the Volume of the Sacred Law; admission of men only and no relations with mixed lodges; sovereignty over the lodges under its jurisdiction; the Three Great Lights displayed; a ban on discussing religion and politics in lodge; observance of the ancient landmarks, customs and usages.
- UGLE position (summarized): to be regular, a Grand Lodge must meet *each* of the principles.
- Recognition is not automatic: it is a sovereign, bilateral act of each Grand Lodge.
- Example: the Grand Orient of France dropped the requirement of belief in the Great Architect in 1877 and is treated as irregular by the UGLE.
- The summaries differ in exact wording and count; check the UGLE Book of Constitutions before quoting clauses.
- Context (whitepaper §1.2): Prince Hall waited until the late 20th century for broad recognition; Le Droit Humain (1893, mixed) is not recognized by the "regular" mainstream.
