# Pillars of the Temple (Jachin and Boaz)

The two bronze pillars that, according to 1 Kings 7 and 2 Chronicles 3, Hiram set up in the porch of Solomon's Temple. Reproduced at the entrance of the lodge, they are among its most visible symbols. For Mackey, the whole Temple is "the link" between operative and speculative masonry.

**Traditional readings**
- **Strength and stability.** Jachin ("He shall establish") and Boaz ("in it is strength"). This is Mackey's reading.
- **Providence.** Preston linked the pillars to the pillars of cloud and of fire in Exodus.
- **Government and Religion** (Caldecott); **Space and Time** (Covey-Crump); the tropics of Cancer and Capricorn; birth and generation (rabbis and Kabbalists, the reading Haywood says he prefers). Passing between them would be a new birth.
- **Duality.** Common readings see in them the pairs of opposites (active/passive, severity/mercy) that the initiate must balance.
- **Historicity.** The biblical sources disagree on their height (18 or 35 cubits). The globes on top of the pillars are a late ritual addition, possibly by Preston. Haywood sums up: a symbol that says only one thing is "hardly a symbol".
- Another frequent triad, distinct from Jachin and Boaz, is the three pillars that "support" the lodge: **Wisdom, Strength and Beauty**.

## In OpenMasonry
- The **column** is the charge of the 5th degree, Steward of Memory (Gratitude; history and archives): "We stand on unmarked foundations; name them."
- The pair of opposites in balance is the theme of the 32nd, Master of the Royal Secret (Balance; double-headed eagle), whose "secret" is that "no one should hold it alone".
- In the architecture, the pillars are the **layers** that hold the system up: social (GitHub), protocol, witnesses and anchor (whitepaper §4.1). See [[chained-ledger]], [[byzantine-fault-tolerance]], [[opentimestamps]].
- The 24th, Prince of the Tabernacle, interpreter of symbols, adopts Haywood's principle: "A symbol is a question that each generation must answer again."
