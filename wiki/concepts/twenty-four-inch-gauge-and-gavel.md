# 24-Inch Gauge and Common Gavel

The working tools of the Entered Apprentice (1st degree). The 24-inch gauge is an ordinary two-foot rule. The common gavel is the hammer used to knock off the rough edges of the stone.

**Traditional readings**
- **Gauge = well-ordered time.** The 24 inches recall the 24 hours of the day. The traditional division splits the day into three parts: service to God and to those in need, one's usual work, and rest. Haywood generalizes: time is the opportunity to live, and wisdom lies in fitting time to life, judging each act by whether it ennobles or degrades.
- **Gavel = moral dressing of the stone.** For Haywood the gavel is a tool for shaping, not for breaking. Just as the mason knocks the rough corners off the stone, the Mason removes vices and excesses from himself to become a "living stone" of the spiritual building.
- **Gavel as authority.** The Worshipful Master's gavel, used to preside, is another reading (the power to open and order the work). From Haywood's viewpoint it should be read as an instrument of form, not of force.

## In OpenMasonry
- Time is literally the "price" of a degree: the **waiting period** (see [[waiting-period]]) measures in days the minimum stay in each degree, and `canon.minimum_days_to(n)` sums those periods. That is 90 days to the 3rd and 3,415 to the earliest possible seat in the 33rd.
- Time also measures influence: [[light-score|Light]] decays with a 180-day half-life, and 365 days without activity make a member [[dormancy|dormant]]. Members who stop giving time to the commons fade from the board.
- The gavel as dressing corresponds to the **graduated sanctions** (admonish → suspend → expel), always by ballot (see [[ostrom-principles]]). The gavel as authority is deliberately weak: no office is for life (Landmark V).
