# Level and Plumb

Together with the square, the tools traditionally associated with the Fellowcraft (2nd degree) and with the jewels of the Wardens. The **level** tests horizontal surfaces. The **plumb**, a weighted line, tests the vertical.

**Traditional readings**
- **Plumb = rectitude.** For Mackey, the plumb is the symbol of rectitude of conduct. The Webb Monitor (1821), quoted by Haywood, enjoins walking uprightly in every station. Haywood connects *rectitude* to the Latin *rectus* and sees in the plumb the inner faculty that judges whether one's character is "plumb".
- **Level = equality.** The level recalls the common ground that unites people. Haywood quotes [[albert-pike]]: Freemasonry was "the first apostle of equality". On this reading, everyone meets "on the level", whatever their social station.
- **The pair.** Haywood combines them: the plumb drives one toward self-improvement, the level calls one to share with others. In common readings, the vertical is the axis of conscience and the horizontal that of community.

## In OpenMasonry
- The **level** is the charge of the 2nd degree, Fellow of the Open Craft (Diligence; Logic, Arithmetic and Geometry), and the **plumb** that of the 8th, Intendant of the Works (Diligence in service; stewardship of shared work) (`canon/degrees.toml`).
- The level became a hard rule in Landmark IV, **One Member, One Vote**: a degree grants duties and eligibility, never voting weight. In `ledger.decide` each revealed vote counts 1 (the count is in `ledger._ballot_close`). See [[decision-function]].
- Landmark I, the **Open Door**, takes the level further than the tradition ever practiced: no one is refused for sex, origin, belief or class (see [[eternal-landmarks-of-openmasonry]], [[regularity-and-recognition]]).
- The plumb, as verifiable rectitude, corresponds to the [[chained-ledger]]: an entry is accepted only if the prior state allows it.
