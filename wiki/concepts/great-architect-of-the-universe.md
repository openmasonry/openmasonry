# Great Architect of the Universe (G.A.O.T.U.)

The allegorical name for the Deity in Freemasonry. The image of God as architect appears in Thomas Aquinas and often in Calvin (*Institutes*, 1536). It is thought to have entered the 1723 *Constitutions* through [[james-anderson]], a Presbyterian minister. In the lodge, the expression lets Christians, Jews, Muslims and others work together without defining *which* God.

**Readings**
- **Ecumenical (the regular current):** a common denominator. Each member projects their own faith onto the term, and belief in a Supreme Being is a requirement for admission (see [[regularity-and-recognition]]).
- **Deist / Enlightenment:** a watchmaker God, the rational orderer of the cosmos, compatible with the "religion in which all men agree" of Charge I (see [[old-charges]]).
- **Symbolic / adogmatic:** a principle of order, or an ideal of building, with no need for theistic belief. This has been the position of the Grand Orient de France since 1877.
- **Esoteric:** the Gnostic demiurge, the divine potential in the human being (Hermeticism), a Supreme Being emanating from the Absolute (Rosicrucianism).
- **Architectural:** if the universe has an architect, each person is at once stone and builder. This is the root of the ethic of work well done.

## In OpenMasonry
- **No belief is required or forbidden.** Landmark I, the Open Door, forbids refusing or ranking anyone for "belief or unbelief" (`canon/eternal.toml`). The Declaration signed in the petition does not mention God (the `[declaration]` section of `canon/eternal.toml`).
- The symbol survives as a **plural image**: the 24th degree, Prince of the Tabernacle, studies semiotics, "symbols, allegory and their many honest readings", and the 17th, Knight of East and West, studies comparative traditions ("No single tradition owns the light; each one carries a lamp").
- The **delta** (triangle), the traditional emblem associated with the G.A.O.T.U., is the charge of the 33rd degree, Sovereign Guardian. There it stands not for divine authority but for the virtue of **self-limitation**. See [[degree-symbols-and-virtues]].
- The place of the "Architect" as the source of order is taken, without any metaphysical claim, by **verifiable law**: the order of the system comes from public rules, published as text and as code wherever the ledger enforces them, whose whole history anyone can replay (Landmark VI).
