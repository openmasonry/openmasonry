# Trowel

The mason's trowel, which spreads the mortar and joins the stones into a single structure. In many Anglo-American rites it is the proper working tool of the Master Mason (3rd degree).

**Traditional readings**
- **The cement of brotherhood.** For Haywood, the trowel and the cement it spreads stand for the kindly, pervasive and irresistible spirit of Brotherhood. The division of labor has a meaning: the Apprentice roughs out the stone, the Fellowcraft squares it, the Master **sets** it and binds it into the building. The stones "that were many" become one.
- **Brotherly love and concord.** The manuals speak of spreading the "cement of brotherly love" that binds the members into a society of friends.
- **Rebuilding.** The biblical image of Nehemiah 4:17 (the builders of the walls of Jerusalem, one hand on the work and the other holding a weapon) links the trowel to the sword in the rebuilding degrees of the Scottish Rite.

## In OpenMasonry
- The lesson of the 15th degree, Knight of the East (the Rebuilder), updates the image: "Build with a trowel in one hand and a backup in the other" (`canon/degrees.toml`, charge `sword`). The art of the degree is resilience: backups, mirrors, recovery and continuity.
- The "cement" is what binds the entries: each one carries `prev = H(eᵢ₋₁)` and an SSH signature, and the stones "that were many" become a single, tamper-evident chain. See [[chained-ledger]].
- Sponsorship is the social mortar: only Masters (3rd+) sign `vouch`, and each answers for the candidates they bring in (see [[sybil-resistance]]).
