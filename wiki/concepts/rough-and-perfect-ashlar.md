# Rough and Perfect Ashlar

Two stones displayed in the lodge: the **rough ashlar**, "as taken from the quarry, in its rude and natural state", and the **perfect ashlar** (or cubic stone), the same stone made ready for the building. The square tests both.

**Traditional readings**
- **Moral and individual.** The rough ashlar is the apprentice who arrives from the "quarries of humanity". The perfect ashlar is the upright, trustworthy man of the old *Lectures*. Haywood insists that "perfect" means **complete**, not flawless.
- **Purpose.** For J. W. Lawrence, the perfect ashlar is the *summum bonum* of Freemasonry. Emerson, quoted by Haywood, speaks of the "power of self-recovery".
- **Political.** [[albert-pike]] reads the rough ashlar as the people and the perfect one as the well-ordered state. Josiah Royce adds the idea of a "beloved community".
- **Cosmological.** The cube, venerated "from the earliest ages", would represent the immensity of space.
- There is also a controversy over the "broached thurnel", which Mackey identified with the perfect ashlar, against what the old *Lectures* say.

## In OpenMasonry
- The 1st degree, Apprentice of the Open Craft, has the **rough ashlar** as its charge (`charge = "rough-ashlar"`, on a plain argent field) and Humility as its virtue. The degree's lesson, "Before you shape the stone, learn to see the stone that you are", opens the whitepaper.
- Dressing the stone is not a private act: every step requires a public **masterwork** (see [[masterwork]]). The polishing is therefore recorded and verifiable in the [[chained-ledger]].
- The whitepaper extends Pike's political reading to the **commons**: the perfect ashlar is also the improved repository (§11.2, "the fraternity builds its own infrastructure").
