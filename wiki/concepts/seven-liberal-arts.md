# The Seven Liberal Arts

The curriculum of "liberal" education (fit for a free person) since late Antiquity, which the Middle Ages organized into two cycles: the **trivium** (grammar, rhetoric, logic), the arts of the word, and the **quadrivium** (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy), the arts of number. In Masonic tradition they appear in the Fellowcraft degree, illustrated by a staircase of seven steps.

**History and readings**
- Haywood reports that the medieval operative lodges already claimed these arts. A 14th-century London regulation is said to have required masters to know them.
- In William Preston's ritual revision (18th century), the arts moved from the 1st to the 2nd degree, which became a "short course" of instruction.
- **Humanist reading:** through them the guild aims to be more than a trade body. Even modest knowledge makes a brother more useful, tolerant and humble.
- **Geometric reading:** Geometry, identified with Masonry itself in the Old Charges, holds the place of honor (see [[old-charges]]).
- **Contemplative reading:** the climb up the seven steps represents the path of the intellect toward contemplation.

## In OpenMasonry
The arts become **method**, not ornament, spread across the three Craft degrees (whitepaper §11.1; `canon/degrees.toml`):

| Degree | Arts | Virtue |
|---|---|---|
| 1st Apprentice | Grammar and Rhetoric: reading the work and speaking clearly | Humility |
| 2nd Fellow | Logic, Arithmetic and Geometry | Diligence |
| 3rd Master Builder | Music and Astronomy: harmony, cycles and the measure of a life | Fidelity |

From the 4th degree on, each degree studies **one** contemporary art: information ethics, archives, cryptography, dispute resolution and so on, up to the balance of powers. The study produces a public [[masterwork]], and the fraternity grows "as a side effect" of its members' education. See [[degree-symbols-and-virtues]].
