# Square and Compasses

The best-known emblem of Freemasonry: a square and a pair of compasses interlaced. In many jurisdictions it often appears with the letter G in the center. Both are drawing tools of the operative mason: the square tests right angles, the compasses describe circles and transfer measurements.

**Traditional readings** (interpretations, not dogma)
- **Square = upright conduct.** Haywood finds the square as an image of rectitude in very different cultures, from the Chinese *Great Learning* to a bridge inscription in Limerick (1517). He links it to [[albert-pike]]'s "Doctrine of the Equilibrium", that is, to the balance of opposing forces.
- **Compasses = measure and limit.** The 19th-century American manuals say the compasses serve to "circumscribe" our desires and keep our passions within due bounds. This is self-mastery.
- **The pair.** A frequent reading: the square (earth, the rectilinear) stands for our relationship with others, and the compasses (heaven, the circular) for our relationship with ourselves and with the transcendent. According to some interpreters, the relative position of the two tools marks the initiate's progress. There are also alchemical and geometric readings of the same pair, and none of them is official.

## In OpenMasonry
- The pair is split across the heraldry: the **compasses** are the charge of the 3rd degree, Master Builder (`charge = "compass"`), and the **square** that of the 12th, Master Architect (`charge = "square"`), both with a chevron (`canon/degrees.toml`). The virtue of the 3rd is Fidelity. That of the 12th is Prudence, with system design as its art.
- The ethic of the square (acting uprightly and verifiably) became a mechanical rule: every entry of the [[chained-ledger]] is checked by `rules(Sᵢ₋₁, eᵢ)`, and no "exception to the rule" is possible without breaking verification.
- The compasses as a limit echo Landmark V, "No Permanent Power" (see [[eternal-landmarks-of-openmasonry]]), and the virtue of the 33rd degree, **self-limitation**.
