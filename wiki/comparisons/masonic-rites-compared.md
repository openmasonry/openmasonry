# Masonic rites compared

A **rite** is a system of degrees and ceremonies. All regular rites share the **3 Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees** (Entered Apprentice, Fellowcraft, Master Mason), conferred in the lodge. The difference lies in the **higher degrees**, which each rite organizes into its own bodies. None of these degrees sits "above" the Master Mason in authority.

## Overview

| Rite | Origin | No. of degrees | Bodies (high degrees) | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **AASR (Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite)** | Expanded from the Rite of Perfection (25 degrees, 1762); first Supreme Council in Charleston, 1801 | 33 | **SJ:** Lodge of Perfection (4–14), Chapter of Rose Croix (15–18), Council of Kadosh (19–30), Consistory (31–32), Supreme Council (33). **NMJ:** Perfection (4–14), Princes of Jerusalem (15–16), Rose Croix (17–18), Consistory (19–32) | The most widespread high-degree rite in the world |
| **York Rite (American)** | Legend of an assembly at York; American system consolidated between the late 18th and the 19th century | 3 + ~10 | **Royal Arch Chapter** (Mark Master, Past Master, Most Excellent Master, Royal Arch). **Cryptic Council** (Royal, Select, Super Excellent Master). **Commandery of Knights Templar** (Red Cross, Malta, Temple) | Autonomous bodies with no single "top". The Templars require Christian faith |
| **French Rite** | Ritual of the London "Moderns"; codified by the Grand Orient of France between 1781 and 1786 | 3 + 4 Orders = 7 (up to 9 with the 5th Order) | Orders of Wisdom: Elect, Scottish, Knight of the East, Rose Croix | Liberal. The Grand Orient de France dropped compulsory belief in God in 1877 |
| **Swedish Rite** | Scandinavia | 10 (an 11th, honorary, in some jurisdictions) | One graded system, not separate bodies | Christian |
| **Rectified Scottish Rite** | J.-B. Willermoz; Convents of Lyon (1778) and Wilhelmsbad (1782) | Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees, then an inner chivalric order | Knights Beneficent of the Holy City | Christian |
| **Emulation (English Craft working)** | Emulation Lodge of Improvement, London, 1823 | **3** | None within the working; the Holy Royal Arch and other orders are separate | The most common working of the Craft degrees under the United Grand Lodge of England (UGLE), taught word for word by the Lodge of Improvement |

> **Mind the sources.** The figures above come from the sources listed in the frontmatter; the Swedish, Rectified Scottish and Emulation rows are kept to the broad, well-attested facts.

## Underlying differences

- **Hierarchy vs. federation.** The AASR has one Supreme Council per country (or jurisdiction) governing degrees 4–33. The American York Rite is a federation of bodies with no single top. The French Rite concentrates the high degrees in a Grand Chapter tied to the grand body that works it. The Swedish Rite is one graded system run by a single national body.
- **Belief.** Templar York, the Swedish Rite and the Rectified Scottish Rite require Christian faith. The AASR and symbolic York require belief in a Supreme Being. The liberal French Rite requires no belief.
- **Quantity is not prestige.** Emulation, the most common working under UGLE, stops at the 3 Craft degrees; any further degrees belong to separate orders that a Master Mason may join or not.

## Where OpenMasonry stands

OpenMasonry adopts the **numbering and the House architecture of the AASR-SJ** because it is the most publicly documented 33-degree system. It changes the content of each degree, however: each becomes an art and a verifiable duty ([[catalog-of-the-33-degrees]]). Like every regular rite, it holds that the 3° is already a full member. Like the liberal French Rite, it requires no belief (Landmark I).
