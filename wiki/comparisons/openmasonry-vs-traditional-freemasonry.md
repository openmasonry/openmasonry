# OpenMasonry vs. traditional Freemasonry

OpenMasonry **keeps the forms** of Freemasonry (degrees, sponsors, ballot, lodge, the builder's symbol) and **removes the three conditions that, according to the whitepaper, enable capture**: secrecy, discretion and permanence (§1.3). It is **not** a grand lodge or other Masonic body and is not recognized by any grand lodge or Supreme Council (§12.1).

| Aspect | Traditional Freemasonry ("regular" mainstream) | OpenMasonry |
|---|---|---|
| Who may join | Generally only adult men who believe in a Supreme Being. There are mixed and women's grand lodges (for example, *Le Droit Humain*, 1893), generally not recognized by the mainstream | Any adult, regardless of sex, origin, belief or unbelief (Landmark I) |
| How one joins | Invitation or request, sponsors, a background check and a secret ballot. In the USA, unanimity is usually required | Application in a public issue, invitation by a Master Builder (the first sponsor), signed petition on GitHub, a second Master sponsor and a *commit–reveal* ballot. One fifth of black balls rejects ([[ballot-and-blackball]]) |
| Cost | Initiation fee, dues and per-degree fees (vary by grand lodge) | None. No fees, no token, no buying of degrees or access (Landmark III) |
| Degree structure | 3 Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees. High degrees depend on the rite: 33 in the AASR, about 10 in York, 7 in the French Rite ([[masonic-rites-compared]]) | 33 degrees in 6 Houses, referencing the AASR-SJ ([[catalog-of-the-33-degrees]]) |
| What a degree grants | Honor, study and, in the higher bodies, access to offices. The 33° is honorary and comes by invitation | Only **duties and eligibility**, never extra votes (Landmark IV) |
| Ritual | Private. Words, signs and oaths are reserved | Original, public ceremonies. No one else's ritual is used or revealed (§11.3) |
| Closed spaces | The tyled lodge: what happens in a meeting is reserved to members | One study room per degree, closed but never secret: its existence, rules and roll are public, nothing said in it has force and no one owes silence about it (Landmark II, §10.6; see [[rooms]]). The rooms opened at the public launch |
| Records | Private minutes and registers. Members cannot verify ballots or promotions | Public ledger, signed and hash-chained, which anyone can replay offline ([[chained-ledger]]). No record is ever rewritten or erased; a wrong is repaired only by a new public entry (Landmark VI) |
| Offices | Variable terms. Supreme Councils with life or long-serving active members | One-year terms, renewable once. Supreme Council by lot, 3 years, once in a lifetime |
| Founder and exceptional power | A Grand Master holds prerogatives of office, which Mackey counts among the landmarks and which vary by jurisdiction ([[landmarks]]) | As a member, no founder has any power another Master Builder lacks, save the fallback of taking up the Custodian office again while no custodian key is live and few other Masters exist. Until the Sunset a founder named Custodian (by the genesis, which at the founding ceremony names the first founder, or by a founder's claim) also holds that office, with the powers of the Corrections article and no others: public, signed corrections that may undo, freeze or pause and never grant, which the Masters stop by objection ([[custodian-and-corrections]], [[founder-sunset]]) |
| Time to the top | Depends on the grand lodge. In the USA, degrees 4–32 are often conferred in concentrated meetings of a few days; other grand lodges require waiting periods between degrees | Minimum of 3,415 days (≈ 9.4 years) before one can enter the lot for the 33° ([[waiting-period]]) |
| Leaving | Request a demit (certificate of good standing) or resign. The records stay with the grand lodge | Free to leave, with one's own records, and a right to fork (Landmark VII) |
| Recognition | Regularity and mutual recognition among grand lodges ([[united-grand-lodge-of-england]]) | Legitimacy is earned in public, under verifiable rules |

## What is inherited on purpose

- The degree as curriculum: the [[seven-liberal-arts]] in degrees 1–3.
- Sponsorship as reputation put at stake.
- The ballot, with the one-fifth rule from historical French practice.
- The idea of immutable [[landmarks]].
- The guard at the door, the [[tyler]].
- The thesis that the 3° is already a full member and that the high degrees are study, not command. The SJ valleys themselves say so ([[deg-sj-valley-degree-list]]).

## What is left out

Secret governance, restrictions by sex or creed, lifetime offices and all existing ritual text.

## What is kept with open eyes

One office with a power no other member holds: the Custodian, until the Sunset. It is the place where OpenMasonry is closest to the prerogative of a Grand Master, and it differs in four ways: its acts are a closed list, each is public with its reason, the Master Builders can stop one by objection and remove the holder by simple majority, and the office ends by a rule of the ledger, seven years after it was named at the latest. While few Master Builders exist, corrections are checked mostly by publicity and the right to fork.
