## Overview
- [[overview]]: **OpenMasonry overview**. OpenMasonry is an open, token-free fraternity of 33 degrees that anyone can verify, running on GitHub.

## Entities
- [[albert-mackey]]: **Albert Gallatin Mackey**. Physician from Charleston, South Carolina, and the foremost American Masonic jurist and encyclopedist of the nineteenth century.
- [[albert-pike]]: **Albert Pike**. Lawyer, poet, soldier and the most influential leader of the **Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite** in the United States.
- [[david-stevenson]]: **David Stevenson**. Scottish historian, professor emeritus at the University of St Andrews, specialist in seventeenth-century Scotland.
- [[elinor-ostrom]]: **Elinor Ostrom**. American political scientist and economist at Indiana University (Bloomington).
- [[grand-orient-de-france]]: **Grand Orient de France (GODF)**. The oldest French Masonic body (1773) and the largest "adogmatic" one in continental Europe.
- [[james-anderson]]: **James Anderson**. Scottish Presbyterian minister settled in London.
- [[jean-theophile-desaguliers]]: **Jean Théophile Desaguliers**. Natural philosopher, Anglican clergyman and a central figure of the first Grand Lodge of London.
- [[le-droit-humain]]: **Le Droit Humain (International Order of Mixed Freemasonry)**. The International Order of Mixed Freemasonry, the first Masonic order to admit **men and women on equal terms**.
- [[margaret-jacob]]: **Margaret C. Jacob**. American historian of the Enlightenment and of science, professor emerita at UCLA.
- [[maria-deraismes]]: **Maria Deraismes**. French writer, public speaker and campaigner for women's rights and secularism.
- [[p2-lodge]]: **Propaganda Due (P2) Lodge**. An Italian lodge, originally under the Grand Orient of Italy (GOI), which under Licio Gelli became a **secret network of parallel power**.
- [[prince-hall-freemasonry]]: **Prince Hall Freemasonry**. A branch of American Freemasonry founded by and for African Americans, originating in the lodge of Prince Hall in Boston.
- [[prince-hall]]: **Prince Hall**. Boston community leader and abolitionist.
- [[supreme-council-aasr-southern-jurisdiction]]: **Supreme Council of the AASR, Southern Jurisdiction (USA)**. The world's first Supreme Council of the AASR, founded in Charleston in **May 1801** (traditionally May 31).
- [[united-grand-lodge-of-england]]: **United Grand Lodge of England (UGLE)**. The governing body of "regular" Freemasonry in England, Wales and overseas districts.
- [[william-schaw]]: **William Schaw**. Master of Works to King James VI of Scotland from 1583, with authority over the royal buildings.

- [[great-architect-of-the-universe]]: **Great Architect of the Universe**. The allegorical name for the Deity in Freemasonry.
- [[landmarks]]: **Landmarks**. The "ancient landmarks" of Freemasonry: principles that, by definition, no Masonic authority can change.
- [[level-and-plumb]]: **Level and Plumb**. Together with the square, the tools traditionally associated with the Fellowcraft (2nd degree) and with the jewels of the Wardens.
- [[light-score]]: **Light (a decaying score)**. Light is OpenMasonry's public measure of a member's recent service: a sum of signed activity that halves every 180 days.
- [[lodge]]: **Lodge**. The basic unit of Freemasonry.
- [[masterwork]]: **Masterwork**. In the operative craft, a journeyman proved his skill by presenting a *masterwork* to the masters of his trade before being admitted among them.
- [[old-charges]]: **Old Charges**. The medieval and early modern manuscripts of the English and Scottish stonemasons.
- [[opentimestamps]]: **OpenTimestamps (anchoring)**. Peter Todd's open protocol (2016) for proving that a piece of data existed **before** a given date, by anchoring its hash in the Bitcoin blockchain.

- [[pillars-of-the-temple]]: **Pillars of the Temple (Jachin and Boaz)**. The two bronze pillars that, according to 1 Kings 7 and 2 Chronicles 3, Hiram set up in the porch of Solomon's Temple.
- [[regularity-and-recognition]]: **Regularity and Recognition**. Two distinct but linked concepts.
- [[rooms]]: **Rooms of Study**. Each of the 33 degrees has a room: a closed place where the people who study that degree talk, ask questions, share drafts and mentor one another.
- [[rough-and-perfect-ashlar]]: **Rough and Perfect Ashlar**. Two stones displayed in the lodge: the **rough ashlar**, "as taken from the quarry, in its rude and natural state", and the **perfect ashlar** (or cubic…
- [[seven-liberal-arts]]: **The Seven Liberal Arts**. The curriculum of "liberal" education (fit for a free person) since late Antiquity, which the Middle Ages organized into two cycles: the **trivium**…
- [[square-and-compasses]]: **Square and Compasses**. The best-known emblem of Freemasonry: a square and a pair of compasses interlaced.
- [[sybil-resistance]]: **Sybil Resistance**. In a Sybil attack, someone creates many fake identities to outvote the honest members.
- [[tracing-board]]: **Tracing Board**. A degree's tracing board is a painted panel, or a drawing, that gathers the symbols of a degree: pillars, ashlars, tools, a ladder, heavenly bodies.
- [[trowel]]: **Trowel**. The mason's trowel, which spreads the mortar and joins the stones into a single structure.
- [[twenty-four-inch-gauge-and-gavel]]: **24-Inch Gauge and Common Gavel**. The working tools of the Entered Apprentice (1st degree).
- [[tyler]]: **Tyler**. The Tyler guards the door.
- [[verifiable-lottery]]: **Verifiable Lot**. The public, reproducible sortition used to choose examiners and to fill the Supreme Council: anyone with the same beacon obtains the same draw.
- [[waiting-period]]: **Waiting Period**. In Masonic tradition the waiting period is the **minimum time** a member must remain in a degree before being raised to the next.
