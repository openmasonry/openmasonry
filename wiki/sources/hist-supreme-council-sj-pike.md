# Source: Supreme Council 33° (Southern Jurisdiction, USA) and Albert Pike

Notes on the Supreme Council of the Scottish Rite's Southern Jurisdiction, founded in Charleston in 1801, and on Albert Pike.

## The Supreme Council
- Founded in Charleston (South Carolina) in **May 1801** by eleven Masons, among them John Mitchell and Frederick Dalcho. It calls itself the "Mother Council of the World": it is the first Supreme Council of the 33-degree **Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite** (AASR).
- It invokes the "Grand Constitutions of 1786", attributed to Frederick II of Prussia. The attribution is **widely disputed**.
- In 1813 the Northern Jurisdiction appears, in New York, and received 15 states in 1823. The Southern Jurisdiction today covers 35 states.
- It nearly disappeared after the anti-Masonic wave of the Morgan affair (1826): **four members in 1844**. Since 1870 it has resided in Washington, in the House of the Temple (1915).
- Degree structure: Lodge of Perfection (4–14), Chapter of Rose Croix (15–18), Council of Kadosh (19–30), Consistory (31–32). The 33° is honorific, and the Council has at most 33 active members. See [[catalog-of-the-33-degrees]].

## Albert Pike
- [[albert-pike]] (1809–1891) was a lawyer, Confederate general and Grand Commander from 1859 to 1891 (32 years). He rewrote the rituals and published *Morals and Dogma* (1871/1872).
- Controversies: documented racist positions; an alleged link to the Ku Klux Klan that is **unproven**; the "Luciferian" letter attributed to him is a **forgery** linked to the Léo Taxil hoax, confessed in 1897.

## Lessons for OpenMasonry
- A **self-perpetuating** top body, with at most 33 life members and decades-long terms, is the opposite of what Landmark V ("No permanent power") proposes. See [[verifiable-lottery]].
- The near-extinction of 1844 shows how fragile closed bodies are in the face of reputational crises.
