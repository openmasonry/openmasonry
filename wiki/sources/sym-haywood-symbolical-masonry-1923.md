# Haywood, *Symbolical Masonry* (1923)

Haywood's interpretive handbook, in the public domain, walks through the three Craft (Blue Lodge) degrees and explains each symbol in moral language. (The author signs as H. L. Haywood.) It is useful because it often presents **several readings** of the same symbol and because it treats the ballot as a problem of lodge government.

**Main points used**
- The **ballot** is the "most vexatious" subject in lodge government. Haywood compares the rules: unanimity in the USA, three black balls in England and **one fifth negative votes in France**. That French rule is the declared origin of OpenMasonry's `5B ≥ T` (see [[ballot-and-blackball]], [[decision-function]]).
- The tools receive moral readings: the [[twenty-four-inch-gauge-and-gavel]] speak of time and of rough-dressing; the [[level-and-plumb]] and the square (see [[square-and-compasses]]) speak of equality, uprightness and conduct; the [[trowel]] speaks of the cement of brotherhood.
- The stones admit plural readings, from the summum bonum to a "well-ordered State" (Pike). See [[rough-and-perfect-ashlar]].
- On the pillars, Haywood states that a symbol with a single meaning is "hardly a symbol". See [[pillars-of-the-temple]].
- He shows the operative origin of the [[lodge]] (a site shed) and the migration of the [[seven-liberal-arts]] to the 2nd degree in Preston's revision.

**Limits.** Haywood writes as an American Mason of 1923, for Masonic readers. The etymologies and ancient parallels he proposes are speculative. Chapters on obligations, words and signs were not used in this wiki.
