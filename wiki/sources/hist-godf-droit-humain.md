# Source: French liberal Masonry and the admission of women

Notes on the Grand Orient of France, which stopped requiring belief in God in 1877, and on Le Droit Humain, the mixed order founded in 1893.

## Grand Orient of France
- Formed in **1773** by the reorganization of the Grand Lodge of France (traditionally dated to 1738).
- **Convent of 1877:** at the request of the pastor Frédéric Desmons, it adopts "absolute freedom of conscience" and stops requiring belief in God and in the immortality of the soul. See [[great-architect-of-the-universe]].
- **1878:** the UGLE expresses "profound regret" and breaks off relations. This is the origin of the split between "regular" and "liberal" Masonry. See [[regularity-and-recognition]].
- **Affair of the Cards (1904):** GODF lodges supplied the War Ministry with files on officers' religious opinions, used in promotions. It is a classic case of a discreet network used as an instrument of power.
- **2010:** lodges become free to initiate women, a decision contested internally.
- It has about 52,000–55,000 members.

## Le Droit Humain
- **1882-01-14:** [[maria-deraismes]] is initiated by the lodge *Les Libres Penseurs*, which declared itself autonomous to do so. Its grand lodge did not recognize the initiation.
- **1893-04-04:** Deraismes and Georges Martin found the first **mixed** Masonic order. One source gives March 14 and another has "1883", probably an error.
- Around 1900–1901 the high degrees are adopted and a Supreme Council is created. In **1902**, with Annie Besant, the British Federation is born.
- It admits men and women equally, regardless of origin or belief. It is not recognized by "regular" Masonry.

See: [[grand-orient-de-france]], [[le-droit-humain]].
