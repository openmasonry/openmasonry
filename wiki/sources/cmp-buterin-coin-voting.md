# Source: Vitalik Buterin, "Moving beyond coin voting governance" (2021-08-16)

An essay by Ethereum's co-founder on why **token voting** is a dangerous foundation for decentralized governance.

## Arguments (summarized)

1. **Plutocracy:** large token holders ("whales") coordinate easily, and small holders carry almost no weight.
2. **Rational apathy:** since a small vote almost never decides anything, nobody researches or votes. Result: low turnout.
3. **Misaligned incentives:** only holders vote, and they tend to prioritize the token price over the community.
4. **Vote buying:** the right to vote can be separated from the economic risk (lending, wrappers, custody on an exchange). The vote can thus be **rented**.
5. **Alternatives:** limit what token voting decides; proof of personhood (one person, one vote); proof of participation (weight by contribution); "skin in the game" (futarchy, forks that punish bad voters, reputation); hybrid models.

The author says token voting is attractive because it "feels credibly neutral". That neutrality, however, is only apparent.

## Relation to OpenMasonry

OpenMasonry follows two of the alternatives: one person, one vote (Landmark IV), and status from work rather than capital (Landmark III). Beyond that, it gives up the token entirely (see [[openmasonry-vs-token-daos]], [[sybil-resistance]]).
