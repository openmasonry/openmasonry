# Source: the Regius Poem (c. 1390–1450)

The **Regius Poem** is the oldest of the known [[old-charges]]: a regulation for English **operative** masons, in Middle English verse, now in the British Library (Royal MS 17 A I). It is the only one of the Old Charges written in verse.

## What the text says
- It opens with a **legendary history**: Euclid supposedly created geometry ("masonry") to educate the children of the nobility in Egypt, and the craft supposedly reached England under King Athelstan (10th century). This is **legend**, not history.
- It sets out **fifteen articles** (for masters) and **fifteen points** (for workmen): pay a fair wage according to the cost of living, work honestly, do not take another's work, look after apprentices, attend the annual assembly.
- It includes the legend of the Four Crowned Martyrs, the [[seven-liberal-arts]] (with geometry as the art that separates the false from the true) and advice on good conduct.
- The first article requires the master to be "stedefast, trusty, and trwe" (steadfast, trustworthy and true) and to pay his fellows fairly.

## Dating (disputed)
- Halliwell (1840) and 19th-century Masonic authors: c. 1390.
- E. A. Bond of the British Museum, and modern palaeography: second quarter of the 15th century (c. 1425–1450).
- This wiki therefore writes **"c. 1390 (traditional) to c. 1450"**.

## Why it matters for OpenMasonry
- It shows that the documented origin of Masonry is a **professional code of conduct** with written rules, not a mystical secret.
- The annual assembly and the fair wage anticipate ideas of governance by public rules that OpenMasonry takes up (see [[historical-lessons-for-openmasonry]]).
