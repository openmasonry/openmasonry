# Source: Prince Hall and belated recognition

Notes on Prince Hall, initiated in Boston in 1775, and on the long wait of the lodges that descend from him for recognition.

## Facts
- **1775:** [[prince-hall]] and 14 other free Black men of Boston, refused by the local white lodge, were initiated by **Lodge No. 441** (Irish Constitution), a military lodge of the British 38th Regiment.
- **1776:** when the regiment left Boston, the group was left with only a limited permit to meet as **African Lodge No. 1**, without the power to confer degrees.
- **1784:** the Grand Lodge of London issues a warrant dated **1784-09-29** for **African Lodge No. 459**. According to secondary sources, the document only reached Boston in 1787.
- **1791 or 1808:** sources date the formation of an African Grand Lodge differently. In 1791 Hall already appeared as Provincial Grand Master; in 1808, lodges in Boston, Philadelphia and Providence organized themselves.
- **1813:** after the English union, the lodge disappears from the UGLE register. **1827:** it declares independence from England.
- **Recognition:** Connecticut was the first "regular" American Grand Lodge to recognize a Prince Hall Grand Lodge (**1989-10-14**). The UGLE recognized the Massachusetts one on **1994-12-14**. In 2026, four Grand Lodges in the southern USA still recognize none.
