# Mackey, *The Symbolism of Freemasonry* (1869)

A work by [[albert-mackey]] in 31 chapters (public domain; Project Gutenberg, number 11937). It interprets Freemasonry as a system of symbols inherited from the ancient "Mysteries" and centered on Solomon's Temple.

**Main points used**
- Solomon's Temple is "the link" between operative and speculative Masonry (ch. XII). See [[pillars-of-the-temple]].
- The lodge has the form of an "oblong square", represents the world and has the sky as its roof (chs. XIII and XVI). See [[lodge]].
- The three principal officers allude to the rising sun, noon and the setting sun (ch. XIV).
- Light symbolizes truth and knowledge (ch. XXII). This is the symbolic root of the [[light-score]] metric.
- "The plumb is a symbol of rectitude of conduct" (preface). See [[level-and-plumb]].

**Limits.** The solar readings and "ancient" lineages Mackey proposes are not accepted by current historiography. This wiki presents them as **interpretation**. By the same author, the list of 25 landmarks (1858) is summarized in [[sym-landmarks-anderson-mackey-pound]].
