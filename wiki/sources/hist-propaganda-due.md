# Source: P2, the classic case of capture

Notes on Propaganda Due (P2), the Italian lodge that Licio Gelli ran as a clandestine network and whose list of 962 names was found in 1981.

## Facts
- It began as *Propaganda Massonica* (Turin, **1877**), a lodge of the Grand Orient of Italy (GOI) for prominent members, with confidential membership.
- Licio Gelli took it over in the 1970s (the Grand Master's representative in 1970, Worshipful Master in 1975). The GOI **withdrew its warrant in 1976**; dates vary between 1974 and 1981 depending on the source. Gelli kept running it clandestinely.
- **March 1981:** a search of Gelli's properties finds a list of **962 names**: ministers, members of parliament, the heads of all the secret services, generals, judges, bankers and journalists. The government falls.
- The **Anselmi Commission** (1981–1984) concludes that P2 was a secret organization that had infiltrated the state. **Law 17/1982** dissolves P2 and bans secret associations.
- P2 was linked to the collapse of Banco Ambrosiano, the death of Roberto Calvi (1982) and, by court rulings in the 2020s, the financing of the Bologna massacre (1980).

## Reading
- P2 had already been **repudiated by its own Grand Orient** before the scandal. The problem is not "Freemasonry" as such: it is **secrecy + patronage + permanence** without external audit.
- It is the central example for the whitepaper's thesis (§1.2–1.3) and for Landmark II ("Light, not secrecy").
