# Source: Anderson's Constitutions (1723) and the founding of 1717

Notes on *The Constitutions of the Free-Masons* (London, 1723) and on the debate about the founding date of 1717.

## The book
*The Constitutions of the Free-Masons* (London, 1723), known as "Anderson's Constitutions" after the Presbyterian minister [[james-anderson]]. It was approved by the Grand Lodge of London and contains:
1. a **legendary history** of Masonry since Adam (legend, not history);
2. **six Charges**, adapted from the [[old-charges]];
3. **39 General Regulations**, compiled by George Payne;
4. songs.

The dedication is signed by [[jean-theophile-desaguliers]]. The Museum of Freemasonry says the book helped establish the Grand Lodge of London as the authority of English Masonry and carried modern Masonry to the world. The 2nd edition (1738) greatly expanded the legendary history.

## Key passages (public domain)
- **Charge I:** a Mason obeys the moral law and will "never be a stupid Atheist, nor an irreligious Libertine", but is required only to hold "that Religion in which all Men agree", leaving particular opinions to each person. Masonry becomes the "Center of Union". See [[great-architect-of-the-universe]].
- **Charge III:** excludes bondmen, **women** and immoral men. This is the textual root of the exclusion of women.
- **Charge IV:** all preferment is grounded "upon real Worth and personal Merit", never on seniority.
- **Regulation VI:** admission only with the **unanimous consent** of those present. See [[ballot-and-blackball]].
- **Regulation XXXIX:** the Grand Lodge may change the rules provided the "old Land-Marks" are preserved. A change requires a prior written proposal, open to reading "even of the youngest Apprentice", and a **majority** of those present. See [[landmarks]].

## The 1717 debate
- **Traditional version (UGLE):** on 1717-06-24, four London lodges formed the first Grand Lodge and elected Anthony Sayer.
- **Problem:** the only account is Anderson's own, in the **1738** edition.
- **Prescott and Sommers (2017):** there is no contemporary evidence for 1717. The Apple Tree tavern, cited by Anderson, appears not to have existed at the time. The organized Grand Lodge would begin on **1721-06-24**, with the installation of the Duke of Montagu. Regulation XXXIX itself records approval "by about 150 brethren" on St John's Day **1721**.
- **Reception:** John Hamill agrees that evidence for 1717 is lacking. Ric Berman and David Harrison accept neither 1721 nor the fabrication thesis.
- **Status in this wiki:** 1717 is the **traditional date**; 1721 is the first **documented** date.
