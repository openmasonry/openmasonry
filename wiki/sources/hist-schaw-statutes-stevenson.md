# Source: the Schaw Statutes and David Stevenson's thesis

Notes on the two sets of rules William Schaw issued for Scottish masons in 1598 and 1599, and on David Stevenson's reading of them.

## The Statutes
[[william-schaw]], Master of Works to King James VI of Scotland, issued two sets of rules for Scottish masons.
- **First Statutes (1598-12-28):** the duty to obey wardens and masters; **annual election** of a warden per lodge; at most three apprentices per master; a seven-year apprenticeship; a **written record** of each apprentice and each new fellow (with his mark); a minimum quorum for admission; fines; a ban on working with *cowans* (unqualified builders).
- **Second Statutes (1599-12-28):** an order of precedence among the lodges (Edinburgh, Kilwinning, Stirling), memory tests and, again, a requirement to keep records.

## Stevenson's thesis (1988), in brief
- Freemasonry organized in lodges, with its own rites and secrets, was born in **Scotland around 1600**, not in the England of 1700. Schaw's reform is the decisive moment.
- Scottish lodges keep **minutes dating from 1599**, the oldest in the world. Exactly which are the oldest varies by source.
- Over the 17th century, non-operative "gentleman" members join the lodges.
- He links this culture to the Renaissance (the art of memory, Hermeticism), in contrast with the Enlightenment, London-centered emphasis of historians such as [[margaret-jacob]].

## Why it matters for OpenMasonry
Written records and annual elections are the oldest form of Masonic **accountability**. OpenMasonry's [[chained-ledger]] is, in a sense, Schaw's minute book made public and verifiable.
