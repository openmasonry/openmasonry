# Source: *The Principles of Masonic Law* (Mackey, 1856)

A treatise on Masonic law by [[albert-mackey]], in the public domain (Project Gutenberg, number 12186). Two years later, in 1858, Mackey published his list of **25 [[landmarks]]**.

## Key points
- **Definition of a *landmark*:** "immemorial" usages, ritual or legislative, whose alteration would destroy the identity of the institution. Grand Lodge laws, however old, are not landmarks and may change.
- **Unanimity in the ballot:** Regulation VI of 1721/1723 requires unanimous consent. The English "New Regulations" of 1754 came to accept up to **three black balls**. According to Mackey, almost all American Grand Lodges kept unanimity. See [[ballot-and-blackball]].
- **Secrecy of the vote:** the ballot must be secret to guarantee the voter's independence, and no Mason may be called to account for the vote he cast. Mackey admits that voting against out of a personal grudge is wrong, but the lodge cannot judge motives. This is the structure of the **anonymous, unaccountable veto**.
- **Exclusion of women:** Mackey asserts that no woman can be made a Mason and treats this as beyond dispute.

## Reception of the landmarks
- The word comes from Regulation XXXIX (1723), which never defined it.
- Mackey's list was adopted by some North American Grand Lodges. Other lists range from 3 to 54 items.
- [[albert-pike]] wrote in 1888 that the question had "never been definitively settled".

## Contradiction with the whitepaper (resolved)
Caution: Mackey (1856) and Haywood are sometimes credited with the French rule of rejection by **one fifth** of the votes. That rule **does not appear** in this book and remains unverified.
