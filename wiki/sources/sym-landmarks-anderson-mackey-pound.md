# Old Charges, Anderson (1723) and the lists of Landmarks

A clip bringing together four pieces on the written law of the craft, from the medieval Old Charges to the modern lists of landmarks.

- The medieval Old Charges (Regius, c. 1390; Cooke, c. 1425);
- the six Charges of [[james-anderson]]'s *Constitutions* (1723);
- [[albert-mackey]]'s list of 25 landmarks (1858);
- Roscoe Pound's reduction to 7 landmarks (1911).

**Main points**
- General Regulation XXXIX of 1723 orders the "old landmarks" preserved **without defining them**. Since then, each jurisdiction has had its own list, from 7 to 54 items in the USA. See [[landmarks]].
- Charge I of 1723 combines the duty to obey the moral law with a religion "in which all men agree". This is the foundation of the lodges' religious pluralism and of the [[great-architect-of-the-universe]]. See [[old-charges]].
- Items 18 (only free men without disability), 19–20 (belief in God and in a future life) and 23 (secrecy) of Mackey's list are precisely the ones OpenMasonry rejects in the [[eternal-landmarks-of-openmasonry]].
- Mackey declares the landmarks unalterable (item 25). It is the same gesture as the `ETERNAL_PREFIX` in the code, but with opposite content.

**Limits.** There is no consensus on who drafted the 1723 Charges. Adoption of Mackey's list is a minority position.
