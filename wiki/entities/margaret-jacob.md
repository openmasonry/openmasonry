# Margaret C. Jacob (b. 1943)

American historian of the Enlightenment and of science, professor emerita at UCLA. She is one of the leading authors in the academic historiography of Freemasonry.

## Relevant works
- *The Radical Enlightenment: Pantheists, Freemasons and Republicans* (1981).
- *Living the Enlightenment: Freemasonry and Politics in Eighteenth-Century Europe* (Oxford UP, 1991).
- *The Origins of Freemasonry: Facts and Fictions* (2006).

## Main thesis (summary)
- Eighteenth-century lodges were among the main spaces where **modern civil society** took shape. They were self-governments in miniature, with constitutions, laws, elections and representatives. The book has a chapter on the creation of "constitutional societies". See [[enlightenment-sociability]].
- The whitepaper (§1.1) sums this up by saying the lodges were "schools of government" [Jacob 1991]. It is a **paraphrase** of the thesis, not a verified literal quotation.

## Debate
- [[david-stevenson]] places the origin of organized Freemasonry in Renaissance Scotland (c. 1600) and criticized Jacob's 2006 book for projecting the Enlightenment Freemasonry of the eighteenth century onto seventeenth-century Scotland.
- Masonic readers sometimes note that Jacob favors the political dimension over the symbolic one.

## For OpenMasonry
Jacob provides the historical basis for the project's central bet: the value of Freemasonry lay in its **forms of self-government** (constitution, election, minutes) as much as in its symbolism. OpenMasonry tries to preserve those forms by making them public and verifiable.
