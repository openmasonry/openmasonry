# Albert Pike (1809–1891)

Lawyer, poet, soldier and the most influential leader of the **Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite** in the United States.

## Life
- Born in Boston (Dec 29, 1809), died in Washington (Apr 2, 1891). As a lawyer in Arkansas, he represented Native American nations in suits against the federal government.
- He was a captain in the Mexican–American War and a **Confederate brigadier general** in 1861. He negotiated treaties between the Confederacy and Native American peoples, and his troops fought at Pea Ridge (1862). He resigned the same year.

## Masonic role
- **Sovereign Grand Commander** of the [[supreme-council-aasr-southern-jurisdiction]] from 1859 until his death (32 years). The exact date of his election in 1859 varies between sources.
- He rewrote the rituals of the 4th to 32nd degrees and published *Morals and Dogma* (1871/1872), a long philosophical commentary on the degrees. See [[catalog-of-the-33-degrees]].
- On the [[landmarks]], he acknowledged (1888) that the question had "never been definitively settled".

## Controversies (with status)
- **Racism:** documented. He opposed Black suffrage and made racist statements.
- **Ku Klux Klan:** alleged membership, **unproven**. His biographer (W. L. Brown, 1997) found no reliable evidence, although some historians assert it.
- **"Luciferian" letter:** a **forgery** originating in Léo Taxil's anti-Masonic hoax, which Taxil himself confessed in 1897. It must not be cited as authentic.
- **Statue in Washington:** toppled and burned in 2020. Wikipedia records a reinstallation in 2025, not verified here.

## For OpenMasonry
Pike shows both sides of lifelong leadership: **enormous intellectual output** and **personal power with no term limit**. OpenMasonry uses the degree curriculum as study material but forbids offices without a fixed term (Landmark V). See [[verifiable-lottery]].
