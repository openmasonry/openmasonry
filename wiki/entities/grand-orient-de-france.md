# Grand Orient de France (GODF)

The oldest French Masonic body (1773) and the largest "adogmatic" one in continental Europe. It has about 52,000–55,000 members in more than 1,400 lodges.

## History
- **1773:** born from the reorganization of the Grand Lodge of France, traditionally dated to 1738.
- **1877:** at the request of the pastor Frédéric Desmons, it adopts **absolute liberty of conscience** and stops requiring belief in God and in the immortality of the soul. It later removes the invocation of the [[great-architect-of-the-universe]] from its rituals.
- **1878:** the UGLE breaks off relations. This marks the split between **"regular"** Freemasonry (Anglo-American, theist) and **"liberal"** Freemasonry (continental, adogmatic). See [[regularity-and-recognition]].
- During the Third Republic it was heavily engaged in politics: the law on associations (1901) and the separation of Church and State (1905).
- **Affair of the Cards (Affaire des Fiches, 1904):** lodges supplied the War Ministry with files on officers' religious convictions, used to block promotions. The scandal brought down the Minister of War.
- **1940:** dissolved by Vichy, with members persecuted.
- **2010:** lodges become able to initiate women, through decisions contested internally.

## For OpenMasonry
The GODF shows that Freemasonry can open the door on **belief** without disappearing. The Affair of the Cards shows the risk of a discreet network becoming an **instrument of state power**. The same affair is a reminder that **secrecy is not privacy**: hidden files can come out. OpenMasonry publishes its rules and decisions, and keeps only the minimum of personal data on the ledger (whitepaper §12.3). It also tells members plainly that a closed study room can leak, and keeps nothing of lasting value there (§10.6).
