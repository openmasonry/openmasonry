# Prince Hall Freemasonry

A branch of American Freemasonry founded by and for African Americans, originating in the lodge of [[prince-hall]] in Boston. It is the oldest continuously existing Black fraternal institution in the United States.

## Origin
- **1775:** initiation of Prince Hall and 14 companions by an Irish military lodge, after they were turned away by Boston's white lodge.
- **1784:** English charter for **African Lodge No. 459**.
- **1791 or 1808** (sources differ): organization of an African Grand Lodge. **1813:** the lodge disappears from the English register after the union. **1827:** it declares independence.
- From this came the Prince Hall Grand Lodges of the various states, as well as their own high-degree bodies.

## Recognition
- For almost two centuries the white Grand Lodges refused recognition. The arguments were technical (exclusive territorial jurisdiction, validity of the charter), but the context was **racial segregation**.
- **1989:** Connecticut grants recognition for the first time. **1994:** the UGLE recognizes the Prince Hall Grand Lodge of Massachusetts.
- As of 2026, 47 of the 51 regular Grand Lodges in the US recognize the Prince Hall Grand Lodges. Mississippi, Louisiana, South Carolina and West Virginia **still do not**.
- Wikipedia gives more than 300,000 members and 4,500 lodges, a figure whose counting basis is uncertain. These members are **not included** in the MSANA statistics. See [[hist-membership-decline]].

## For OpenMasonry
It is the clearest case of how **recognition**, a tool meant to protect regularity, was used to keep a **door closed**. OpenMasonry answers with Landmark I (no refusal on grounds of origin or color) and with its rejection of any central recognition authority. See [[regularity-and-recognition]] and [[openmasonry-vs-traditional-freemasonry]].
