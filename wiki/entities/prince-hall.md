# Prince Hall (c. 1735?–1807)

Boston community leader and abolitionist. He founded the first Masonic lodge of Black men in the Americas, which gave rise to [[prince-hall-freemasonry]].

## Facts
- **Birth uncertain.** "C. 1735" is the most cited date, and the claim that he came from Barbados is unproven. He died in Boston in December 1807.
- **1775:** turned away, along with 14 other free Black men, by Boston's white lodge, he was initiated by an Irish military lodge (Lodge No. 441, attached to the British 38th Regiment). When the regiment left (1776), the group received only a limited permit to meet as *African Lodge No. 1*.
- **1784:** he obtained from the Grand Lodge of London a charter (dated Sep 29, 1784) for **African Lodge No. 459**, of which he was Master. Secondary sources say the document arrived in 1787.
- In the 1790s he acted as Provincial Grand Master. He was active in petitions against slavery and for the education of Black children in Boston.

## Why it matters
- Hall used the institutional form available, a **written charter from a recognized authority**, to get around local exclusion. The 1784 charter would later become the center of the disputes over legitimacy.
- His successors waited about **two centuries** for recognition by the white Grand Lodges: Connecticut in 1989, the UGLE in 1994. See [[regularity-and-recognition]].

## For OpenMasonry
He is the strongest argument for Landmark I ("The Open Door") and Landmark VII ("Exit and Fork"). No one should need permission from those who exclude them in order to exist legitimately.
