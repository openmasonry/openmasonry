# William Schaw (c. 1550–1602)

Master of Works to King James VI of Scotland from 1583, with authority over the royal buildings. He also styled himself "General Warden" of the Scottish masons. He is the author of the **Schaw Statutes**.

## The Statutes
- **Dec 28, 1598 (Edinburgh):** **annual** election of wardens in each lodge; written records of apprentices and of new fellows with their marks; a minimum apprenticeship of seven years; a quorum for admissions; fines for absences and for working with unqualified builders.
- **Dec 28, 1599 (Holyrood):** precedence among the lodges of Edinburgh, Kilwinning and Stirling; memory tests; renewed insistence on records.

## Why it matters
- After him, Scottish lodges keep **minutes** from 1599 onward. They are the oldest known lodge records, which is why [[david-stevenson]] places the origin of organized Freemasonry in **Scotland around 1600**.
- Schaw turned oral customs into **written, auditable rules**: who entered, when, and with which witnesses.

## For OpenMasonry
The [[chained-ledger]] takes up the same intuition, that every admission should leave a record with witnesses. The difference is that the record becomes **public, signed and hash-chained**, instead of being kept in the lodge's book.

## Caution
Schaw was a court administrator, not a "mystical founder". Attributing esoteric intentions to him is speculation. Stevenson links the cultural climate of the period to the Renaissance art of memory, but that is interpretation.
