# United Grand Lodge of England (UGLE)

The governing body of "regular" Freemasonry in England, Wales and overseas districts. Successor to the world's first Grand Lodge, it is the reference point of the Anglo-American system of [[regularity-and-recognition]].

## Timeline
- **1717 (traditional) / 1721 (documented):** four London lodges form the Grand Lodge and elect Anthony Sayer. The 1717 date depends on [[james-anderson]]'s 1738 account and is **disputed** (Prescott and Sommers, 2017). See [[hist-anderson-constitutions-1717]].
- **1719:** [[jean-theophile-desaguliers]] is Grand Master. **1721:** the Duke of Montagu is the first noble Grand Master.
- **1723:** publishes the *Constitutions* (Anderson).
- **1751:** the rival Grand Lodge of the **"Ancients"** appears, calling the first one the "Moderns". For 63 years neither recognizes the other as regular.
- **Dec 27, 1813:** the two bodies unite to form the UGLE, under the Duke of Sussex.
- **1878:** breaks with the [[grand-orient-de-france]] after the latter stops requiring belief in God.
- **1929:** publishes the "Basic Principles" for recognition: belief in a Supreme Being, the Volume of the Sacred Law, men only, a ban on religious and political debate. See [[sym-regularity-ugle-1929]].
- **1994:** recognizes the Prince Hall Grand Lodge of Massachusetts. See [[prince-hall-freemasonry]].

## Numbers
- 2023/24: **168,961** members (150,124 in the home base, 18,837 overseas). In 2021 there were 175,657.
- A decline of about **2.5% a year since 2008**, from about 200,000 to just over 150,000 in the home base. See [[hist-membership-decline]].
- Recent responses: openness campaigns and reduced dues for under-25s.
- England has two women's Grand Lodges, which the UGLE does not recognize as regular but with which it maintains dialogue.

## For OpenMasonry
The UGLE invented the modern form of a **federation of lodges under a written constitution**. It also invented the **power to recognize**, that is, to decide who is a legitimate Mason. OpenMasonry does without that central authority: the legitimacy of a fork is judged in public (Landmark VII). See [[openmasonry-vs-traditional-freemasonry]].
