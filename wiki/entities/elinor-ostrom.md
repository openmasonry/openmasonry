# Elinor Ostrom (1933–2012)

American political scientist and economist at Indiana University (Bloomington). The first woman to receive the **Nobel Prize in Economics** (2009, shared with Oliver Williamson), "for her analysis of economic governance, especially the commons".

## Life
- Born Elinor Awan in Los Angeles (1933), into a family of modest means. Died Jun 12, 2012.
- With Vincent Ostrom she founded Indiana University's *Workshop in Political Theory and Policy Analysis*.

## Central work
*Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action* (Cambridge UP, 1990). Against the "tragedy of the commons", she showed through hundreds of case studies (Swiss pastures, Japanese forests, Spanish and Philippine irrigation, Maine lobster fisheries) that communities can **self-govern common-pool resources** for centuries when their institutions follow certain **design principles**. See [[ostrom-principles]].

She cautioned that not all the principles need to be present and that they tend to work together.

## Relation to Freemasonry and OpenMasonry
- Ostrom **did not write** about Freemasonry. The bridge is built by the OpenMasonry whitepaper (§3.2), which maps each of the eight principles onto project mechanisms: clear boundaries, graduated sanctions, peer monitoring, nested enterprises (Lodge → House → Supreme Council).
- Read in hindsight, the longevity of lodges is consistent with several of her principles: clear membership boundaries, local rules and annual elections. The lack of **independent monitoring**, however, is exactly where traditional Freemasonry failed, as the [[p2-lodge]] case shows.
