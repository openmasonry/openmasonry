# David Stevenson (b. 1942)

Scottish historian, professor emeritus at the University of St Andrews, specialist in seventeenth-century Scotland.

## Relevant works
- *The Origins of Freemasonry: Scotland's Century, 1590–1710* (Cambridge UP, 1988).
- *The First Freemasons: Scotland's Early Lodges and their Members* (1988).

## Main thesis (summary)
- Freemasonry organized in **permanent lodges**, with rites, secrets and records, emerged in **Scotland around 1600**, not in England in 1717.
- The turning point is the Statutes of [[william-schaw]] (1598–1599), which required elected wardens and record books. Hence the Scottish minutes dating from 1599.
- By 1710 there were about 25 such lodges in Scotland, and non-operative "gentlemen" joined them over the course of the seventeenth century.
- The cultural setting would have been Renaissance (the art of memory, Hermeticism) rather than Enlightenment.

## Debate
Stevenson contrasts with [[margaret-jacob]], who stresses the Enlightenment, London-centered character of speculative Freemasonry. The traditional "English" view, anchored in 1717 and [[james-anderson]], has also been shaken from another direction, by the debate over 1721. See [[hist-anderson-constitutions-1717]].

## For OpenMasonry
Stevenson shows that the institution was born from **written rules and records**, before any mystique. This is the lineage that the [[chained-ledger]] claims.
