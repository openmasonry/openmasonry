# Supreme Council 33° of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite, Southern Jurisdiction (USA)

The world's first Supreme Council of the AASR, founded in Charleston in **May 1801** (traditionally May 31). For that reason it styles itself the "Mother Supreme Council of the World". Headquartered in Washington, in the House of the Temple (1915). It covers 35 American states; the other 15 belong to the Northern Jurisdiction, created in 1813.

## Structure
- It administers degrees **4 to 32** through subordinate bodies: Lodge of Perfection (4–14), Chapter of Rose Croix (15–18), Council of Kadosh (19–30) and Consistory (31–32). The **33°** is honorary and conferred by the Council itself.
- The Council has at most **33 active members**, led by a Sovereign Grand Commander. There have been 21 Commanders since 1801.
- In Masonic tradition the Master Mason degree (3°) is the highest in symbolic (Craft) Freemasonry. The "high degrees" are degrees of **study**, not of command. See [[catalog-of-the-33-degrees]] and [[masonic-rites-compared]].

## History
- Its documents invoke the "Grand Constitutions of 1786", attributed to Frederick II of Prussia. The attribution is **disputed** by historians.
- It nearly died out after the anti-Masonic wave following the Morgan affair (1826): **four members in 1844**.
- [[albert-pike]] was Grand Commander for 32 years (1859–1891), rewrote the rituals and published *Morals and Dogma*. [[albert-mackey]] was its Secretary General.
- It maintains significant philanthropy, such as the RiteCare speech and language programs.

## For OpenMasonry
OpenMasonry adopts the 33-degree **form** but inverts the design at the top. Its Supreme Council is filled by **verifiable lottery**, no one sits twice, and degrees confer **duties**, never extra votes. A self-perpetuating council of life members is exactly the pattern that Landmarks IV and V prohibit. See [[verifiable-lottery]].
