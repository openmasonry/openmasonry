# Albert Gallatin Mackey (1807–1881)

Physician from Charleston, South Carolina, and the foremost American Masonic jurist and encyclopedist of the nineteenth century. He was Secretary General of the [[supreme-council-aasr-southern-jurisdiction]] and a collaborator of [[albert-pike]].

## Main works
- *The Principles of Masonic Law* (1856), in the public domain. See [[hist-mackey-principles-masonic-law]].
- "The Foundations of Masonic Law" (Oct 1858), where he published his list of **25 [[landmarks]]**, later reproduced in the *Text Book of Masonic Jurisprudence* (1859).
- *Encyclopedia of Freemasonry* (1874) and *The Symbolism of Freemasonry* (1869). See [[sym-mackey-symbolism-of-freemasonry-1869]].

## Core ideas
- **Landmarks:** "immemorial" usages whose alteration would destroy the identity of the Order. His list was adopted **in full by only a few** Grand Lodges, and other lists range from 3 to 54 items.
- **Ballot:** he defended unanimity (Regulation VI of 1721/1723) and the **inviolable secrecy** of the vote. No Mason is answerable for the black ball he cast. See [[ballot-and-blackball]].
- **Exclusion:** he wrote that "No woman can be made a Mason" and called this landmark "so indisputable" that no argument was needed to support it.

## Assessment
- Mackey gave American Freemasonry a **legal grammar** and a vocabulary that are still in circulation.
- But his landmarks present as "immemorial" usages that are historically recent. The Master Mason degree, for example, did not exist in 1717. Historians and some Grand Lodges, such as that of British Columbia and Yukon, reject any official list.

## For OpenMasonry
OpenMasonry keeps Mackey's intuition that there should be an **unchangeable core**, but makes it **explicit, short and executable**: seven Landmarks in `canon/eternal.toml` that the verifier refuses to amend. It replaces the anonymous, unaccountable veto with [[commit-reveal]] and a public result.
