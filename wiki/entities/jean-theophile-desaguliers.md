# Jean Théophile (John Theophilus) Desaguliers (1683–1744)

Natural philosopher, Anglican clergyman and a central figure of the first Grand Lodge of London. He illustrates the link between nascent speculative Freemasonry and **Newtonian science**.

## Life
- Born in La Rochelle (Mar 12 or 13, 1683) into a Huguenot family, French Protestants under persecution, who went into exile in England. Died in London on Feb 29, 1744.
- He studied at Oxford and became experimental demonstrator of the **Royal Society** at Isaac Newton's invitation. He was elected a *Fellow* in 1714 and received the Copley Medal three times (1734, 1736, 1741).

## Masonic role
- **Third Grand Master** of the Grand Lodge of London (1719), after Anthony Sayer and George Payne. He was Deputy Grand Master in 1722–23 and 1725.
- He signed the dedication of the 1723 Constitutions ([[james-anderson]]). Many historians credit him with a decisive role in drafting the Charges and in attracting noblemen and scientists, such as the Duke of Montagu (1721).
- **Caveat:** there is little documentary evidence of his Masonic activity before 1719, and the extent of his authorship of the Charges is **debated**.

## Why it matters
Desaguliers represents Freemasonry as a **club of Enlightenment reason**: a place of sociability among scientists, clergy and aristocrats under written rules. See [[enlightenment-sociability]] and [[margaret-jacob]]. For OpenMasonry, he is a precedent for an association that values **demonstrable knowledge**, just like the verifiable ledger (see [[chained-ledger]]).
