# Le Droit Humain

The International Order of Mixed Freemasonry, the first Masonic order to admit **men and women on equal terms**. Headquartered in Paris, with federations in many countries.

## History
- **1882:** [[maria-deraismes]] is initiated by the lodge *Les Libres Penseurs* (Le Pecq), which had broken with its grand lodge in order to do so.
- **Apr 4, 1893:** Deraismes and the physician and senator Georges Martin found in Paris the *Grande Loge Symbolique Écossaise Mixte de France "Le Droit Humain"* (Mixed Symbolic Scottish Grand Lodge of France). One source gives Mar 14, 1893, and the British Federation's page says "1883", probably an error. **The date differs across sources.**
- Around 1900–1901 it adopts the degrees of the AASR up to the 33° and creates a **mixed Supreme Council**.
- **1902:** Annie Besant founds in London the lodge Human Duty No. 6, origin of the British Federation. From then on the Order becomes international.

## Principles
It admits men and women equally, without distinction of ethnic or social origin or of religious, political or philosophical conviction.

## Recognition
It is not recognized by the "regular" Freemasonry tied to the UGLE, which admits only men. See [[regularity-and-recognition]].

## For OpenMasonry
Le Droit Humain is the **historical proof** that a 33-degree Freemasonry open to everyone can work for more than a century. The whitepaper (§1.2) cites it as an example of openness that was left on the margins of the recognition system. OpenMasonry goes one step further by dispensing with recognition as a source of legitimacy.
