# The Custodian's charter

OpenMasonry lives on GitHub, and a GitHub repository always has an administrator. OpenMasonry also gives that administrator, for a time, one bounded power in the ledger: the power to repair damage by **corrections**. This charter says in public what the Custodian may do, what the Custodian may never do, how every act is recorded, and how the members stop an act or end the office.

This file is a governed file: its SHA-256 is pinned in the genesis entry, and it changes only by a passed amendment (Constitution Art. VII). It puts Article XVI and Article XII of the [Constitution](../../CONSTITUTION.md) in plain words. Where this text and the Constitution disagree, the Constitution governs; where either disagrees with the code, the code decides what the ledger accepts ([SPEC](../whitepaper/SPEC.md), sections 4.8, 4.9, 6.19 to 6.25, 6.31 to 6.33 and 7.9).

## 1. Who the Custodian is

- The **Custodian** is a founder who holds a live custodian key, named by the genesis entry or by a founder's claim (section 9). At the genesis the office is held by the founder who administers the canonical repository and the rooms on GitHub.
- The office is a **role outside the 33 degrees**. It is no degree, gives no rank and carries no vote. It is none of the four powers of the Constitution (Art. II.6): it makes no rule, judges no one and decides no ballot.
- **Two hats.** The person who holds the office is also a member: a founder, of the 3rd degree, with one vote like every other Master Builder. As a member, no founder has any power another Master Builder lacks. Until the Sunset a founder named Custodian also holds that office, with the powers of the Corrections article and no others. The two hats are kept apart by the ledger itself: a member act is signed by the member key under the member's login; a Custodian act is signed by the custodian key under the Custodian's name, which is never a member login. A Custodian's entries add no activity and no Light to the member.
- Custodians are named **by the genesis entry**, three at most, each linked to a different founder, **or by a founder's claim** (section 9). No other entry, no ballot and no lot creates a Custodian, and nothing gives a Custodian a new key.
- The Custodian is pseudonymous and never has to disclose a legal identity. The Custodian never asks anyone else for theirs.
- The office is **interim**. It ends as section 9 says. While few Master Builders exist any founder, whether or not that founder held it before, may take it up (the founder's fallback, section 9); otherwise nothing replaces it unless the members decide so by amendment.

## 2. The three keys

The person who holds the office holds three keys, each for one purpose. No key is ever used for the purpose of another, and the ledger refuses a genesis, or a founder's claim, in which any two of them are the same key.

| Key | What it signs | Where it is kept |
|---|---|---|
| **Member key** | The founder's acts as a member: votes, invitations, works. It is listed on the member's GitHub account. | On the machine the person works on, like any member's key; a hardware-backed key is recommended. |
| **Custodian key** | Corrections, and the sign of life, under the Custodian's name. Nothing else. | Offline: on a hardware token or an offline medium that is connected only to sign. Never on the machine that holds the member key. |
| **Revoke key** | One entry, once: the removal of its own custodian key. | Offline, on a different device and in a different place from the custodian key. |

- Both the custodian key and the revoke key prove themselves in the entry that names them (the genesis, or a founder's claim), and neither belongs to any GitHub account. Anyone may carry a Custodian's signed entry in a pull request; the signature alone decides whether it stands.
- The ledger cannot check where a key is kept. Keeping the three apart is the Custodian's duty under this charter.
- A custodian key never changes. If it is lost, it lapses; if it is stolen, the revoke key or the members end it ([RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md), section 2). Once no custodian key is live, a founder may claim the office again with two new keys (section 9).
- **Sign of life.** A custodian key that signs no `custodian_alive` entry for 180 days lapses for good. The Custodian signs one at least every 90 days (`om alive`); the ledger takes at most one in 24 hours. A correction is not a sign of life, and a sign of life never extends the term (section 9).

## 3. What the office does on GitHub

1. **Keeps the house in order.** Applies the settings in [LAUNCH.md](../operations/LAUNCH.md), keeps the judge as the required check, rotates the witness token, labels and triages issues.
2. **Reviews code and documents.** Pull requests that change anything outside the ledger need code-owner review (`.github/CODEOWNERS`). The Custodian reviews them on their merits and merges them only when tests pass. Changes to governed files (the Constitution, the canon, this charter, the rules of the rooms, and the files of the protocol core pinned as `@protocol`) are merged only when a ratified amendment requires them. Code outside the protocol core (the command line, the members' tools, the witness node, the other files of `.github/`) is not governed and rests on this review alone: the Custodian merges no change that would let such code decide a verdict, be run by the judge, or stand in for the judge (a second job named `judge`, an import from the core to code outside it, a file that shadows a module of the core).
3. **Moderates conversation.** Hides spam, locks threads that break the [Code of Conduct](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), and blocks accounts that are not members and abuse the repository (spam, harassment, doxxing, impersonation). Each such act is recorded in the ledger as a `note` (section 4).
4. **Handles security reports** under [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md).
5. **Acts in emergencies.** If the repository is under active attack (a leaked token, a malicious workflow, mass spam), the Custodian may pause workflows, revoke tokens or limit interactions at once. Every emergency act is recorded as a `note` within 72 hours, and its public reason states why it was needed and when it will be lifted. A use of the ruleset's emergency bypass is recorded the same way.
6. **Keeps the rooms' doors honest.** Access to a room follows the ledger and is applied by a published program ([`ops/rooms/sync.py`](../../ops/rooms/sync.py)), which runs once the rooms open at the public launch. The Custodian runs that program and does not choose who reads a room ([ROOMS.md](ROOMS.md)).

## 4. What the office does in the ledger: corrections

No entry of the ledger is ever rewritten, reordered or deleted (Landmark VI). A wrong is repaired only by a **correction**: a new signed entry, from a closed menu of acts, that may undo, freeze or pause and never grants.

**Every correction states a ground and a public reason.** The grounds of a repair are closed: a key compromised or lost (`key_compromised`), an entry accepted through a defect of the protocol (`protocol_defect`), fraud of identity (`identity_fraud`), an attack in progress (`attack_in_progress`). A `note` repairs nothing and states the ground `moderation`, which no other act states. Disagreement with an outcome is never a ground. The reason is a text archived in the repository as `ledger/reasons/<sha256>.md`; the correction pins its SHA-256, the judge refuses a correction that comes without it, and it can be neither withheld nor rewritten afterward. A reason names no person beyond a login and holds no personal data.

**Immediate acts** take effect at once, end by themselves and need one custodian key. **Noticed acts** touch a ballot: they are public for 14 days before they can take effect, are then applied by a further signed entry within the next 7 days or never, and every condition is checked again on the day they are applied. Where the genesis names several Custodians, a noticed act needs as many custodian keys as the genesis set (the others co-sign it); a Custodian named by a founder's claim acts alone.

| Act | When it is used | How long it lasts | How members stop it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Freeze a member's key** (`freeze_key`) | A member's key is lost or in other hands; at the member's request or on evidence. Ground `key_compromised` only. | 30 days. A member's key is frozen again only 90 days after the previous freeze of that member began, whatever key the member then holds, unless other Custodians withdrew that freeze. A key is frozen only while fewer keys are frozen than a tenth of the active members (a limit never below one), keys held by a recovery included; the limit holds back freezes, never a key recovery. | Objection. It also ends when the member's key recovery is completed, or when it is withdrawn (section 4, rules). |
| **Freeze a witness key** (`freeze_witness`) | A witness key or its machine is compromised. | 30 days. The last witness that is not frozen is frozen only under `attack_in_progress`. | Objection. A witness ballot removes the witness for good. |
| **Stay a ballot** (`stay`) | An open ballot is being distorted (votes from a stolen key, a defect, fraud) and time is needed to show it. Posted only before the ballot's reveal window opens, so no Custodian pauses a ballot after a vote can be read. | 14 days, once in the life of the ballot. The time is given back to the ballot's windows. | Objection. |
| **Halt the ledger** (`halt`) | An attack on the ledger is in progress. Ground `attack_in_progress` only. | 14 days, at most once in 90 days. No other ballot is closed and no lot is drawn; the clocks of those ballots, of examinations, invitations and petitions stop. | Objection, which is accepted during a halt. Amendment ballots and the ballot that removes the Custodian run through a halt, on their own clocks. |
| **Hold a member out of the rooms** (`hold_room`) | A member is harming others in a room and the harm cannot wait for a ballot. | 14 days. A member is held again only 90 days after their previous hold began, unless other Custodians withdrew that hold. | Objection. A hold is not a sanction; only a sanction ballot can keep someone out longer. |
| **Note** (`note`) | The public record of a moderation act done on GitHub: what was done, to what, under which published rule. | It has no effect in the ledger and is kept apart from the corrections: it counts toward no limit of corrections, and the state keeps the newest 200 notes (every note stays in its entry, the public log). At most ten per Custodian in a UTC day. | Nothing in the ledger to stop: a note cannot be objected to or withdrawn. An appeal is a public issue labeled `appeal` (section 6). |
| **Void an open ballot** (`void_ballot`) | An open ballot cannot give a true result (the same causes as a stay). Posted only before the ballot's reveal window opens. It ends the ballot with no effect and no one is penalized. | 14 days of notice, then applied within 7 days. Once per ballot, unless other Custodians withdrew the earlier one. While it is pending the ballot is paused: no vote is committed or revealed, it is not closed, and the time is given back to its windows. | Objection before it is applied voids it for good. |
| **Undo a closed ballot** (`undo_ballot`) | An admission, or an advancement to any degree but the 3rd, that was obtained by fraud, by a stolen key or through a defect. Posted within 90 days of the ballot's close. | 14 days of notice, then applied within 7 days. Once per ballot, unless other Custodians withdrew the earlier one. It applies only if everything the ballot decided still stands as the ballot left it, and the undo of an admission waits while a sanction ballot on that member is open. | Objection before it is applied voids it for good. After it is applied, the members' remedy is a new ballot, or the removal of the Custodian. |

Rules that hold for every row:

- **What a correction never does.** It never admits, advances, appoints, reinstates or sanctions anyone, and never changes a vote, a key, a governed file, a Landmark or Article XVI. No correction touches an amendment ballot or the ballot that removes a Custodian, and none stays, voids or undoes a ballot that adds a witness or advances a member to the 3rd degree: both count toward the Sunset that ends the Custodians' keys.
- **What no correction prevents.** A member whose key is frozen, and every member during a halt, can still depart, replace their own key, stop a recovery of their own key, and take part in an amendment ballot and in the ballot that removes a Custodian; a halt also keeps no one from asking for, co-signing or opposing a key recovery. **A member whose key is frozen commits and reveals in every ballot**: a freeze never changes who decides. A frozen member cannot object to a correction.
- **Recusal.** The Custodian never stays, voids or undoes a ballot whose subject, opener or sponsor is the Custodian's own member, and never holds that member out of the rooms. No Custodian stays, voids or undoes a ballot that adds a witness or advances a member to the 3rd degree. The ledger refuses such an entry.
- **Limits.** All Custodians together make at most five corrections in any 30 days, notes aside, and each Custodian at most its share of them: five divided by the number of live custodian keys, rounded up. An act that objections voided is not posted again on the same target for 90 days.
- **Withdrawal.** The Custodian who posted a correction that is in force or pending may withdraw it alone; the other Custodians withdraw it only together, as many as a noticed act needs. A withdrawn noticed act is never applied. A freeze or a room hold withdrawn by the other Custodians leaves no wait behind, and a void or undo they withdrew does not use up the one of its ballot. A halt and a stay are spent whoever withdraws them.
- **An undone admission is not a sanction.** The record reads `voided`: no offense is stated and no sponsor is banned. The person may be invited again 90 days after the undo, and for those 90 days the members may still expel the record for fraud by a sanction ballot (Constitution Art. IV.4, X.7).
- **Keys of members are not the Custodian's to give.** The Custodian can freeze a key; the Custodian cannot replace one. A member with a lost key recovers the membership through the Master Builders: after a freeze, two co-signers and 21 days of notice; without a freeze, which a recovery never needs, three co-signers, 30 days of notice and the right of the Master Builders to oppose, and while an active Master Builder who is not a sponsor of the member exists, the co-signers are not all sponsors; where fewer Master Builders exist, all of them and 45 days, counting only those who answered within the last year; a request whose co-signatures after 30 days, counted as one at least where another Master Builder exists, are fewer than it needs (and, without a freeze, whose notice had not begun by then) needs only those already given, at least one where another Master Builder exists, who may then all be sponsors, and 45 days of notice: after a freeze they start on that thirtieth day and the old key stays frozen meanwhile; without one, they start on that thirtieth day if the co-signatures then given are enough, and otherwise at the co-signature that makes them enough; a recovery reverses a departure when it is in time for it (Constitution Art. III.4): asked before the departure or within 30 days of it, or following, within 150 days of that recovery's request or of the old key's stop of it, a recovery in time for that departure, whatever ended that recovery, so that one follow-up may follow another; and a Council seat the departure ended comes back only into a vacancy; whoever holds the old key may stop a recovery once, while it is open, and the recovery that follows, asked no sooner than 90 days after the stop, needs three co-signers and cannot be stopped by that key ([RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md), section 1).

The Custodian drafts a correction with `om correct`; anyone lists them with `om corrections`.

## 5. What the Custodian never does

1. Never edits, deletes, reorders or rewrites any file in `ledger/entries/` or `ledger/reasons/`, and never force-pushes `main`. Witnesses and every clone would detect it; the ledger, not GitHub, decides what is lawful.
2. Never blocks, delays or closes a valid ledger pull request. Ledger-only pull requests that pass the judge merge automatically; the Custodian does not choose who joins, who votes or what passes.
3. Never admits, advances, suspends, expels or reinstates a member. Those are decisions of ballots, and no correction can make them.
4. Never blocks a **member** on GitHub. A block would also stop that member's own ledger entries, their departure included, which no correction may prevent. What the Custodian may do about a member's conduct is listed in section 10: hide or lock, a room hold of 14 days, and asking any Master Builder to open a sanction ballot.
5. Never lets anyone into a room or keeps anyone out by choice. Access follows the ledger; the one exception is the room hold of section 4, which is a correction. The Custodian is named on the public roll of every room it can read ([ROOMS.md](ROOMS.md)).
6. Never changes a governed file without a ratified amendment, and never disables the judge.
7. Never reads what is written in a room in order to measure, rank or build a case, and never collects or publishes personal data beyond what members publish themselves.
8. Never performs a moderation act without recording it, and never rests a correction or a request for a sanction on a fact that only the Custodian's private tools hold (section 8).
9. Never signs a correction on a false ground, to gain advantage in a ballot, or against a person rather than against a harm. That is abuse of a correction, a grave offense (Constitution Art. X.2, XVI.12).
10. Never sells, transfers or shares the administrator account or any of the three keys, except as section 9 prescribes.

## 6. How the Custodian is held to account

- **Every act is a ledger entry.** A correction is a signed entry with a ground and a public reason. A moderation act on GitHub is recorded as a `note`. Together they are the moderation log: there is no other ([MODERATION-LOG.md](MODERATION-LOG.md)). Anyone reads them with `om corrections`, and on the public Observatory once it is published.
- **Objection.** Any active Master Builder, dormant or not, may object to a correction by a signed entry (`om object`), within 90 days of it (of its application, for a noticed act that was applied), also during a halt. The member of the Custodian who posted it or co-signed it, the member the correction is aimed at, and a member whose key is frozen may not. A correction is void for good when its objections reach **one fifth of the eligible Masters, rounded up, and never fewer than one**. The eligible Masters are the active Master Builders who are not dormant, other than the members of the Custodians who signed and the member it is aimed at; a Master whose key is frozen is counted, though they cannot object. With five eligible Masters or fewer, a single objection is enough. An objection after a noticed act was applied is recorded and reverses nothing.
- **Removal.** Any Master Builder other than the Custodian's member may open the ballot that removes the Custodian (`om open custodian NAME`). The Custodian's member does not vote in it. It passes by a simple majority of the votes cast, with the ordinary quorum, in seven days to commit and three to reveal. It runs through any halt, a frozen member may vote in it, and no correction can stay, void or undo it. When it passes, the custodian key signs nothing more, every act it had in force ends, and no noticed act it posted is ever applied.
- **Appeal.** Anyone affected by an act of the Custodian, member or not, may appeal in a public issue labeled `appeal`. The Custodian answers there in public. For a correction, the answer that binds is the members': objection or removal.
- **Yearly report.** Once a year the Custodian publishes a report in the repository: every correction and note of the year with its outcome (in force, lapsed, withdrawn, voided by objection, applied), every use of the ruleset bypass, every change of settings against [LAUNCH.md](../operations/LAUNCH.md), every appeal and its answer, and the categories of data the Console held.
- **Limits by vote.** Members may, by an amendment ballot (Art. VII), tighten this charter or order an early transfer of administration. The Custodian complies.
- **Exit.** If the Custodian ever acts against this charter, the ledger keeps its force everywhere: any member can verify it, mirror it and continue elsewhere ([RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md), sections 4 and 7).

**An honest warning.** While few Master Builders exist, corrections are checked above all by publicity and the right to fork, and only by as many members as can object. While the Custodian's member is the only Master Builder, no one is eligible to object and no one can open the removal ballot. Where no Master Builder who is not a founder exists, no one can stop a founder's claim of the office.

## 7. Conflicts of interest

- The ledger enforces recusal for ballots (section 4) and keeps the Custodian's member out of objections to the Custodian's own corrections and out of the removal ballot.
- Beyond what the ledger enforces, the Custodian uses no administrative power in a dispute in which the Custodian's own member is a party. Such a case goes to the members: a sanction ballot or, once it exists, the Court of Review.
- As a member, the person votes, sponsors and examines like anyone else, and declares the office wherever a conflict could be seen: when sponsoring a candidate, when drawn as an examiner, when a ballot concerns an act of the office.
- The Custodian does not canvass any ballot by means of the office: not through the repository's announcements, not through moderation, not through the rooms.
- The Custodian may freeze the key of the Custodian's own member. It restricts no one else, and with one Custodian it is the only way that key, if stolen, is ever frozen.

## 8. The Console

To watch over the repository the Custodian uses a private tool, the **Console**. Its description is public: [CONSOLE.md](CONSOLE.md) lists its exact data sources and its limits. In short: it never reads what is written in a room; it keeps facts about a person for at most 90 days; any member may ask what it holds about their login; no correction and no sanction rests on a fact that only the Console holds; and it is handed to whoever holds the office next. The Console is a working tool of the Custodian and not a body: it decides nothing, and whatever of its output binds anyone is published. The Console is built and is not operating yet: it starts at the public launch.

## 9. How the office ends

A custodian key signs nothing, for good, from the first of these:

1. **Silence.** 180 days pass without a sign of life from that key.
2. **The Sunset.** The ledger shows seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses. The verifier records that moment, and from it every custodian key is dead, whether the genesis or a founder's claim named it.
3. **Revocation.** The Custodian signs one entry with the revoke key.
4. **Removal.** The Master Builders remove the key by ballot (section 6).
5. **The term.** Seven years (2,555 days) pass from the genesis, or, for a key a founder's claim named, from the application of that claim, whether or not the Sunset has come. Nothing moves the term: no sign of life, no halt.

When a key ends, every act it had in force ends with it, and no noticed act it posted is ever applied. **Where no Custodian key is live, no correction exists.** The ledger then has no freeze, no stay, no halt and no undo. A member whose key is lost is still recovered by their peers, because a recovery never needs a Custodian ([RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md), section 1); replacing a key one still holds needs no one.

**The founder's fallback.** Whenever no custodian key is live, the Sunset has not come and fewer than three active Master Builders who are not founders exist, any founder, whether or not that founder held the office before, may take up the office, as often as needed (`om claim-custodian`). One claim is pending at a time; its name is a login nobody holds, and no one is invited under it while the claim is pending; its two keys were never registered. The claim is signed with the founder's member key, comes from the founder's own GitHub account, and carries a new custodian key and a new revoke key, each proving itself. It is public for 30 days. Until the founder applies it, the objection of any one active Master Builder who is not a founder voids it (`om object-claim`), also after the 30 days; a new claim by any founder then waits 30 days. The founder then applies it (`om apply-claim`) between the 30th and the 60th day, every condition checked again that day; the new Custodian acts alone (a noticed act needs its one key) and is bound exactly as one of the genesis: silence, the Sunset, its revoke key, the removal ballot, and its own term of 2,555 days from the application. No founder against whose custodian key a removal ballot passed ever claims the office again, and no claim is accepted after the Sunset or while three or more active Master Builders who are not founders exist: in those cases the office is filled only by amendment.

At the Sunset the administration of the canonical repository and of the rooms passes to owners who are the witness operators (Constitution Art. XII.3), together with the Console and its history. A Custodian whose key ended earlier, by silence, revocation, removal or the end of its term, hands the administration to the owners the Master Builders name, and until then does nothing on GitHub but remove spam and unlawful content and moderate conversation as section 10 says, reporting each act in a public issue. This lasts no longer than the term of section 9, item 5.

The intended path after the Sunset is a college of Custodians drawn by lot among the Master Builders, for a term. It does not exist: it would be added by amendment, and until the members add it there is no correction power after the Sunset. The office is held in trust for the guardianship of the Supreme Council (Constitution Art. VIII) and gives way to it.

## 10. Moderation in practice

| Situation | What the Custodian does |
|---|---|
| Spam issue, pull request or comment from an account that is not a member | Hide or close it; block the account if it repeats. Record a `note`. |
| A flood of spam applications (public issues) | Limit interactions for a short time; record a `note` whose reason gives the end date. An application is not a ledger entry, and no one writes to the ledger without a Master Builder's invitation, so a flood cannot reach it. |
| An application from a real person | Nothing: the Custodian does not choose who is invited. Any Master Builder may invite the person. |
| A member breaks the Code of Conduct in the repository | Hide the comment if it harms someone, or lock the thread; record a `note`. Ask any Master Builder to open a sanction ballot. No unilateral sanction and no block. |
| A member harms others in a room | The same, in the room. If the harm cannot wait, a room hold of 14 days (a correction, open to objection). Nothing is deleted while a case is open. |
| Someone tries to decide something in a room, or presses others to keep silent about a room | It has no force. Say so in public, and ask a Master Builder to open a sanction ballot: binding anyone to silence about a room is a grave offense. |
| A member reports a lost or stolen key | Freeze the key (`freeze_key`) at the member's request, and point the member to [RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md). A stolen key is the urgent case: a freeze stops the thief at once. |
| A malicious code pull request | Close it with a short reason; treat it as a security report if it targets the judge or keys. |
| A dispute between members about a rule | Point them to the Counselor (`/om-counselor`) and, if needed, to an amendment or a ballot. The Custodian does not rule. |
| A request to reveal who someone is | Refuse. Record nothing about the request beyond its existence. |

Until members of the 10th degree exist to act as moderators (Code of Conduct), the Custodian moderates alone under this charter. From then on the Custodian follows their decisions in matters of conversation; corrections remain the acts of the custodian key, bounded as above.

**Where no custodian key is live.** Until members of the 10th degree exist, the owner of the repository on GitHub moderates under this charter: the acts on GitHub of section 3, item 3, and of the table above, bounded by section 5. A `note` needs a custodian key, so nothing is recorded in the ledger until a custodian key is live again (by a founder's claim, section 9, or by amendment): each act is reported in a public issue instead, with the action, its target and the rule applied, and never the identity of a person who reported harm. A room hold, and every other correction, waits for a custodian key in the same way. This lasts no longer than the term of section 9, item 5.
