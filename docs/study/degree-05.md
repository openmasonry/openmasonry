# 5°: Steward of Memory (study outline)

**House:** The Lodge of Perfection. Duty, stewardship and justice inside the community.  
**Virtue:** Gratitude  
**Art:** History and archives: honoring those who built before us  
**Lesson:** "We stand on unmarked foundations; name them."

## Purpose

The 5th degree asks a member to look back and give credit. Every institution, including this one, was built on the work of people whose names are often lost. A Steward of Memory recovers some of them and records them accurately, so that the fraternity knows what it inherits.

## Virtue: Gratitude

Gratitude is the acknowledgment of what one has received from others, expressed in an act. In historical work it becomes accuracy: naming people correctly, crediting their work and not inflating it. It is misread as nostalgia, as uncritical praise of predecessors, or as a debt that excuses their wrongs.

## Art

History and archives: how to find, evaluate and preserve records of the past. The core skills are distinguishing primary from secondary sources, dating a document, separating fact from tradition and legend, and describing a record so that others can find it. Fraternal societies (guilds, confraternities, mutual aid and burial societies, learned societies, lodges) exist across many regions and centuries, and many are poorly documented.

## Lesson

> "We stand on unmarked foundations; name them."

The lesson asks for names. Foundations are unmarked because those who laid them were not recorded, or were recorded wrongly. Naming them is both an act of gratitude and an act of truth.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> An archive entry documenting a person, lodge or idea from the history of fraternal societies, with sources

Practical pointers:

- Check the existing knowledge base (start at [`wiki/index.md`](../../wiki/index.md)) and choose a person, lodge or idea not yet documented, or documented poorly.
- Work from primary sources where possible, and cite every claim.
- Mark clearly what is documented, what is tradition and what is uncertain.
- Historical names may be cited as public reference; never reproduce or paraphrase any ritual, obligation or other private material of any body.
- Consider subjects from regions and traditions that are underrepresented in the existing catalog.

**Procedure.** At least 30 days in the 4th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 5th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The entry documents one person, lodge or idea from the history of fraternal societies.
- Every factual claim is sourced, and primary sources are used where they exist.
- Fact, tradition and conjecture are clearly distinguished.
- The entry contains no ritual or private material of any body.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Herodotus**, *Histories*, Book I (public-domain translations). History as the preservation of memory.
- **Seneca**, *On Benefits* (public-domain translations). Gratitude as a practice.
- **Marc Bloch**, *The Historian's Craft* (widely available).
- **International Council on Archives**, *Universal Declaration on Archives* (freely available).
- **Ibn Khaldun**, *The Muqaddimah*, introduction (widely available). Method and the criticism of historical reports.

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Curate the History catalog". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
