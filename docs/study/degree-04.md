## Purpose

The 4th degree opens the Lodge of Perfection, where study turns to duty inside the community. Its first duty is to handle information well: to tell what belongs to the public record from what is a person's private trust.

## Virtue: Discretion

Discretion is the judgment of what to say, to whom and when. It is not secrecy: Landmark II permits only four secrets (personal privacy, private keys, a ballot while it is open, and a flaw not yet repaired), and discretion keeps exactly those. It is misread as loyalty that hides wrongs, or as a reason to withhold what the rules make public.

## Art

The ethics of information studies privacy, confidentiality and the duty of silence: who has a claim on a piece of information, and how its context shapes its use. Key ideas are data minimization, purpose limitation, retention limits and consent. The ledger is a case study: it holds logins, keys, hashes and decisions, and personal content lives outside it (Constitution [Art. XIII](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-xiii-data-and-privacy)).

## Lesson

> "A secret that protects a person is a trust; a secret that protects power is a lie."

The lesson gives a test for any secret: whom does it protect? Shielding a person from harm is a trust; shielding an office or a group from scrutiny is a lie told to everyone else.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> A privacy review of one OpenMasonry process, with findings filed as issues

Practical pointers:

- Choose one process (the application, the petition, the rooms, a member tool) and map, step by step, what data it collects, where it is kept, who reads it and for how long.
- Test each step against Landmark II, Constitution Art. XIII and the [rules of the rooms](../governance/ROOMS.md), and file each finding, with its evidence and a proposed remedy, as a separate issue.
- A finding that is an unrepaired security flaw is one of the permitted secrets: report it privately as [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md) describes, and publish it once repaired.

**Procedure.** At least 180 days in the 3rd degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 4th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The process reviewed is clearly bounded and described accurately.
- Every finding cites the rule or principle it concerns and the evidence for it.
- The findings were filed as issues, or reported privately where they concern an unrepaired flaw.
- The review exposes no personal data.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **United Nations**, *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*, Article 12 (1948).
- **OECD**, *Guidelines on the Protection of Privacy and Transborder Flows of Personal Data* (freely available).
- **Helen Nissenbaum**, *Privacy in Context* (widely available).
- **Immanuel Kant**, "On a Supposed Right to Lie from Philanthropy" (public-domain translations).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Serve as a teller of sealed ballots, once encrypted ballots exist". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
