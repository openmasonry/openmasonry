# 3°: Master Builder (study guide)

**House:** The Craft Lodge. Self-knowledge and the tools of the work.  
**Virtue:** Fidelity  
**Art:** Music and Astronomy: harmony, cycles and the measure of a life  
**Lesson:** "The temple is never finished; the builder is measured by what outlives them."  
**Summary page:** [docs/degrees/degree-03.md](../degrees/degree-03.md) (generated from [`canon/degrees.toml`](../../canon/degrees.toml))

This is the full public study guide of the 3rd degree. Everything a candidate for the 3rd degree is examined on is written here or in the documents it links to. How the guides work is explained in the [README](README.md).

## 1. Purpose of the degree

The 3rd degree completes the Craft. A Master Builder is a full member in every sense (Constitution [Art. V.4](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-v-degrees)): they hold the legislative power of amendment together with all other Master Builders ([Art. II.1](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ii-separation-of-powers)), they may invite and sponsor new members, examine, hold Lodge office and found a Lodge. The degrees above it are degrees of further study and service, not of greater membership.

Because a Master Builder answers for others, the degree asks for a sustained, substantial piece of work and the fidelity to keep faith with people and with the work over time. Its art turns from the single task to the long view: harmony among many parts, and the cycles of a life and an institution.

## 2. The virtue: Fidelity

**What it is.** Fidelity is keeping faith over time: with promises made, with the work undertaken, with the people who rely on one, and with the truth. The Latin *fides* gave both "faith" and "confidence"; a faithful member is one others can rely on without checking every time. In OpenMasonry, where everything can be checked, fidelity is what makes checking rarely necessary.

**How it shows in practice.**

- Keeping commitments, or releasing them openly and in time when they cannot be kept.
- Answering for those one sponsors, and saying so publicly if one's trust turns out to have been misplaced.
- Maintaining what one built after the moment of credit has passed.
- Keeping the privacy of others, which the Declaration names as the one secret a member keeps.
- Staying faithful to the purpose of a rule, which sometimes means proposing to amend its letter by the public process.

**Common misreadings.**

- *Fidelity as loyalty to persons or factions.* Fidelity is owed to the Landmarks, to the work and to honest dealing, not to a group against the rest. A promise to cover for someone's wrong is not fidelity; the Declaration keeps "no secret of power", and reporting an offense is never a breach ([rules of the rooms §5](../governance/ROOMS.md#5-no-silence-only-privacy)).
- *Fidelity as rigidity.* Holding to a form after it has stopped serving its purpose can betray the purpose. A faithful builder repairs the structure.
- *Fidelity as exclusivity.* Members of other bodies are welcome and remain bound by their own obligations ([Whitepaper §12.1](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#121-independence)). Fidelity here does not require breaking faith elsewhere.
- *Fidelity as silence.* No one is bound to silence about a room, and binding anyone to silence is a grave offense (Constitution [Art. X.2](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-x-justice-sanctions-and-mercy)).

## 3. The art: Music and Astronomy

Music and astronomy were the last two of the four classical arts of number. Arithmetic studied number in itself, geometry number in space, music number in time and relation, astronomy number in motion and cycle. Both were taught as studies of harmony: how many parts, moving at different rates, can form an order. The canon adds the purpose: "the measure of a life".

### 3.1 Music: harmony and proportion

The oldest result of mathematical music theory, attributed to the Pythagorean tradition and found independently in other early traditions of tuning, is that the intervals the ear hears as consonant correspond to simple ratios of string length or frequency: the octave 2:1, the fifth 3:2, the fourth 4:3. Harmony, in this view, is proportion made audible.

Three ideas from music are useful to a Master Builder:

- **Consonance and dissonance.** Dissonance is not error; it creates tension that resolves. A community without disagreement is not harmonious, only silent. What matters is whether tensions resolve.
- **Tempo and rhythm.** A piece holds together because its parts keep a shared time. Institutions do too: ballots have windows, offices have terms, waiting periods set a pace.
- **Ensemble.** No player plays everything, and the best players listen more than they play. Leadership among equals resembles this more than command.

### 3.2 Astronomy: cycles and measure

The sky was the first clock. The day, the lunar month and the solar year do not divide evenly into one another, and every calendar is a compromise among them. Records of the sky kept across generations, in many cultures, are among the first works that outlived their makers.

OpenMasonry keeps its own cycles, all public and computable:

- The public randomness beacon used for lots produces a round every three seconds; a lot names a round about eight hours ahead (9,600 rounds), so that no one can know it in advance ([Whitepaper §7.4](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#74-the-lot-verifiable-sortition)).
- Light decays with a half-life of 180 days ([§7.6](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#76-light-a-record-of-service)): Light that is not renewed by work fades.
- Waiting periods set the minimum pace of the degrees ([§7.3](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#73-time-as-the-price-of-rank)); offices and Council seats have terms.

### 3.3 The measure of a life

Astronomy has always carried a second lesson: perspective. Seen against the scale of the sky, a single life is brief, and fame is local. The classical image of a dream in which a statesman looks down at the earth from the heavens and sees how small its glories are (Cicero's *Dream of Scipio*) is one expression of an idea found in many traditions. The conclusion drawn is not despair but a change of measure: a life is measured less by what it seizes than by what it leaves in good order for others.

### 3.4 Exercises

None of these is graded. A candidate for the 3rd degree holds the 2nd, so they share attempts in the room of the 2nd degree, which Master Builders also read.

1. **Ratios.** A string of length 60 sounds a note. Which lengths sound the octave, the fifth and the fourth above it? (Answers: 30, 40, 45.)
2. **Decay.** With a half-life of 180 days, what weight does an act carry after 360 days? After 540? (Answers: one quarter; one eighth.)
3. **Pace.** Using [`canon/degrees.toml`](../../canon/degrees.toml), add the waiting periods of the 1st to the 3rd degree and check that a member waits at least 90 days after admission to become a Master Builder, as the [degree table](../degrees/README.md) states. Then add what the two examinations and ballots take at their fastest (a beacon round 8 hours after each opening, 7 days to commit, a gap of 6 hours 15 minutes, every reveal at the first second of the window). (Answer: 105 days, 4 hours and 30 minutes.)
4. **What outlives.** List three things made by people you never met that you rely on daily. For one of them, find out who maintains it now.

## 4. The lesson, unpacked

> "The temple is never finished; the builder is measured by what outlives them."

**"The temple is never finished."** The builder's allegory reaches its center here. The shared structure (the fraternity, its commons, any institution worth having) is never complete. Every generation inherits it unfinished and passes it on unfinished. This is not failure; it is the condition of anything alive. A builder who waits for completion before feeling the work worthwhile will never feel it.

**"The builder is measured."** There is a measure, and it is not rank. Degrees grant duties, never weight (Landmark IV). The measure is applied by others, later.

**"By what outlives them."** What outlives a builder is what they made well enough, and shared openly enough, that others could keep using and repairing it: a clear text, a maintained tool, a member they mentored, a rule they improved. Work that depends on its author's presence, or that was kept private, does not outlive them. The lesson connects the 3rd degree to the 2nd (knowledge shared is a debt paid) and to the 33rd ("Guard the house so well that it never needs you").

## 5. The masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> A substantive masterwork (2,000+ words or equivalent work), defended before three examiners: public written questions and answers in the masterwork's public thread, never in a room

### 5.1 Choosing the work

"Substantive" means the work makes a real contribution: it says or does something that was not available before, with enough depth that a reader learns from it. The work may be:

- **A written work of 2,000 words or more:** an essay, a study, a sourced history, a reasoned proposal for the fraternity.
- **Equivalent work:** a contribution of comparable substance in another form, such as a significant body of code, a curriculum, a design with its rationale, a data set with its documentation, or a work of music or art with a written account. The canon does not define equivalence. A candidate who chooses this path should state, in a cover note, why the work is equivalent in substance and effort to 2,000 words of serious writing, so that examiners can judge it against that claim.

Choose a subject in which the virtue, the art or the lesson of the degree can be made concrete: for example, a study of how an institution kept faith with its purpose across a long cycle; an analysis of harmony and dissonance in a community's decision-making; a maintained tool and an account of keeping it alive; a proposal to improve a rule of OpenMasonry, with evidence.

### 5.2 Producing it

**A suggested structure** for a written work (adapt for other forms):

1. **Abstract** (100 to 150 words): the question, the approach, the conclusion.
2. **Introduction:** why the question matters and to whom.
3. **Background:** what is already known, with sources.
4. **Body:** the argument or the work itself, in sections, each with one main point.
5. **Objections:** the strongest arguments against your conclusion, stated fairly, and your answers.
6. **Conclusion:** what follows, what remains open, what the reader can do with it.
7. **References:** every source cited, in a consistent style.

**A suggested schedule** for the 60 days of waiting: two weeks to scope, two to research and outline, three to draft, one to revise and publish.

**Sources.** Prefer primary sources and freely available ones, so that examiners and readers can check them. Quote briefly, attribute every quotation and borrowed idea, and distinguish clearly between what a source says and what you conclude.

**Authorship.** The work is the candidate's. Comments on drafts are welcome and should be acknowledged; work done by someone or something else and presented as one's own fails.

**Publishing.** Publish the work at a stable public `http(s)` address. Content lives outside the ledger, which keeps only references, so that an author can erase their own content ([Whitepaper §12.3](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#123-data-protection)). The work may be recorded with `bin/om work <url>` (one `work` entry per member in any 24 hours).

### 5.3 What "defended" means here

The canon defines the defense: public written questions and answers in the masterwork's public thread, never in a room. The specification records an examination as the draw of the examiners, each examiner's verdict (`pass` or `fail`) and, optionally, the address of the examiner's published reason ([SPEC §6.10](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#610-exam_report)); the defense itself is not a ledger entry. So a defense is:

- **Public and written.** Examiners judge only what is public (Constitution [Art. XV.5](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-xv-rooms)). Questions an examiner wants answered are asked in the masterwork's public thread, where anyone can read them (for example as comments on the published work), and the candidate answers there. A good defense addresses every question asked.
- **Never in a room.** No examination is held or canvassed in a room ([Art. XV.1 and XV.5](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-xv-rooms)).
- **Within the time limit.** All of it must fit within the 60 days the examiners have to report.

### 5.4 From work to degree: the examination and the ballot

The rules below are those of Constitution [Art. V](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-v-degrees) and of the specification ([§6.8 to §6.11](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#68-exam_open), [§7.3](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), [§6.4.2](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#642-kind-advancement)). Where this summary and the specification differ, the specification governs.

1. **Waiting period.** The examination can be opened once the candidate has held the 2nd degree for at least **60 days**.
2. **Opening.** Only the candidate opens it, with `bin/om exam 3 --work <url>`. The entry freezes the pool of possible examiners and their number, and names a public beacon round about eight hours ahead.
3. **The examiners.** The canon asks for **three**, drawn from the members of the 3rd degree and above, the candidate and their sponsors excluded where enough others exist; where too few members exist, the number is lowered to those who can be drawn. In short: three examiners, or fewer, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder).
4. **The draw** is posted by a witness or any member (`bin/om draw`) after the beacon round; a lot not drawn within 7 days of its round ends the examination without penalty.
5. **Conflicts.** An examiner with a conflict of interest recuses and is replaced by the next in the lot's order; if nobody is left, the examination lapses without penalty and may be opened again at once.
6. **Verdicts.** The examination passes with a strict majority of "pass" reports among the drawn examiners and fails when "fail" reports reach half of them. With three examiners, two reports decide it either way.
7. **Time limit and retries.** Without a majority verdict 60 days after opening, the examination times out with no penalty and may be opened again at once; examiners who never reported are left out of the next lot where possible, and that lot seats everyone else who can examine. After a failure, a new examination may be opened 90 days after the failed one was opened.
8. **The advancement ballot.** After a passed examination, the candidate or one of its examiners opens the ballot (`bin/om open advancement <login>`). The electors are the Master Builders, without the candidate, dormant ones included (the quorum counts the present ones). The ballot has seven days to commit and, after a gap of 6 hours 15 minutes, three to reveal (it closes early once every elector has revealed), needs a quorum, and is lost when black balls reach one fifth of the votes cast; a ballot lost on the votes counts as a failed examination.

## 6. Examination criteria

Examiners judge the work against the degree's stated art and virtue (Constitution [Art. V.5](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-v-degrees)) and the requirement of the canon, publish a short reasoned verdict and declare any conflict. A "pass" should rest on all the criteria below; a "fail" should name which is not met and why.

| Criterion | Met when |
|---|---|
| 1. Requirement | A written work has at least 2,000 words of the candidate's own text (quotations and references not counted), or the cover note of another form makes a reasoned case for equivalence that the examiner accepts on stated grounds. The work is published. |
| 2. Substance | The work contributes something a reader could not easily find elsewhere: an argument, a synthesis, a tool, a finding. It goes beyond summary. |
| 3. Art (music and astronomy) | The work engages harmony, cycles or the measure of a life, in its subject or its method: for example by treating long time scales, the fit of many parts, or what endures. |
| 4. Virtue (fidelity) | The work keeps faith with its sources (accurate quotation and fair summary), with its readers (claims supported, limits stated) and with its subject. |
| 5. Defense | Every question an examiner asked in public was answered in public within the time of the examination. |
| 6. Authorship and sources | The work is the candidate's own; help is acknowledged; sources are traceable. |

Not criteria: whether the examiner agrees with the conclusion, the subject chosen among those that fit the degree, the candidate's beliefs, style or language variety, their Light, their activity in the rooms, their sponsors, or their seniority.

## 7. Reading

- **Plato**, *Timaeus* (Jowett translation, public domain, on Project Gutenberg). Proportion, harmony and the cosmos in the classical imagination.
- **Cicero**, *The Dream of Scipio* (the closing book of *On the Republic*; public-domain translations). The measure of a life seen from the heavens.
- **Cicero**, *On Duties*, Book I (public-domain translations). Good faith as the foundation of justice.
- **Hermann von Helmholtz**, *On the Sensations of Tone* (A. J. Ellis translation, public domain). The physics and perception of consonance.
- **Marcus Aurelius**, *Meditations* (public-domain translations). Brevity of life and the work that remains.
- **OpenStax**, *Astronomy* (open textbook). Cycles of the sky, calendars and the scale of the universe.
- **Open Music Theory** (an open, collectively written textbook). Intervals, harmony and form.

## 8. Questions for the room

These prompts are for conversation in the room of the 2nd degree, where candidates for the 3rd degree study, and in the room of the 3rd degree. No examination, examiner or candidate is canvassed in either.

1. What is the difference between fidelity to a person and fidelity to the work? When can they conflict?
2. Which tension in OpenMasonry's rules is a productive dissonance, and which one should be resolved?
3. Why does the fraternity measure its pace in days that cannot be bought?

## 9. What the degree unlocks

The canon states: "Sponsor candidates; examine candidates for the 2nd and 3rd degrees when drawn by lot, and for any higher degree whose own members are too few; be elected to Lodge office; found a Lodge". In the terms of the Constitution:

- **Sponsor:** invite and sponsor candidates, holding at most four sponsorships in any 365 days, live invitations included (an invitation for 90 days after it lapsed too; a sponsored member who departed no longer counts) ([Art. IV](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-iv-sponsorship)).
- **Examine:** every Master Builder belongs to the rungs from which examiners are drawn when the members of the degree sought are too few ([Art. V.7](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-v-degrees)), besides the first rung of the 3rd degree's own lots.
- **Lodge office:** the Worshipful Master and the Wardens are 3°+ ([Art. IX.2](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ix-lodges-and-offices)).
- **Found a Lodge:** a Lodge is a group of at least seven members with a regular rhythm of study; until federation all members belong to Open Lodge No. 1 ([Art. IX.1](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ix-lodges-and-offices)).

A Master Builder also votes on amendments and on witnesses, and may object to corrections. All of this is duty and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV): a Master Builder holds one vote, as every member does.

## 10. Beyond the Craft

The 4th degree, the first of the Lodge of Perfection, requires at least 180 days in the 3rd. Its [outline](degree-04.md) describes it. No member is ever required to go further: the 3rd degree is the whole of membership.
