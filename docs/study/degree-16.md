# 16°: Prince of the Rebuilt City (study outline)

**House:** The Chapter of the Rose. Rebuilding, liberty of conscience and active charity.  
**Virtue:** Civic courage  
**Art:** Civic governance: assemblies, mandates and accountability  
**Lesson:** "A city is rebuilt by citizens, not by princes."

## Purpose

The 16th degree prepares members to convene their peers. Self-government needs assemblies where members deliberate in the open, and assemblies need conveners who make them fair, accessible and accountable.

## Virtue: Civic courage

Civic courage is the willingness to speak and act in public for the common good, at personal cost. It is misread as boldness for its own sake, or as the claim to speak for others without a mandate.

## Art

Civic governance: assemblies, mandates and accountability. The study covers agendas, rules of procedure, facilitation, inclusion of those who cannot attend at one time or in one language, minutes, the mandate of delegates, and the line between deliberation and decision: in OpenMasonry, decisions are made only by signed entries in the ledger.

## Lesson

> "A city is rebuilt by citizens, not by princes."

Rebuilding is done by many hands with equal standing. The lesson rejects the idea that a community is saved by a leader; a convener serves the assembly, not the reverse.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> Organize and document one assembly, with minutes published in the repository

Practical pointers:

- Publish the agenda, the rules of procedure and the means of access in advance.
- Make participation possible for those in other time zones or with disabilities.
- Keep minutes that record what was discussed and proposed, naming participants only with their consent.
- Make clear that any decision must follow by ledger ballot; the assembly itself decides nothing.
- Publish the minutes in the repository.

**Procedure.** At least 60 days in the 15th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 16th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The assembly was announced openly and was accessible to all eligible members.
- The minutes are accurate, complete, published in the repository and respect privacy.
- The convener's role was facilitation, not control.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Aristotle**, *Politics*, Book III (public-domain translations).
- **Thucydides**, the Funeral Oration in *History of the Peloponnesian War*, Book II (public-domain translations).
- **John Stuart Mill**, *Considerations on Representative Government* (public domain).
- **Jo Freeman**, "The Tyranny of Structurelessness" (widely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Convene assemblies". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
