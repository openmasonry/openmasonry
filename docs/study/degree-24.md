## Purpose

The 24th degree studies symbols and their meanings. The fraternity uses public symbols (the builder's tools, the open book) and keeps a catalog of them. An Interpreter of Symbols documents what they have meant, with sources, without imposing a single reading.

## Virtue: Understanding

Understanding is grasping a thing from more than one side, including from the perspective of those who see it differently. It is misread as having the one correct interpretation.

## Art

Semiotics: symbols, allegory and their many honest readings. The study covers the sign and what it refers to, convention and resemblance, allegory, the history of a symbol's uses, and the difference between documented use and later speculation.

## Lesson

> "A symbol is a question that each generation must answer again."

A symbol does not deliver its meaning once and for all. Each generation inherits the question it poses and must answer it again, in its own terms.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> Ten entries in the Symbols catalog with sources and multiple interpretations

Practical pointers:

- Choose ten public symbols not yet documented in the catalog, or poorly documented.
- For each, give documented historical uses with sources, and at least two honest interpretations, from different traditions where possible.
- Distinguish documented use from speculation.
- Reveal nothing private of any body; public symbols only.

**Procedure.** At least 60 days in the 23rd degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 24th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- Ten entries, each with sources and more than one interpretation.
- Documented use and speculation are clearly distinguished.
- No private material of any body is included.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Ferdinand de Saussure**, *Course in General Linguistics* (widely available).
- **Charles Sanders Peirce**, writings on signs (widely available).
- **Umberto Eco**, *A Theory of Semiotics* (widely available).
- **Albert G. Mackey**, *The Symbolism of Freemasonry* (1869, public domain), as historical reference.
- **Paul Ricoeur**, *The Symbolism of Evil*, conclusion "The Symbol Gives Rise to Thought" (widely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Curate the Symbols catalog". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
