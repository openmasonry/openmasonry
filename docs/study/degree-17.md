# 17°: Knight of East and West (study outline)

**Art:** Comparative traditions: philosophies, religions and cultures in dialogue  
**Lesson:** "No single tradition owns the light; each one carries a lamp."

## Purpose

The 17th degree turns study outward to the many traditions of humankind. OpenMasonry admits people of any belief or unbelief (Landmark I), and its members need the knowledge to meet one another's traditions with understanding.

## Virtue: Universality

Universality is the recognition of what is shared across traditions without erasing what is distinct. It is misread as the claim that all traditions say the same thing, or as a neutral stance that understands none of them deeply.

## Art

Comparative traditions: philosophies, religions and cultures in dialogue. The study covers comparison without ranking, reading each tradition in its own terms and sources, the problem of translating key concepts, and the voices of practitioners as well as scholars.

## Lesson

> "No single tradition owns the light; each one carries a lamp."

Each tradition carries a light of its own and none holds all of it. The lesson does not say all lamps are the same; it says none may claim to be the only one.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> A comparative study across at least three traditions, published in its original language and in one translation into a second language

Practical pointers:

- Choose one theme (hospitality, the stranger, justice, work, death, the image of the builder) and study it in at least three traditions from different regions.
- Work from primary texts and describe each tradition as its own practitioners would recognize it.
- Publish the study in the language it was written in and in one translation into a second language, and record the choices made for key terms.
- Avoid ranking; where traditions disagree, state the disagreement fairly.

**Procedure.** At least 60 days in the 16th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 17th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- At least three traditions are studied, each from its own sources.
- Comparisons are accurate and fair, without ranking.
- The study is published in its original language and in one translation into a second language, and the translation is faithful.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Al-Bīrūnī**, *India* (Edward Sachau translation, public domain). An early comparative study of another civilization.
- **Gotthold Ephraim Lessing**, *Nathan the Wise* (public-domain translations), the parable of the three rings.
- **F. Max Müller, ed.**, *The Sacred Books of the East* (public domain).
- **Huston Smith**, *The World's Religions* (widely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Lead translation and outreach programs". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
