## Purpose

The 10th degree prepares members to protect public conversation. A fraternity that decides in public depends on forums where people can disagree without being driven out by harassment or bad faith.

## Virtue: Tolerance

Tolerance is the acceptance of persons and views one disagrees with, as equals in the conversation. It is misread as indifference, as the acceptance of any behavior, or as neutrality between those who argue and those who try to silence them.

## Art

Moderation: how communities protect dialogue. The study covers codes of conduct, proportional and graduated responses, transparency of moderation acts, appeals, consistency, the difference between moderating behavior and moderating opinions, and the care of moderators themselves. The project's [Code of Conduct](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) sets the frame.

## Lesson

> "Tolerate persons, never the abolition of tolerance."

The lesson states the limit of tolerance precisely: persons and opinions are tolerated; the attempt to abolish tolerance (harassment, threats, silencing) is not. The limit is drawn at acts, not at views.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> Moderate a public forum of OpenMasonry or another community for 60 days with a published moderation log

Practical pointers:

- Moderate a forum in which those responsible for it have given the candidate the role, for at least 60 days. Under the [Code of Conduct](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) the moderators of OpenMasonry are members of the 10th degree and above, so a candidate of the 9th degree usually moderates the public forum of another community.
- Log every moderation act: the action, its target (a login at most), the rule applied, and never the identity of whoever reported.
- Use the lightest effective response first, and offer an appeal.
- Recuse from any thread in which the candidate is a participant in the disagreement.

**Procedure.** At least 30 days in the 9th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 10th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- Moderation covered at least 60 days, and the log is public and complete.
- Each act cites the rule applied and is proportional to the behavior.
- Acts targeted behavior, not opinions.
- The log exposes no personal data and no reporter.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **John Locke**, *A Letter Concerning Toleration* (public domain).
- **Voltaire**, *Treatise on Tolerance* (public-domain translations).
- **John Stuart Mill**, *On Liberty*, Chapter II (public domain).
- **Ashoka**, the Rock Edicts, especially Edict XII on respect among schools of thought (public-domain translations).
- **The Santa Clara Principles on Transparency and Accountability in Content Moderation** (freely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Become a forum moderator". Under the [Code of Conduct](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), moderators are members of the 10th degree and above. These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
