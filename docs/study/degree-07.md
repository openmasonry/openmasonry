## Purpose

The 7th degree prepares members to judge. Arbiters hold part of the judicial power of the fraternity (Constitution [Art. II.3](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ii-separation-of-powers)), so the degree trains the habits a fair judgment requires before any member is given that duty.

## Virtue: Justice

Justice is giving each party what is due by a rule that would be applied to anyone in the same position. It requires hearing both sides, deciding on evidence and keeping the response proportional to the wrong. It is misread as punishment, as the victory of the more sympathetic party, or as strict rule-following that ignores proportion.

## Art

Dispute resolution: how disputes are framed, heard and decided. The study covers the right to be heard, the burden and standard of proof, the weighing of evidence, proportionality, conflicts of interest and recusal, and the structure of a reasoned decision: facts, question, applicable rule, arguments, analysis and outcome. The Constitution sets the frame for OpenMasonry in [Art. X](../../CONSTITUTION.md#article-x-justice-sanctions-and-mercy).

## Lesson

> "Judge the act, not the person; judge yourself first."

The lesson repeats the Declaration and applies it to judging. An arbiter decides whether an act broke a rule, not what kind of person committed it, and an arbiter who has not first examined their own bias is not ready to decide.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> Serve as an observer in one dispute and write a reasoned opinion

Practical pointers:

- Observe only in the capacity the dispute's process allows, and declare any connection to a party first; no one sits in judgment of a matter in which they are involved (Constitution Art. II.5).
- Base the opinion on the public material of the dispute only, and include no personal data.
- Structure the opinion: facts, questions, rules, each side's best argument, analysis, proportional outcome.
- State clearly that the opinion is an exercise with no force, and where it agrees or disagrees with the actual outcome, say why.

**Procedure.** At least 30 days in the 6th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 7th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The opinion states both sides' arguments fairly, in their strongest form.
- Its conclusion follows from stated facts and cited rules.
- The proposed outcome is proportional to the act, and the reasoning shows why.
- No personal data is disclosed, and any conflict of interest was declared.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Aristotle**, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book V (public-domain translations). Justice and equity.
- **United Nations**, *Bangalore Principles of Judicial Conduct* (freely available).
- **Roger Fisher and William Ury**, *Getting to Yes* (widely available).
- **Lon L. Fuller**, "The Forms and Limits of Adjudication" (widely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Sit on dispute juries". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
