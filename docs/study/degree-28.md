## Purpose

The 28th degree applies the methods of science to the fraternity's own rules. Rules rest on assumptions about how people behave; a Natural Philosopher tests one of them against evidence.

## Virtue: Truthfulness

Truthfulness is saying what one believes to be true, in proportion to the evidence, including when it is unwelcome. It is misread as bluntness, or as certainty.

## Art

Science and epistemology: evidence, doubt and the limits of knowing. The study covers how claims are tested, the quality and kinds of evidence, systematic review, bias, uncertainty, and how to state a conclusion with the confidence it deserves.

## Lesson

> "Doubt is the lantern; certainty is where the lantern goes out."

Doubt lights the way to knowledge; the moment a question is treated as settled, inquiry stops. The lesson does not condemn conclusions, only the certainty that refuses to look again.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> An evidence review that recommends changing or confirming one OpenMasonry rule (an amendment it proposes need not pass)

Practical pointers:

- Choose one rule of OpenMasonry that rests on a factual claim (for example about participation, deterrence or decay).
- State the method before gathering evidence, and follow it.
- Use the public record, the literature or reproducible simulations, and publish the data.
- Conclude whether the evidence supports the rule, and with what confidence. A change to a rule happens only by the amendment process; the review recommends, and an amendment it proposes need not pass for the masterwork to be complete.

**Procedure.** At least 60 days in the 27th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 28th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The question and method are stated in advance and followed.
- The evidence is relevant, published and fairly weighed.
- The conclusion matches the strength of the evidence.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **David Hume**, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (public domain).
- **W. K. Clifford**, "The Ethics of Belief" (1877, public domain).
- **Charles Sanders Peirce**, "The Fixation of Belief" (1877, public domain).
- **Karl Popper**, *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (widely available).
- **Cochrane**, *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions* (freely available online).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Chair evidence reviews". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
