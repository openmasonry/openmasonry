## Purpose

The 22nd degree honors labor, especially the labor that goes unseen. Open communities run on maintenance, review, translation, documentation and care that rarely appear in the credits. This degree finds that work and names it.

## Virtue: Respect for work

Respect for work is the recognition of every contribution in proportion to its value, whoever made it and however visible it is. It is misread as celebrating only the famous, or as counting only what is easy to measure.

## Art

Sustainability of labor: maintainers, burnout and fair recognition. The study covers the forms of invisible labor in open projects, methods for attributing contributions, the causes and signs of burnout, and recognition practices that are fair and consented to.

## Lesson

> "Honor the hands that cut the cedar, not only the names carved on it."

The names carved on a building are rarely the names of those who built it. The lesson asks the fraternity to honor the hands as well as the names.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> A recognition audit: identify and credit uncredited contributors

Practical pointers:

- Choose a scope (a repository, a catalog, a period) and state the method used to find contributions: history, reviews, issue threads, translations, non-code work.
- Ask each contributor found whether and how they wish to be credited; some prefer not to be named.
- Correct the record with their consent, through the resource's normal process.
- Publish the method and the results so that the audit can be repeated.

**Procedure.** At least 60 days in the 21st degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 22nd degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- The method is stated and could be repeated by someone else.
- Uncredited contributions were identified with evidence.
- Credit was given with the contributors' consent.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Nadia Eghbal**, *Roads and Bridges: The Unseen Labor Behind Our Digital Infrastructure* (freely available).
- **William Morris**, "Useful Work versus Useless Toil" (public domain).
- **Christina Maslach and Michael P. Leiter**, *The Truth About Burnout* (widely available).
- **The All Contributors specification** (open).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Run the contributor-recognition rota". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
