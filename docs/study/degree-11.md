# 11°: Elect of the Twelve (study outline)

**House:** The Lodge of Perfection. Duty, stewardship and justice inside the community.  
**Virtue:** Equity  
**Art:** Economics of the commons: Ostrom's principles, free riding and fair burdens  
**Lesson:** "A commons dies of silence before it dies of theft."  
**Summary page:** [docs/degrees/degree-11.md](../degrees/degree-11.md) (generated from [`canon/degrees.toml`](../../canon/degrees.toml))

## Purpose

The 11th degree studies the health of shared resources. OpenMasonry is itself a commons (its code, texts, catalogs and ledger) and survives only if its burdens are shared fairly and its rules fit its use.

## Virtue: Equity

Equity is fairness that takes circumstances into account: burdens and benefits distributed in proportion, not merely equally. It is misread as identical treatment regardless of situation, or as the redistribution of credit without regard to work.

## Art

Economics of the commons: how communities govern shared resources without either a single owner or a market. The study centers on Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles for durable commons (clearly defined boundaries; rules that fit local conditions; collective choice by those affected; monitoring; graduated sanctions; accessible conflict resolution; recognition of the right to organize; nested layers of governance), and on free riding and the fair allocation of burdens. The Whitepaper maps the Landmarks to these principles in [§3.2](../whitepaper/WHITEPAPER.md#32-mapping-to-ostroms-principles-for-durable-commons).

## Lesson

> "A commons dies of silence before it dies of theft."

A commons is rarely destroyed by one dramatic theft. It decays first because no one reports problems, no one maintains, and no one speaks when the rules stop fitting. The lesson makes silence the first threat.

## Masterwork

The canon states it exactly as follows:

> A commons-health report on one OpenMasonry resource, scored against Ostrom's eight principles

Practical pointers:

- Choose one resource (a repository, a catalog, the rooms, a process) and define its boundaries and users.
- Score it against each of the eight principles on a stated scale, with evidence for every score.
- Identify free riding and uneven burdens, with data where possible.
- End with prioritized recommendations, each tied to a principle.

**Procedure.** At least 30 days in the 10th degree; then three examiners, drawn from the members of the 11th degree and above, then from the Master Builders, as drawn by lot under the rules in [SPEC.md](../whitepaper/SPEC.md#73-the-examiner-pool-ladder), each publishing a reasoned verdict; then an advancement ballot of peers (steps in the [guide of the 3rd degree](degree-03.md#54-from-work-to-degree-the-examination-and-the-ballot)).

## Examination criteria

- All eight principles are addressed, each with a score and evidence.
- The scale is stated and applied consistently.
- Recommendations follow from the findings.
- The work is the candidate's own; help and sources are acknowledged and traceable.

## Reading

- **Elinor Ostrom**, *Governing the Commons* (widely available).
- **Garrett Hardin**, "The Tragedy of the Commons" (1968, widely available), read with Ostrom's response.
- **Mancur Olson**, *The Logic of Collective Action* (widely available).
- **Charlotte Hess and Elinor Ostrom, eds.**, *Understanding Knowledge as a Commons* (widely available).

## What it unlocks

The canon states: "Review proposed amendments to operational rules for their effect on the commons". These are duties and eligibility, never weight (Landmark IV).
