## Abstract

For three centuries, speculative Freemasonry has been one of humanity's most durable institutions for self-improvement, mutual aid and voluntary association. Its lodges were among the first places where commoners and aristocrats elected officers, kept minutes, wrote constitutions and voted under rules: early practice for constitutional life, as historians of the Enlightenment have documented. The same structure also produced an institution that is **closed** (restricted by sex, sometimes by creed and, historically, by race), **opaque** (its governance and records are private) and therefore **capturable**: a small group with access to secrecy, patronage and permanent office can turn a school of virtue into a network of influence.

**OpenMasonry** keeps what made the Craft durable: the progression of degrees, sponsorship, the ballot, the lodge as a school, the ethic of building. It reverses the conditions that allow capture:

1. **Everything that governs is public and verifiable**: rules, records, decisions, finances and the teaching of every degree. The only secrets are personal privacy, private keys, a ballot in progress and a flaw not yet repaired. Rooms of study may be closed, but they are never secret and they decide nothing (§10.6).
2. **Degrees grant duties and eligibility, never extra votes.** One member, one vote.
3. **No permanent power.** Every office has a term, the highest body is filled by verifiable lot, and a member who stops taking part stops being counted, and keeps the vote.
4. **No tokens, no money, no purchasable rank or access.** Status is never bought. After the founding it is earned by work and conferred by peers or by lot.
5. **The rules are public text, and code wherever the ledger enforces them.** The whole fraternity, every admission, examination, ballot and degree since genesis, can be replayed and checked offline by any person with a copy of the repository. No record is ever rewritten or erased.

One exception to "no one holds special power" is deliberate, bounded and stated here rather than hidden. A young fraternity must be able to repair damage (a stolen key, a ballot captured by fraud, an attack) before it has enough members to judge such things. Until then a founder whom the genesis names holds, besides a member's single vote, the interim office of **Custodian**: the power to make **corrections**, which are new signed public entries from a closed menu, on closed grounds, each with a public reason. A correction may undo, freeze or pause; it never admits, advances, appoints, sanctions or changes a vote, and it rewrites nothing. Master Builders stop a correction by signed objection and remove the Custodian by a simple majority, and the office ends by rule when the ledger shows seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses (§9.2), and in any case seven years after it was taken up. While the Custodian's own member is the only Master Builder, corrections are checked by publicity and the right to fork, not yet by members.

The system runs on **GitHub** as its social layer (issues, pull requests, discussions, Actions), on a **hash-chained, signed ledger committed to Git** as its source of truth, on **independent witness nodes** that checkpoint the ledger, and on **public timestamp anchoring** (OpenTimestamps → Bitcoin) plus multiple mirrors for permanence. It is a blockchain in the only sense that matters here (an append-only chain of signed, hash-linked records that nobody can silently rewrite) without a token, a fee market or mining.

This paper describes the history it draws on (§2), the eternal Landmarks (§3), the architecture (§4), identity and Sybil resistance (§5), the ballot and its decision function (§6), the 33 degrees and the Supreme Council (§7), the integrity and threat model (§8), autonomous operation and the Custodian's interim office (§9), governance (§10), education (§11), ethics and law (§12), and the roadmap (§13). A working reference implementation with tests ships alongside this paper.

## 1. Why an Open Craft

### 1.1 What the Craft got right

Freemasonry endured because it solved real human problems with simple, repeatable forms:

| Problem | Masonic form | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Adults rarely keep learning | **Degrees**: a staged curriculum with symbolic lessons | Progression gives structure, milestones and identity |
| Trust between strangers | **Sponsorship**: a candidate is proposed and seconded by members | Reputation is staked by people who know the candidate |
| Factions and conflict | **The ballot** and **harmony** as a value | Admission requires broad consent, so the group stays cohesive |
| Abuse of office | **Annual elections**, written **constitutions**, minutes | Early practice in rule-bound self-government |
| Isolation | **The lodge**: a regular, local, ritualized gathering | Belonging, mutual aid and friendship |
| Meaning | **Allegory and symbol** (tools of the builder) | Ethics taught through images rather than doctrine |

Historians such as Margaret C. Jacob have argued that eighteenth-century lodges were "schools of government" where members practiced constitutional procedure before their states did [Jacob 1991].

### 1.2 What went wrong

The same institution carries structural weaknesses that are well documented:

- **Closure.** Mainstream "regular" Freemasonry admits only men and, in most jurisdictions, requires belief in a Supreme Being. Mixed and women's grand lodges (for example *Le Droit Humain*, founded in 1893 by Maria Deraismes and Georges Martin) exist but are generally not recognized by the mainstream [Le Droit Humain; Wikipedia: Co-Freemasonry]. Prince Hall Freemasonry, founded by Black Americans in the 1770s–1780s after they were refused by colonial lodges, waited until the late twentieth century for widespread recognition [Wikipedia: African Lodge No. 459].
- **Opacity enables capture.** The most notorious case is the Italian lodge *Propaganda Due* (P2), whose membership list, discovered in 1981, included ministers, generals, intelligence chiefs and bankers; the Grand Orient of Italy had withdrawn its charter in 1976, and it had become a parallel power structure. The lesson is not that fraternities are sinister. **Any network that combines secrecy, patronage and permanence will be captured by the people who benefit from it.**
- **Decline.** The United Grand Lodge of England reports 168,961 members in 2023/24 (about 19,000 of them overseas); in England and Wales alone membership fell from around 200,000 to a little over 150,000, an average decline of about 2.5% per year since 2008 [UGLE 2022; UGLE Annual Report 2023/24]. In the United States, mainstream Grand Lodge membership peaked at about 4.1 million in 1959 and fell below 1 million around 2020 (about 870,000 in 2023) [MSANA]. The forms that once attracted people now repel many of them: secrecy reads as exclusion, ceremony without explanation reads as costume.
- **Unverifiable authority.** A member cannot independently verify who was admitted, how a ballot was counted, or why someone holds high rank. Trust flows top-down.

### 1.3 The thesis

> **Power accumulates where three things meet: secrecy, discretion and permanence.**
> Remove any one and capture becomes expensive. Remove all three and it becomes visible.

OpenMasonry is an attempt to keep the **forms** (degrees, sponsorship, ballot, lodge, symbol) while removing **secrecy** (every rule, record and decision is public and replayable, and so is the teaching of every degree), **discretion** (every rule is public text, and code with an exhaustive test suite wherever the ledger enforces it) and **permanence** (terms, lot, and members who stop taking part no longer being counted). The goal is not to destroy anything. It is to build an open successor that anyone can join, audit and fork, and which no person, including its founder, can own.

One discretion remains for a time, and the thesis is applied to it rather than suspended. While the fraternity is too small to check itself, the Custodian may repair damage by corrections (§9.2). That power is kept from the three conditions of capture one by one: it is **not secret** (every correction is a signed ledger entry with a public reason), it is **bounded discretion** (a closed menu of acts and of grounds, limits the verifier enforces, and the objection of members), and it is **not permanent** (it ends by silence, by revocation, by a ballot of the Master Builders, at the Founder's Sunset or at the end of a seven-year term; no ballot or lot appoints a Custodian, and the one way the office is taken up again is a founder's public claim, open to objection, while few Master Builders exist).

## 2. What we inherit: a short, sourced history

This section is a map, not a history book. The [wiki](../../wiki/) holds the long-form catalog with sources.

```
 c.1390      c.1450      1598-99         1717        1723           1751        1801          1813       1893
   │           │           │               │           │              │           │             │          │
 Regius      Cooke MS    Schaw Statutes  Premier GL  Anderson's     "Antients"  Supreme       UGLE       Le Droit
 Poem        (Old        (Scotland:      of London   Constitutions  vs          Council 33°   (union)    Humain
 (oldest     Charges)    lodges,         (4 lodges)  (6 Charges)    "Moderns"   Charleston,              (mixed
 Old Charge)             minutes)                                               AASR-SJ                  order)
 └───────── operative masonry ──────────┘└──────────────────────── speculative Freemasonry ─────────────────────────┘
```

- **Operative roots.** The *Regius Poem* (traditionally dated c. 1390; some modern scholars prefer c. 1425–1450; 794 lines of verse) is the oldest known of the *Old Charges*, the traditional histories and rules of medieval English stonemasons [Wikipedia: Old Charges]. In Scotland, the *Schaw Statutes* (1598/1599), issued by William Schaw, Master of Works to James VI, regulated lodges and required them to keep records, which is why Scottish lodges hold some of the oldest minutes in the world [Stevenson 1988].
- **The speculative turn.** On June 24, 1717, by the traditional account, four London lodges formed a Grand Lodge and elected Anthony Sayer Grand Master; the account depends heavily on James Anderson's 1738 narrative, and modern scholars treat its details with caution [Wikipedia: Premier Grand Lodge of England]. Anderson's *Constitutions* (1723) contained a legendary history, six *Charges* and the General Regulations, and remain the founding legal text of the Craft [Museum of Freemasonry].
- **Schism and union.** A rival "Antients" Grand Lodge (1751) contested the "Moderns" until the 1813 union formed the United Grand Lodge of England.
- **The higher degrees.** The Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite organized a system of 33 degrees; its first Supreme Council was founded in Charleston in 1801 (Southern Jurisdiction). Degrees 4–32 are conferred by subordinate bodies traditionally grouped as Lodge of Perfection (4–14), Chapter of Rose Croix (15–18), Council of Kadosh (19–30) and Consistory (31–32); the 33rd is honorary, conferred by the Supreme Council [Wikipedia: Supreme Council, Scottish Rite (SJ)]. Albert Pike's *Morals and Dogma* (1871) is the Rite's best-known commentary. **Important:** in the Masonic tradition the 3rd degree (Master Mason) is the highest degree of the Craft; the "higher" degrees are degrees of further study, not of command.
- **Opening the door.** Prince Hall and fourteen other free Black men were initiated in 1775 in a military lodge attached to a British regiment. When the regiment left in 1776 they received only a limited permit to meet as African Lodge No. 1; in 1784 the Grand Lodge of England warranted them as African Lodge No. 459, with Hall as Master [Wikipedia: African Lodge No. 459]. *Le Droit Humain* admitted women and men on equal terms from 1893 [Le Droit Humain].
- **Governance tradition.** The *Landmarks*, the supposedly unchangeable principles, were never agreed universally. Albert Mackey's list of 25 (1858) was adopted in full by few Grand Lodges [Wikipedia: Masonic Landmarks]. Secrecy is among Mackey's landmarks (No. 23) and the secret ballot is near-universal practice; unanimity for admission is near-universal custom in the United States, while French lodges historically rejected a candidate on one-fifth of the votes [Haywood 1923, ch. III]; England allowed up to three black balls from the eighteenth century [Mackey 1856].

**What OpenMasonry takes:** degrees as curriculum; sponsorship; the ballot; written constitutions; the lodge as a school; the builder's allegory; the idea of Landmarks. **What it leaves:** secrecy of governance, restriction by sex or creed, life offices, and every ritual text of every existing Masonic body. Any ceremony OpenMasonry adopts will be original and public (§11.3).

## 3. The Seven Eternal Landmarks [N]

The Landmarks are the part of the Constitution that **cannot be amended by any ballot**. The verifier rejects any amendment whose target begins with `canon/eternal.toml`, compared without regard to case (see `ledger.py`, `ETERNAL_PREFIX`), and reports a tree whose `canon/eternal.toml` differs by a single byte from the genesis pin as unratified. Changing them requires a **fork**: a new genesis, a new name, and the public's judgment of legitimacy.

| # | Landmark | Statement |
|:-:|---|---|
| **I** | **The Open Door** | No one is refused or ranked for sex, gender, origin, color, belief or unbelief, class or disability. The only conditions of admission the fraternity sets are adulthood, sponsorship, the signed Declaration, and the ballot. |
| **II** | **Light, not Secrecy** | All rules, records, ballots after they close, decisions and finances are public, and so is the teaching of every degree. The only secrets permitted are personal privacy, private keys, a ballot while it is open, and a flaw not yet repaired. Rooms of study may be closed but never secret: their existence, rules and roll are public, their door opens by rule and never by favor, nothing said in them has force, and no one is bound to silence about them except to protect a person's privacy. There are no hidden ranks, no hidden members and no hidden bodies. |
| **III** | **Nothing for Sale** | There is no token, coin, share or fee that buys membership, degree, vote, office or access. Status is never bought. After the founding it is earned by work and conferred by peers or by lot. |
| **IV** | **One Member, One Vote** | Every active member eligible for a ballot has exactly one vote. Degrees grant duties and eligibility, never weight. |
| **V** | **No Permanent Power** | Every office has a term. The Supreme Council is filled by verifiable lot and no one sits twice. A member who stops taking part stops being counted, and keeps the vote. |
| **VI** | **Verifiable Law** | Every rule that binds members is published as text, and as code wherever the ledger enforces it; anyone can replay the full history from genesis and obtain the same state. No record is ever rewritten or erased: a wrong is repaired only by a new public record, and a repair made by an office may undo, freeze or pause but never grant. |
| **VII** | **Exit and Fork** | Any member may leave at any time and keep their personal records; anyone may fork the code and the ledger. Legitimacy is not owned. It is earned in public. |

**Closed is not secret.** Landmark II separates two things that fraternities have usually kept together. A *secret* hides that something exists, what its rules are or who belongs to it; the fraternity keeps none beyond the four the Landmark names. A *closed room* is a place of study that not everyone reads but everyone can see: its existence, its rules and its roll are public, its door opens by rule, and nothing said in it has force (§10.6).

**What an office is.** An *office*, in the sense of Landmark V, is a seat a person holds by election, lot or naming: the Lodge offices (§10.2), the Stewards of the rooms, the seats of the Supreme Council (§7.5) and the Custodian (§9.2). Each has a term (Constitution Art. II.7). The duty a degree unlocks is not an office, and neither is a seat drawn by lot for one matter (an examiner, a juror), which ends with that matter. A witness is a key, not an office: the genesis or a witness ballot names it, and a witness ballot may remove it at any time. The last sentence of Landmark V is dormancy (§6.2): a member who signs nothing for a year is no longer counted in quorums, examiner pools or the counts of Masters, and still holds the vote.

### 3.1 The Declaration (signed at petition)

Each candidate, once invited by a Master Builder (§5.2), signs with their own key a petition that commits to the SHA-256 of this Declaration (the UTF-8 bytes of `[declaration].text` in `canon/eternal.toml`; `openmasonry declaration` prints it). The genesis entry records that digest (`declaration_sha256`), and the verifier refuses a petition that commits to anything else. Replay compares a petition with the digest in the ledger, never with a file of the verifier's tree, and the tree check (`verify --tree`) confirms that the recorded digest is that of the Declaration in the pinned Landmarks:

> *I come freely. I will seek light and share it. I will judge acts, not persons, and myself first. I will keep the secrets of others' privacy and no secret of power. I accept the Landmarks, and I may leave at any time.*

### 3.2 Mapping to Ostrom's principles for durable commons

Elinor Ostrom identified eight design principles shared by long-lived self-governed commons [Ostrom 1990]. OpenMasonry is designed against them explicitly:

| Ostrom principle | OpenMasonry mechanism |
|---|---|
| 1. Clear boundaries | Membership is a public, signed set in the ledger |
| 2. Rules fit local conditions | Lodges adopt bylaws within the Constitution (§10) |
| 3. Those affected can change the rules | Every Master Builder votes on amendments (§6.4) |
| 4. Accountable monitoring | Witnesses, auditors (31°) and the public all replay the ledger |
| 5. Graduated sanctions | Admonish → suspend → expel, each by ballot, with reinstatement (§10.4) |
| 6. Cheap conflict resolution | Arbiters (7°) and juries drawn by lot |
| 7. Recognized right to organize | MIT license and Landmark VII: no outside party can revoke the right to fork |
| 8. Nested enterprises | Lodge → House → Supreme Council; lodges federate (19°) |

## 4. Architecture: a tokenless chain on Git

### 4.1 Layers

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  SOCIAL LAYER — GitHub                                                       │
 │  Issues (applications, work), Pull Requests (ledger entries, amendments),    │
 │  Discussions (the Lodge), Projects (study circles), wiki/ (catalogs)         │
 └──────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────▲───────────────┘
                │ a PR adds one signed JSON entry               │ job summary (state)
 ┌──────────────▼───────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────┐
 │  PROTOCOL LAYER — the Tracing Board (ledger/entries/NNNN/NNNNNN.json)        │
 │  hash chain · SSH signatures · deterministic replay (src/openmasonry)        │
 │  The Tyler (GitHub Actions) judges every PR with the base branch's verifier. │
 └──────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────▲───────────────┘
                │ checkpoints (at most hourly)                  │ re-verify
 ┌──────────────▼───────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────┐
 │  WITNESS LAYER — any VPS running the witness Docker container                │
 │  pull · replay · close ballots · draw lots · sign `witness` checkpoints      │
 │  n ≥ 3f + 1 witnesses would tolerate f faulty ones (target: 4 → 7 → 13)      │
 └──────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                │ Merkle root named by a checkpoint
 ┌──────────────▼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  ANCHOR LAYER — permanence                                                   │
 │  OpenTimestamps → Bitcoin block headers · Software Heritage archive ·        │
 │  mirrors: Codeberg, GitLab, Radicle, IPFS, members' clones                   │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The rooms of study (§10.6) stand outside these layers. They are closed places of conversation hosted beside the repository; they are not part of the ledger and carry no authority. Access to them is derived from the ledger, and nothing in the ledger is derived from them.

### 4.2 Why Git is the chain

A Git repository is already a Merkle DAG: every commit hashes its tree and its parents. OpenMasonry adds a **second, protocol-level chain** inside the repository (`ledger/entries/`) so that validity does not depend on Git's history, hosting provider, or SHA-1 legacy:

- each entry `eᵢ` carries `prev = H(eᵢ₋₁)` with `H = SHA-256` over canonical JSON;
- each entry is signed by its actor in the **OpenSSH signature format** (SSHSIG, namespace `openmasonry`), the format Git uses for SSH commit signing; whether a signature is valid is decided by the verifier's own code, never by an installed OpenSSH (§5.1);
- an entry is valid **only if the state produced by all previous entries permits it**.

Validity is therefore a pure function:

```
 valid(e₀ … eₙ)  ⇔  ∀i: sig_ok(eᵢ, key(Sᵢ₋₁, actorᵢ)) ∧ prevᵢ = H(eᵢ₋₁) ∧ rules(Sᵢ₋₁, eᵢ)
 Sᵢ = apply(Sᵢ₋₁, eᵢ),   S₋₁ = ∅
```

Any person can compute `Sₙ` with `python -m openmasonry verify`. There is no privileged server whose word must be trusted.

### 4.3 Why there is no token

Token-based DAOs fail the Landmarks by construction: governance weight follows capital (Landmark III/IV violated), speculation crowds out purpose, and financialized tokens bring securities-law exposure. Proof-of-work and proof-of-stake exist to settle *who may append* among anonymous strangers. OpenMasonry does not need that: **the right to append is defined by membership**, membership is defined by the ledger, and ordering is settled by Git's fast-forward rule plus witness checkpoints. Bitcoin is used only as a **public clock and notary** (OpenTimestamps), which costs members nothing: the calendar servers batch many hashes into one transaction, and a calendar can at worst withhold service, never forge a timestamp [OpenTimestamps].

### 4.4 Ordering and forks

Two valid but conflicting entries with the same `seq` can only be created by two different PRs; GitHub merges them in some order, and the second one fails `prev` and is rejected by the Tyler. If an attacker with repository admin rights force-pushes a different history, every witness and every clone sees that `H(eₖ)` changed for some already-checkpointed `k`, and the anchored Merkle roots prove which history existed first. **The canonical history is the valid chain at the location the ratified `network.toml` names (§8.3); after the loss of that location, the longest valid chain that extends the last checkpointed and anchored head.**

## 5. Identity, petition and sponsorship [N]

### 5.1 Keys

A member *is* a GitHub login bound to an SSH public key, and the ledger also records the numeric id of the GitHub account behind the login (`github_id`: stated by the genesis for a founder and by the invitation for everyone else, §5.2). GitHub already publishes every user's keys at `https://github.com/<login>.keys`; the petition records the key the candidate chose, so a later GitHub compromise cannot silently change it. Logins follow GitHub's rules (1 to 39 letters, digits or single inner hyphens) and are unique without regard to case, so `Alice` and `alice` can never be two members, or a member and a witness. An entry must spell a login exactly as the ledger records it: a member admitted as `Alice` signs as `Alice`. Keys are of two types, both bare strings with no options or comment (`awk '{print $1" "$2}' ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub` prints one): `ssh-ed25519 <base64>`, and `sk-ssh-ed25519@openssh.com <base64>`, the same kind of key held by a hardware token that signs only when it is touched (`ssh-keygen -t ed25519-sk`). The base64 must be the one encoding of the exact OpenSSH key blob (for a hardware-backed key, with the application `ssh:` and no other), and the 32 key bytes must be the canonical encoding of a curve point of prime order (`crypto.valid_public_key`); every key OpenSSH generates with its defaults is one. Members, witnesses and founders may hold either type, and so may the two keys of a Custodian (§9.2); a hardware-backed key is recommended for founders, witnesses and Master Builders: a key that cannot be copied off its token cannot be stolen by copying a file. Two keys with the same 32 bytes are the same key, whatever their types.

**Replacing a key.** A member replaces their own key with a `rekey {key, proof}` entry. It is signed by the key the ledger holds, the one being replaced; `key` is the new key, and `proof` is a signature **by the new key** over a fixed message that names the login, the old key and the new key, so nobody can register a key they do not hold. The new key must be held by nobody and must never have appeared in the ledger before, under either type: a key once replaced never returns. A member may rekey once in 30 days; a suspended member may, and so may a member whose key is frozen and any member while the ledger is halted (§9.2); only a record that is no longer a membership (departed, expelled or voided) may not. The entry is not activity. From the next entry on only the new key signs for the login. Votes already sealed are untouched: a commitment binds the login, not the key, and is revealed with the new key (§6.1). Online, the judge requires the new key to be listed at `https://github.com/<login>.keys`. A rekey needs the old key.

**Recovering a lost or stolen key.** A member who no longer holds their key, or who shares it with a thief, recovers the membership through their peers. No office decides it, no correction replaces a key, and **a recovery never needs a Custodian**. The runbook is [RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md); the commands are `om recover`, `om recover-cosign`, `om recover-oppose`, `om recover-object` and `om recover-apply`.

1. **The request.** The member signs `rekey_recover {key, proof}` with the **new** key, which nobody holds and which never appeared in the ledger, with the same proof as a rekey. Replay accepts the request from any such key; that the asker is the member is the judge's check: the pull request must come from the GitHub account the ledger records for the member, and that account must list the new key. The request changes no key. It is of one of two kinds. A recovery **after a freeze** is asked while a Custodian's `freeze_key` of the login is in force (§9.2). An **open** recovery is asked when no freeze is in force: no Custodian key is live, or no Custodian froze the key.
2. **Master Builders co-sign** (`rekey_cosign`), answering that the person asking is the member: two after a freeze, three for an open recovery (not all of them the member's sponsors while an active Master Builder who is not a sponsor of the member exists). **No requirement exceeds the people who exist**: with fewer other active Master Builders than that number, all of them are needed and that is enough, and the only Master Builder needs none. **Nor does silence hold a request**: one that has been open 30 days with fewer co-signatures than it needs (those given by then, counted as one at least where another Master Builder exists; without a freeze, only if its notice had not begun) then needs only those already given, and never fewer than one where another Master Builder exists, and they may then all be sponsors of the member. **Nor does an inert Master**: these numbers count only the other Master Builders who revealed a vote or reported in an examination within the last 365 days, or reached the 3rd degree within them. One who signs a work a year and answers nothing keeps the power to oppose, and is not waited for; where no other Master answered, the recovery rests on the identity gate, the 45 days of notice, the old key's objection and the opposition of any Master Builder, as it does for the only Master.
3. **Notice, then application.** The notice is **21 days** after a freeze, counted from the request, and **30 days** for an open recovery, counted from the entry that completes its co-signatures; whenever fewer co-signers than the number above were enough, because fewer Master Builders exist or because the request stood 30 days, it is **45 days**. For a request that stood 30 days, those 45 days start on that thirtieth day after a freeze, and the old key stays frozen meanwhile; without a freeze, they start on that thirtieth day if the co-signatures then given are enough, and otherwise at the co-signature that makes them enough. While the notice runs the old key is frozen, also after a freeze has run its 30 days. Between the end of the notice and **60 days** after its start, the new key signs `rekey_recover_apply`; the co-signers are counted again among the active Master Builders of that moment, and the judge's checks are made again. From the next entry on the new key is the member's key, the old one never returns, any freeze ends, and a departure is reversed: a recovery reverses a departure when it is asked before the departure or within 30 days of it, or when it follows, within 150 days of that recovery's request or of the old key's stop of it, a recovery in time for that departure, whatever ended that recovery, so one follow-up may follow another, since the ledger cannot tell the member from whoever else holds the key. The person the peers answered for holds the membership again, a Council seat the departure ended comes back only into a vacancy, and they may leave at once with the new key (Constitution Art. III.4).

Whoever holds the old key can **stop** a recovery once with `rekey_object` (`om recover-object`), at any time while it is open, also from a frozen key and during a halt, and can never complete one. If the holder is the member (the key was found), they simply rekey. If it is a thief, the member asks again no sooner than 90 days after the stop, and that request is **final**: the old key cannot stop it, and it needs three co-signers, or as many other Master Builders as exist. The Master Builders stop a recovery they doubt by objecting to its freeze (§9.2) or, for an open recovery, by opposing it (`rekey_oppose`, `om recover-oppose`): one fifth of the other active Master Builders, at least one, void it. A login may ask once in 90 days, and not before 90 days after the old key stopped a recovery of it. The protocol defines no revocation of a key by ballot.

**What a valid signature is.** Members sign with OpenSSH (`ssh-keygen -Y sign -n openmasonry`), but whether a signature is valid is decided by the verifier's own code (`crypto.signature_valid`), never by an installed OpenSSH, so every machine reaches the same verdict. The `sig` field must be, byte for byte, the text OpenSSH writes: the `BEGIN` and `END` lines around base64 in lines of 70 characters, LF line ends and one final LF, holding an SSHSIG blob of version 1 with the signer's key, namespace `openmasonry`, an empty reserved field, hash `sha512` and nothing after the signature. The Ed25519 signature inside is checked in the strict form of RFC 8032, without the cofactor: `S` must be below the group order, `R` must be canonically encoded, and `[S]B = R + [k]A` must hold. Any other spelling of the same signature is a bad signature, so nobody can change an entry's hash without its signer's key; the exact rules are in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md#13-signatures).

**Hardware-backed keys.** A signature by an `sk-ssh-ed25519@openssh.com` key follows OpenSSH's format for security keys (`PROTOCOL.u2f`): the blob carries that key and, after the Ed25519 signature, the token's flags (one byte) and signature counter (four bytes). The signature covers the hash of the application (`ssh:`), the flags, the counter and the hash of the same signed data a plain key signs, so none of them can be altered. The verifier requires the user-presence flag: a signature made without a touch is refused, although an installed OpenSSH would accept it when asked to verify. The protocol makes **no claim about attestation**: it checks what the key signed, not that the key lives in a token, and a member who registers a software key under this type deceives only themselves. The reading of the format is tested against OpenSSH itself, with keys and signatures built in software.

**One name, one key, one account, one role.** A name (without regard to case) has one holder at a time among six: the members of any status, the witnesses, the Custodians (named at genesis or by a founder's claim, whether or not their key still signs), the live invitations, the petitions that are live or under an open admission ballot, and the witnesses proposed by an open ballot; a founder's claim that is still alive holds its name too. A key has one holder among the same, invitations aside (an invitation names no key), and among the recoveries that are alive, which hold their new key; a Custodian holds two keys, the custodian key and the revoke key, for good. Keys are compared by their 32 bytes, so the same key under the other type is the same key. A GitHub account (`github_id`) has one holder among the members of any status, the live invitations and the petitions that are live or under an open ballot: **one GitHub account is one member**, and the same account under another login is refused, at an invitation and among the founders of the genesis (`State.id_holders`). An invitation, a petition, a rekey, a genesis founder or witness, and a ballot that adds a witness are each refused when their name, key or account is already held (`State.name_holders`, `State.key_holders`), and the check is made again when an admission or witness ballot closes: a collision found then makes that ballot fail. A member, a witness and a Custodian can therefore never share a name or a key. The one exception is a returning login: a member who left, was expelled or whose admission a correction undid, and who has waited the period of §10.4. The login may be invited again, and it is theirs; so is their key. **The person is the GitHub account, not the login**: a login admitted again under the same account carries what follows the person (§10.4), and one admitted under another account is another person and inherits nothing.

**Who may push an entry (online, in the judge).** Offline replay proves that an entry was signed by the key the ledger knows; it cannot prove that a petition's key, or the new key of a rekey, belongs to the login it claims, that the person opening the pull request is the actor, or that a login still names the GitHub account a Master Builder meant to invite. The judge therefore also runs `verify --pr-author <login> --since <base length> --pr-author-id <id>` (`identity.pr_problems`). **A person is a GitHub account, and an account is its numeric id**, so the author of a pull request is held to the `github_id` the ledger records for the actor, never to the login: every new entry a member signs must come from a pull request whose author has that numeric id. Three kinds of entry anyone may carry: entries a registered witness signs with its witness key (`witness`, `ballot_close`, `exam_draw`, `council_draw`), entries a custodian signs with its custodian key or its revoke key (Appendix A), and a member's `depart` and `rekey_object`, so that a member whose GitHub account is in other hands can still leave and still stop a recovery of their key. The genesis is accepted only from the founder's account: the numeric id set as `founder_github_id` in `src/openmasonry/network.toml`, a file of the pinned core; while it is 0 every genesis is refused. A petition's key must be listed at `https://github.com/<login>.keys` at the time of judging, and so must the new key of a `rekey` and of a recovery (at its request and again at its application). The login of a new `invite` must be, at that time, the GitHub account with the numeric id the entry states (`https://api.github.com/users/<login>`), and the same holds for each founder of a genesis. And a petition's pull request must come from the account its invitation names: the numeric id of the PR's author, as GitHub's pull request event gives it, must equal the invitation's `github_id`. A login that was renamed, or taken over by another account after the invitation, therefore cannot petition under it. If GitHub cannot be reached, the check fails closed. These rules bind the gate, not replay: a historical entry is never re-checked against today's GitHub.

### 5.2 Path of admission

No one who is not a member writes to the ledger, except by a petition under a live invitation. Admission takes five steps:

1. **Application (outside the ledger).** A person who wishes to join introduces themselves, in their own words, in a public issue of the repository. The application asks for no personal data, is not a ledger entry and gives no right. Any adult may apply: a person who is 18 years old or has reached the age of majority where they live, whichever is higher. Membership is a public roll under the name OpenMasonry, and the form says so.
2. **Invitation.** A Master Builder who is ready to answer for the person signs `invite {login, github_id}` (`om invite`), naming the person's GitHub login and the numeric id of that account. The inviter must be an active Master Builder (or, while a succession is in force, a successor, §9.2) under no sponsorship ban and with room in the budget of four sponsorships at a time (§5.3), and cannot invite themselves. The account must be held by nobody else: an invitation is refused when its `github_id` is already that of a member of any status under another login, of another live invitation or of another pending petition, so no person enters twice under two logins of one account (a returning member comes back under the login of their record). The invitation holds the login and the account against every other use (§5.1), makes the inviter the candidate's **first sponsor**, and counts in the inviter's budget exactly as a vouch on a live petition does. It lapses by the passing of time alone if no petition follows within 30 days (`INVITE_EXPIRY_DAYS`); no entry removes it. A lapsed invitation holds neither the login nor the account, but it keeps its place in the inviter's budget for 90 days after it lapsed, so a Master cannot hold many doors open by inviting again and again. The 30 days of an invitation and the 180 days of a petition do not count time during which the ledger was halted (§6.1).
3. **Petition.** The invited person signs `petition {key, declaration_sha256}` with their own key. It is valid only under a live invitation for exactly that login, and it turns the invitation into the petition: the inviter is already its sponsor (a `vouch` from the inviter is neither needed nor accepted), and the petition carries the invitation's `github_id`, which the admitted member keeps. A petition lapses if it is not admitted within 180 days.
4. **Second sponsor.** A second Master Builder signs `vouch {candidate}`. Outside the Founding Phase an admission needs two sponsors. Inside it, as many as there are active Masters, at most two; and whenever fewer than seven Master Builders are present (neither dormant nor silent: throughout the Founding Phase, and later while fewer answer), only one once the petition has been live for 30 days (§5.3): the inviter alone suffices while only one Master exists, and a Master who never answers holds no door shut.
5. **Ballot.** A sponsor opens the admission ballot (§6).

    │  issue; not in the ledger)   │                               │                              │

    │                              │  login + GitHub id; 30 days)  │                              │
    │◄─────────────────────────────┤                               │                              │

A candidate who is refused, or whose petition lapses, needs a new invitation to petition again. A candidate whose admission ballot **lapsed** for want of a quorum keeps the petition, and a sponsor opens another ballot on it (§6.1). A member who left or was expelled, or whose admission was undone by a correction (status `voided`, §10.4), needs a new invitation once the waiting period of §10.4 has passed; that period is checked when the invitation is signed and again at the petition. The Landmark is unchanged by this path: the only conditions of admission the fraternity sets are adulthood, sponsorship, the signed Declaration and the ballot (Landmark I), and the invitation is the first act of sponsorship. A sponsor answers for the candidate's identity, adulthood and good faith, never for their opinions (Constitution Art. IV.3).

### 5.3 Sybil resistance

A Sybil attack creates many fake identities to outvote honest members. OpenMasonry closes the door of the ledger to them and bounds what passes through it with four independent costs.

**The closed door.** An outsider cannot write to the ledger. The only entry signed by a key the ledger did not already know is a petition, and a petition is valid only under a live invitation signed by a Master Builder for exactly that login (§5.2). A flood of fake accounts can therefore reach the issue tracker, as applications, but never the ledger: every petition has a Master who answers for it, each invitation takes a place in that Master's budget, and the judge binds the invitation and the petition to one numeric GitHub account (§5.1).

1. **Sponsorship by Masters.** Only 3° members may invite or vouch (and, while a succession is in force because no Master Builder is left, the members of the highest degree present, §9.2), and a 3° member needs at least 90 days and two examinations by *other* members (§7).
2. **Sponsorship budget.** Each Master may hold at most `b = 4` sponsorships at a time, counting the members they sponsored who were admitted in the last 365 days and have not departed, their invitations that are **live** (for 30 days, or until the petition is filed) or lapsed less than 90 days ago, and the **live** petitions they sponsor, by invitation or by vouch (a petition is live until it is admitted, refused or lapses, more than 180 days after it was filed, and while its admission ballot is open). A lapsed petition holds nothing: neither its login nor its key, nor a place in a sponsor's budget; a lapsed invitation holds only that place, for 90 days. A Master therefore cannot invite or vouch for many pending candidates at once, and a refused or lapsed petition frees its sponsors. A cluster of `s` attacker-controlled Masters can therefore admit at most `s·b/2 = 2s` new identities per year with two sponsors per admission, and at most `s·b = 4s` during the Founding Phase, where one sponsor can be enough (below).
3. **The ballot.** Admissions are decided by the 2°+ members (§6.2); a candidate is rejected when black balls reach one fifth of the votes cast (§6.3). If `h` honest electors vote no, a hostile admission passes only when `5h < h + a`, that is **`a > 4h`**: the attacker needs more than four times the honest turnout.
4. **Time.** New identities start at 1° and need ≥ 90 days to become Masters who can sponsor in turn.

**During the Founding Phase** (fewer than 7 active Masters, §9.2) the sponsorship cost is lower: an admission needs only as many sponsors as there are active Masters, at least one and at most two (`max(1, min(2, M))` for `M` active Masters), and one once the petition has been live for 30 days, so a second Master who never answers cannot keep a candidate out; every other Master still votes, and the blackball stands (`ledger.sponsors_required`). With a single Master the inviter is therefore the only sponsor an admission needs. Outside the phase every admission needs two sponsors, the inviter and one more. In every phase, an examination whose ladder cannot seat the `k` examiners of the degree proceeds with every member of its last rung instead (§7.2). The phase is in force whenever fewer than 7 active Masters exist, so it can resume if Masters go dormant or leave.

Combining (2) and (4), attacker growth is bounded by the recurrence `s(t+1) ≤ s(t) + 2s(t)·(fraction of a year)`, with no admissions passing a vigilant electorate when `a ≤ 4h`. Graph-based defenses such as SybilLimit accept O(log n) Sybils per *attack edge* (an honest member fooled into vouching) and prove that any defense based on the mixing of the social graph must accept at least Ω(1) per attack edge [Yu et al. 2008]; the budget `b` makes the per-edge cost explicit and small. When a member is expelled by a sanction ballot with the measure `expel_for_fraud` within 730 days of their admission (730 days to the second, inclusive), each of their sponsors loses the right to invite and to vouch for 730 days; the verifier enforces the ban when a sponsor invites or vouches and again when an admission ballot opens (`ledger.may_sponsor`; Constitution Art. IV.4). The ban follows the person: leaving and being admitted again does not lift it.

**Repair.** An admission obtained by identity fraud, by a stolen key or through a defect can also be taken back, for 90 days after its ballot closed, by a noticed correction (`undo_ballot`, §9.2). That removes the false member; it judges no one. A ban on the sponsors and a record of fraud come only from a sanction ballot (`expel_for_fraud`). While such a ballot on the member is open the undo is not applied, so the members decide first; and for 90 days after an undo the `voided` record may still be expelled for fraud, by a ballot with that measure alone.

### 5.4 Privacy

The ledger stores only logins, numeric GitHub account ids, public keys, hashes and protocol decisions. An application lives in the issue tracker, never in the ledger, and asks for no personal data. Personal reflections and works live in the member's own repositories or as hashes, so a member who leaves can delete their content while the ledger keeps only the hash (§12.3).

## 6. The ballot [N]

### 6.1 Commit–reveal

Every ballot has a commit window of 7 days and a reveal window of 3 days. Only the opener of an amendment states other windows, of 2 to 30 days to commit (no fewer than 7 while any of its electors is dormant) and 1 to 14 to reveal: a text may need a longer reading, and nobody else is kept waiting by it. The reveal window does not start when the commit window ends: it opens `REVEAL_GAP = CLOCK_TOLERANCE + 15 minutes` later (a gap of 6 hours and 15 minutes), so that no commitment can still be merged with a backdated timestamp (§8.3) after any vote has been revealed. The reveal window is closed at both ends, its first second included.

**The close.** A ballot is closed (`ballot_close`, by a witness or any active member) once its reveal window has ended, or **as soon as every elector has revealed**, since nothing can change its votes any more. A ballot whose revealed votes do not reach the quorum (§6.3) **lapses**: it decides nothing and penalizes nobody (a petition stays live, an exam is passed and free again). A lapsed ballot may be **re-run** once, within 30 days, with at least 7 days to commit, by anyone who may open that kind: the same kind and subject, with a quorum that no longer waits for the electors who did not vote in the lapsed one, who keep the vote all the same. A re-run is never re-run, and neither is a ballot that gave less than 7 days to commit: if it lapses, it is opened again as a new ballot, which waits for every elector.

```
 open ──── commit (cd days) ────┤ gap of 6¼ hours ├──── reveal (rd days) ────┤ close
```

**The ballot's own clock.** The windows are counted on the ballot's own clock. If a correction stays the ballot, halts the ledger, or would void the ballot and is still pending (§9.2), that time is taken out of the ballot's clock, so every window is extended by exactly the time that was paused. No stored deadline is ever rewritten: the pause is computed from the entries, and it ends by itself. Two kinds of ballot run on wall time, and nothing pauses them: an amendment and the ballot that removes a Custodian (§6.2).

```
 commitment = SHA-256( ballot_id ‖ "|" ‖ login ‖ "|" ‖ vote ‖ "|" ‖ salt )
```

During the commit window only commitments are visible, so no one votes under the influence of a running tally. Each elector commits once. During the reveal window each elector publishes `(vote, salt)`, once, with `vote ∈ {yes, no}` and a salt of 32 to 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters (`om vote` draws 16 random bytes); the verifier checks it against the commitment (`openmasonry commit BALLOT LOGIN VOTE SALT` prints it). Unrevealed commitments count as abstentions.

**Known limitation:** after the reveal, individual votes are public. This honors Landmark II but weakens the traditional secret ballot that protects dissenters from retaliation. v0.3 is planned to replace it with **encrypted ballots** in the style of MACI: votes are encrypted to a threshold key held by Keepers of Discretion (4°) and only the tally plus a proof of correct tallying are published (§13). Until then, retaliation for a vote is a sanctionable offense (Constitution Art. VI).

### 6.2 Electorates

| Ballot kind | Electors | Fallback (decided on the members present) | Opened by |
|---|---|---|---|
| admission | members of 2°+ | all members if nobody of 2°+ is present (a silent member is not present here, §9.2), every member of 2°+ keeping the vote | a sponsor of the candidate |
| advancement to degree *d* | members of degree ≥ *d*, excluding the candidate | the Masters' rung (all Master Builders except the candidate), if fewer than 3 are present on the first rung (a silent member is not present here, §9.2); the successors, if no Master Builder is present (§9.2); in either case every member who had the vote on the first rung keeps it | the candidate, or an examiner of the passed exam |
| amendment, witness | all Master Builders (3°+) | none | a Master Builder |
| sanction | Arbiters (7°+), excluding the accused | all Master Builders except the accused, if fewer than 3 are present | an Arbiter where three or more active Arbiters other than the accused exist, else a Master Builder; never the accused |
| custodian (the removal of a Custodian) | all Master Builders (3°+), excluding the Custodian's own member | none | a Master Builder other than the Custodian's own member |

The electorate is fixed when the ballot opens, and nothing is added to it or removed from it afterwards. **Dormant members keep the vote**: every eligible member is an elector, and a dormant one may commit and reveal like any other. What dormancy changes is the **quorum base**: the quorum and the turnout count only the electors who were present (not dormant) at the opening (§6.3). A fallback only widens an electorate: **no fallback ever removes a vote** (Landmark V), so the electors of the first rung keep theirs when a ballot falls to a wider one. A witness opens no ballot; a dormant member may open one (`ballot_open`, `om open`), under these rules:

- **Exact subjects.** Each kind has exactly these subject fields: admission `{candidate}`, advancement `{member, degree}`, amendment `{path, new_sha256}`, sanction `{member, measure}`, witness `{action, name, key, proof}` (`key` and `proof` only to add), custodian `{remove}`. Ballot ids are unique strings of 1 to 64 characters, never used twice.
- **One open ballot per subject.** While a ballot on a subject is open, no other ballot of the same kind on the same subject may open (compared case-insensitively). The subject of a sanction is the member **and** the measure, so a ballot on a light measure never holds the place of a grave one; the subject of an amendment is the path **and** the text proposed, so nobody holds a path by opening a bogus text on it.
- **Limits.** A member has at most three ballots of their own open at once, and apart from them at most three amendment ballots; the ballot that removes a Custodian is never counted. The opener of a sanction or witness ballot that failed or lapsed opens one on the same subject again only 90 days after its close, unless it is the re-run (§6.1); another member may open one at once.
- **Admission.** Only one of the candidate's sponsors may open the admission ballot, and only for a live petition with enough sponsors (§5.2, §5.3). The sponsors are validated again at that moment: only those who are still active Master Builders (or successors) under no ban count, and only they become the sponsors of record. The ballot **freezes the petition**: the key, the GitHub account id and the sponsors the electors vote on are copied into the ballot, the new member is created from that copy, and no further sponsor can be added.
- **Advancement.** Only for the next degree (up to 32°), after the waiting period, and only with a passed examination; each passed examination supports a single advancement ballot.
- **Sanction.** The measure is one of `admonish`, `suspend`, `expel`, `expel_for_fraud`, `reinstate` (§10.4); the member must exist, must not have departed, and must be in a standing the measure applies to (§10.4). **No sanction on a Master Builder (any measure but `reinstate`) is decided by a single elector**: such a ballot needs at least two electors to open and two votes cast to be decided, and otherwise lapses.
- **Witness.** A `witness` ballot adds a witness (with a valid name and a key of either type that nobody else holds, §5.1) or removes an existing one. While the ballot is open it holds that name and that key. It is decided by the amendment rule (§6.3).
- **Custodian.** A `custodian` ballot takes one decision: to **remove** the key of a Custodian (`om open custodian NAME`). Any Master Builder may open it except the Custodian's own member, who neither opens it nor votes in it. Its windows are fixed at seven days to commit and three to reveal, on wall time. No correction can stay, void or undo it; it goes on while the ledger is halted; and a member whose key is frozen may open it, vote in it and close it. If it passes, the key signs nothing from the next entry on (§9.2). No ballot appoints a Custodian or gives one a new key.
- **Amendment.** The amendment ballot shares the protections of the removal ballot: it runs on wall time, no correction stays, voids or undoes it, it goes on while the ledger is halted, and a member whose key is frozen may open it, vote in it and close it. The entries of these two kinds belong to the **unstoppable set** (§8.3), which no halt, cap or waiting period stops.

A member is **dormant** once more than 365 days have passed since their last signed activity. Dormant members keep their degree and their vote, but they are not counted in the quorum base, which prevents **quorum death**, the slow paralysis of associations whose rolls fill with inactive names, without giving the first dormant member who wakes the power of an electorate of one.

### 6.3 The decision function D

Let `N` be the ballot's **quorum base**, the number of its electors who were present (not dormant) when it opened (1 when none was, less the non-responders of a re-run, §6.1), `Y` and `B` the revealed yes and no votes, `T = Y + B`. Every revealed vote counts, a dormant elector's included. A ballot with `T < Q(N)` **lapses** (§6.1) and the rule of its kind is not consulted; so does a sanction on a Master Builder (any measure but `reinstate`) with fewer than two votes cast.

```
 Q(N) = min( N, max(3, ⌈N/3⌉) )                       quorum of votes cast

 D_admission(Y,B,N)  = T ≥ Q(N)  ∧  5B < T               rejected by one fifth of black balls
 D_advancement       = D_admission
 D_amendment(Y,B,N)  = T ≥ Q(N)  ∧  3Y ≥ 2T  ∧  3T ≥ N   two-thirds, one-third turnout
 D_sanction          = D_amendment
 D_witness           = D_amendment
 D_custodian(Y,B,N)  = T ≥ Q(N)  ∧  Y > B                simple majority of the votes cast; a tie fails
```

**Why one fifth, not unanimity.** The unanimous ballot preserves harmony in small lodges but lets a single member veto anyone forever, a known source of abuse. Under `5B < T`, a lone black ball still blocks admission whenever five or fewer votes are cast (small lodges keep their intimacy), while in larger bodies a single member's grudge cannot override the consensus of the rest. This follows historical French practice [Haywood 1923, ch. III].

Properties, each tested exhaustively for all `N < 60` in `tests/test_protocol.py`:

- **Monotonicity:** adding a yes vote never turns a pass into a fail.
- **Quorum safety:** no decision passes with fewer than `Q(N)` votes.
- **Small-body veto:** if `T ≤ 5`, one black ball rejects.
- **Bounded veto:** for `T > 5`, rejection requires at least `⌈T/5⌉` black balls.

**Why a simple majority removes a Custodian.** Removing a power is deliberately easier than changing a rule. The removal of a Custodian grants nothing and can harm no member; the worst a mistaken removal does is leave the fraternity without corrections (§9.2). It therefore needs only the quorum and more yes than no, among Masters from whom the Custodian's own member is excluded.

The function is defined for the six kinds above and for nothing else: the verifier treats any other kind as an error, never as a default rule (`ledger.decide`). A quorate ballot is decided: it **passes** when the rule holds and its effect can still be applied lawfully, and otherwise **fails**, with the consequences of its kind; a ballot that misses the quorum **lapses**, and an absent elector therefore never costs a candidate a refusal and a wait. With three present electors or fewer the quorum is all of them.

### 6.4 Amendments

Ordinary rules change by an amendment ballot that pins the SHA-256 of the new text. Genesis pins six **governed paths**, exactly these:

| Path | What is pinned |
|---|---|
| `canon/degrees.toml` | SHA-256 of the degree canon |
| `canon/eternal.toml` | SHA-256 of the eternal Landmarks and the Declaration (never re-pinned) |
| `CONSTITUTION.md` | SHA-256 of the Constitution |
| `docs/governance/CUSTODIAN.md` | SHA-256 of the Custodian's charter |
| `docs/governance/ROOMS.md` | SHA-256 of the charter of the rooms of study (§10.6) |
| `@protocol` | the digest of the **pinned core**: the files that decide what is valid, listed one by one in the manifest `src/openmasonry/protocol.manifest` (`openmasonry digest`). The manifest is a file of the tree, one path per line in sorted order, and it lists itself. Today it lists the package marker `src/openmasonry/__init__.py`; the modules `ledger.py`, `crypto.py`, `canon.py`, `lot.py`, `govern.py`, `identity.py`, `beacon.py`, `rooms.py` (the rule that derives the readers of each room, §10.6) and `verify.py`; `src/openmasonry/network.toml`, which names the canonical repository and the founder's GitHub account id; the manifest; and the three workflows of the repository, `.github/workflows/judge.yml` (the judge), `.github/workflows/pages.yml` (the Observatory) and `.github/workflows/tyler.yml` (the audit of `main` and the tests). The digest is one SHA-256 over the listed files in the manifest's order; each file contributes the length of its path, the path, the length of its content and the content (both lengths as 8 bytes, big-endian). A listed file that is missing, or is not a regular file, is an error, and so is a manifest that is not in its one strict form. Git is not consulted and no directory is listed, so a checkout and an unpacked archive of the same commit give the same digest |

An amendment may target any path inside the repository or `@protocol`, never one starting with `canon/eternal.toml` and never one under `ledger/` (the entries are protected by the chain, not by a pin); a passed amendment on a new path makes it governed, and an amendment that pins sixty-four zeros on a path a passed amendment made governed **removes** that pin (the six genesis pins are never removed). Only Master Builders open an amendment. Two amendments of one path with different texts may be open together; each records the pin it replaces (its **base**) when it opens, and one whose base is no longer the pin at its close fails, so the first text to pass wins and the other cannot overwrite it. A passed amendment of `@protocol` advances the **protocol epoch** (`S.epoch`). A later core that changes a rule makes the change apply from a fixed point that the voted text states, a time or a `seq` still in the future when the ballot opens, and never from the epoch alone, so the entries already merged replay exactly as before; a candidate core is checked against the whole ledger before it can merge (§8.3). Paths are matched exactly, case included: an amendment of a path that differs from a governed one only by case is refused at its opening, and one whose path has become, by its close, the case twin of a governed path fails, so two pins can never name one file. `openmasonry verify --tree` compares every governed path in the working tree against the hash ratified for it, so the Tyler refuses any PR that changes a governed file, or the code that judges the ledger, without a matching ballot.

**A closed set of workflows.** The repository holds three workflows (`judge.yml` and `tyler.yml` of the Tyler, and `pages.yml`, which publishes the Observatory) and all three are pinned. A tree that holds anything under `.github/workflows/` that its manifest does not list is unlawful: `verify --tree` reports it (`govern.stray_workflows`), and the judge's own gate refuses the pull request before the verifier runs. Without this rule a pull request could add a workflow with a job named `judge`, which would answer to the same required check as the real judge. A new workflow is lawful only once an amendment of `@protocol` ratifies a tree whose manifest lists it.

**Upgrading the core through the judge.** The judge runs the base branch's code on the pull request's tree. The manifest is therefore **data read from the tree being judged**, never a list inside the code that judges: the old code and the new code compute the same digest for the same tree. To change the core (a rule, a new module, a new workflow, a file taken off the list), the new tree is prepared, an amendment pins its digest, and the pull request with exactly that tree then passes the old judge, which reads the new manifest; after the merge the new judge computes the same digest. A manifest changed without that amendment, to add a file or to drop one, gives a digest the ledger does not pin, and the tree is unlawful. Before anyone votes, the **candidate core** is run over the current ledger (`verify --candidate-root DIR`, the `candidate` job of `tyler.yml` on every pull request): it must reach the same head and, for the same state document version, the same state digest as the pinned core, so a new rule cannot change the replay of what is already kept. One thing still cannot change this way: the computation of the digest itself (SPEC §12, point 18); its procedure is written in [RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md).

**What is not pinned.** The rest of the code is outside `@protocol`: the command line `om` and the members' tools, the witness node, the launchers and scripts, the other files of `.github/` (issue forms, code owners) and the MCP engine. It changes by an ordinary pull request with a code-owner review. This is safe because none of it can change a verdict. The pinned modules import only one another and the Python standard library (a test fails otherwise), the judge runs nothing of the repository but `python -P -m openmasonry.verify`, the judge refuses any file under `src/` that could be found in place of a pinned module or of a standard one, and no workflow exists outside the core. `om verify` is the same pinned verifier, called from the command line.

**Transition.** Right after an amendment passes, the tree still holds the old text until the PR that applies the new text is merged. During that transition the verifier accepts either the newly pinned hash or the **prior pin** of that path; any other content is refused. The prior pin stays lawful for 30 days of ledger time after the ballot closes (`PRIOR_PIN_DAYS`).

**Canon history.** Replay must not depend on today's file. Every ratified version of the degree canon is archived as `canon/history/<sha256>.toml` (genesis archives the first one), and the verifier reads waiting periods and examiner counts from the version pinned at each point of the history (`canon.degrees_for`). An amendment of `canon/degrees.toml` therefore **cannot be opened until its text is archived**: the `ballot_open` is refused while `canon/history/<new sha256>.toml` is missing, so a PR adding that file (a byte-for-byte copy of the proposed text) merges first, and the new table is in every verifier's tree before anyone votes on it. The archive only grows: the judge refuses a pull request that changes or deletes a file there, and the tree must hold every degree table the ledger ever pinned. The archived text must also be a well-formed degree table: exactly the degrees 1 to 33, each once, each with exactly the fields of the canon, a waiting period of 0 to 3,650 days and 0 to 33 examiners, at least one for every degree reached by examination (`canon.parse_degrees`). A text that is not can never be ratified: the ballot that proposes it is refused at its opening, and a genesis that pins such a text is invalid. A clone that lacks the archived text of the pinned canon reports it (`canon_pending` in `om me --json` and `om state --json`) and asks for `git pull` instead of failing.

## 7. The 33 degrees and the Supreme Council [N]

### 7.1 Structure

```
                                   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                                   │   VI  SUPREME COUNCIL   33°   │  33 seats · drawn by lot · 3-year term · once
                                   └──────────────▲───────────────┘
                                   ┌──────────────┴───────────────┐
                                   │   V   CONSISTORY      31–32°  │  audit, judicial review, balance of powers
                                   └──────────────▲───────────────┘
                     ┌─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
                     │   IV  COUNCIL OF KADOSH                       19–30°        │  philosophy, law, anti-capture
                     └─────────────────────────────▲─────────────────────────────┘
                          ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
                          │   III CHAPTER OF THE ROSE                15–18°   │  rebuilding, liberty, charity
                          └────────────────────────▲────────────────────────┘
               ┌────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┐
               │   II  LODGE OF PERFECTION                                     4–14°        │  duty, stewardship, justice
               └────────────────────────────────────▲────────────────────────────────────┘
          ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │   I   CRAFT LODGE      1° Apprentice · 2° Fellow · 3° Master Builder                 │  the whole of membership
          └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Following the tradition, **the 3° Master Builder is a full member in every sense**: voting on amendments, sponsoring, holding office. Degrees 4–32 are degrees of **further study and service**; each one teaches a virtue and an art and unlocks a specific *duty* (for example: 6° may operate a witness node, 7° sits on dispute juries, 30° joins the security Watch, 31° sits on the Court of Review). A duty is not an office (§3). The full table is in Appendix B and in [`docs/degrees/`](../degrees/README.md), generated from [`canon/degrees.toml`](../../canon/degrees.toml).

### 7.2 The eligibility predicate

For a member `m` holding degree `d < 32`, advancement to `d+1` at time `t` requires all of:

```
 E(m, d+1, t) =  active(m)
              ∧  t − since(m, d) ≥ W(d+1)                        waiting period
              ∧  work(m, d+1) published and referenced            masterwork
              ∧  exam(m, d+1) passed                               majority of k(d+1) drawn examiners
              ∧  D_advancement(ballot(m, d+1)) = true              ballot of peers
```

**What each degree asks** is the public teaching in [`docs/study/`](../study/README.md): one guide per degree, with its virtue, art, lesson, the masterwork as the canon states it, and the criteria an examiner applies (the summary pages generated from the canon are in [`docs/degrees/`](../degrees/README.md)).

**Opening an examination.** Only the candidate may open their own examination (`exam_open`), for the next degree only (up to 32°), after the waiting period and with a reference to the published work. At that moment the verifier **freezes the examiner pool and the number `k`**, before anyone can know the beacon (§7.4), so nobody can shape the pool after seeing the lot.

**The pool** is the first rung of this ladder that can seat `k(d+1)` examiners (active, not dormant): (1) members of degree ≥ `d+1`, excluding the candidate and the candidate's sponsors; (2) any Master Builder, excluding the same; (3) any Master Builder except the candidate; (4) while a succession is in force and no Master Builder is present, the successors except the candidate (§9.2). A Master Builder who is silent (called to at least two examinations or ballots that ended in the last 180 days, two calls counting as two only when they end at least 7 days apart, and answered none of them, §9.2) is not drawn in rungs (2) and (3), and during a succession a silent member is no successor, so rung (4) never holds one. When the candidate is a founder, the same rungs are first tried without the founding cohort (§9.3). The examiners of the candidate's previous exam for that degree who never reported in it are left out of every rung, unless nobody else could be drawn. **After an exam that timed out**, the next lot does not stop at the first rung that seats `k(d+1)`: it seats every member any rung holds (the rungs taken together, the founding cohort included even for a founder), never fewer than `k(d+1)`, so that every examiner who never answers is called and falls silent within about three exams, however many they are. When no rung can seat `k(d+1)`, the exam proceeds with every member that any rung holds, the rungs taken together, and `k` is lowered to their number, in every phase (never to zero): no requirement exceeds the people who exist, and no lot alternates between two members who never answer, each drawn too rarely to fall silent (a silent member drawn beside one who answers costs one exam that times out, and is then left out), so the 32nd degree, which asks for seven examiners, can be reached in a fraternity of seven Master Builders, where the six others examine.

**Verdicts.** Each drawn examiner reports `pass` or `fail` once. The exam **passes** with a strict majority of `pass` among the `k` drawn examiners, and **fails** when `fail` reaches half of them; an exam always has at least one examiner, and one with none could never pass. An exam also counts as failed when the advancement ballot opened on it is lost on the votes. A new exam for the same degree may be opened after a failure only once **90 days have passed since the failed exam was opened**, so a candidate cannot reshuffle the draw. A candidate cannot choose examiners, so collusion requires corrupting a random sample.

**Timeout.** An exam with no majority verdict more than **60 days** after it was opened has **timed out**: reports and the draw are no longer accepted, and it ends **without penalty**. Running out of time is no failure; the candidate may open a new exam at once, the examiners who stayed silent are skipped in its lot, and that lot seats everyone else any rung holds. An exam whose lot was not drawn within **7 days** of its beacon round (for example because the beacon is unavailable) times out in the same way. **Non-responders never block.**

**Lapse.** An exam can also end through no fault of the candidate, and then it **lapses**: it gives no verdict and carries no penalty, and the candidate may open a new one at once. That happens in two cases. A conflicted examiner recuses and nobody is left in the lot's order to take the place (an exam never goes on with fewer than its `k` examiners). Or the advancement ballot is won on the votes but cannot be applied, because at its close the member had departed, been expelled or was suspended; if the member is later reinstated, nothing stands in the way of a new exam. An advancement ballot that lapses for want of a quorum gives the exam back, passed and unused, for another ballot. A recusal is accepted only while the exam is undecided, so it can never undo a verdict. An examiner whose key is frozen by a correction (§9.2), or whose admission was undone, stands aside exactly as if they had recused, and the next member in the lot's order takes the seat. The 60 days of an exam and the 90 days before a new one do not count time during which the ledger was halted. An advancement that a correction undoes leaves its exam lapsed, with no penalty.

### 7.3 Time as the price of rank

Waiting periods are the only "cost" of a degree, and time cannot be bought:

| Milestone | Minimum days from initiation | ≈ years |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3° Master Builder | 90 | 0.25 |
| 14° Perfect Elect | 570 | 1.6 |
| 18° Knight of the Rose | 930 | 2.5 |
| 30° Kadosh, Guardian against Capture | 1,955 | 5.4 |
| 32° Master of the Royal Secret, Equilibrium | 2,685 | 7.4 |
| 33° Sovereign Guardian (earliest possible seat) | 3,415 | 9.4 |

These are lower bounds; examinations and works make real paths longer. The table is computed by `canon.minimum_days_to(n)` from the current canon.

### 7.4 The lot (verifiable sortition)

```
 rank(x) = SHA-256( beacon ‖ "|" ‖ purpose ‖ "|" ‖ x )        draw(pool, k) = the k members with smallest rank
```

The **beacon** must be unpredictable when the draw is requested and public afterwards. The lot therefore happens in two steps, both enforced by the verifier:

1. `exam_open` (or `council_call`) must declare `beacon_round = drand_round(time) + BEACON_DELAY_ROUNDS`, with `BEACON_DELAY_ROUNDS = 9600`: a round of the League of Entropy's quicknet chain (3-second rounds) about **8 hours** in the future, which nobody can know yet. The pool and `k` are frozen in the same entry. The delay exceeds the 6-hour `CLOCK_TOLERANCE` (§8.3), so even an entry backdated by the maximum the Tyler accepts still names a round that had not happened when it was merged;
2. `exam_draw` (or `council_draw`) is accepted only from that round's time on and within 7 days of it (and, for an exam, before the exam times out); a lot not drawn in time lapses with no penalty, and a new exam or call names a new round. The draw carries its 32-byte randomness in lowercase hex; it draws from the frozen pool. Rounds are positive integers computed in integer arithmetic. A member or a witness may post it; witnesses do it automatically (§9.1).

The offline verifier trusts the recorded randomness; `openmasonry verify --online` (run by the Tyler on every pull request and on every audit of `main`) re-fetches every round and rejects a fabricated value. Because the purpose string includes the candidate, degree and round, a beacon cannot be reused to shop for a favorable draw. Uniformity is tested statistically.

### 7.5 The Supreme Council

The 33° is **never applied for**. Its 33 seats are filled by lot among members who:

- hold the 32° for at least the waiting period the degree canon sets for the 33° (730 days today),
- are active and not dormant,
- have **never sat before** (one seat per lifetime in v0.1).

Seats last three years (3 × 365 days). A seat is counted for its whole term whatever happens to the standing of its holder: a suspended member keeps the seat, it is not filled a second time, and so the Council can never exceed 33 (an expelled or departed member loses the seat; a departure that a recovery reverses takes its seat back only into a vacancy, §5.1). A draw happens in two steps (§7.4):

1. **`council_call`.** When seats are vacant, any active member may post a `council_call` naming the beacon round 9,600 rounds ahead; only one call may be pending, and a call not drawn within 7 days of its round has lapsed and may be replaced. The call **freezes the eligible pool** at that moment. Witnesses never post the call: someone must ask for the draw.
2. **`council_draw`.** After the round, a member or a witness posts the beacon; the verifier ranks the whole frozen pool by the lot and seats the first names, in that order, until the vacancies at that time are filled, skipping anyone who is no longer active, has gone dormant, no longer holds the 32° or has sat before. Seats the order cannot fill stay vacant for a later call.

Since draws happen whenever someone calls them for vacant seats, terms naturally stagger. A Past Sovereign Guardian keeps the title and the 33° numeral but no power.

**Powers, deliberately few (Constitution Art. VIII).** These powers are procedural: the verifier records who sits on the Council, but it does not enforce the delay, the convocation or certifications (§8.5).

1. **Suspensive delay:** by two-thirds of seated members, suspend a *passed* decision for up to 30 days and refer it to the Court of Review (31°). The Council can delay, never veto.
2. **Constitutional convocation:** call an extraordinary assembly of all Masters.
3. **Certification:** certify or decertify official mirrors (reversible by any amendment ballot). Witness nodes are added and removed only by a witness ballot (Constitution Art. XI.2).

The Council **cannot** admit, promote, sanction, spend, or amend. Its job is to make itself unnecessary, which is the lesson of the 33°: *"Guard the house so well that it never needs you."*

**How hard is it to capture by lot?** If a fraction `p` of the eligible pool is hostile, the number of hostile councillors in a full draw of 33 follows a hypergeometric distribution. For an eligible pool of 300:

| hostile share of pool `p` | P(hostile two-thirds ≥ 22 of 33, the only majority with power) | P(hostile blocking minority ≥ 12 of 33) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20% | 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ | 1.6% |
| 30% | 4.1 × 10⁻⁶ | 26% |
| 40% | 0.098% | 74% |
| 50% | 3.2% | 97% |

Staggered draws and the Council's narrow powers mean a hostile majority would also have to persist across years and could still only *delay* decisions by 30 days.

### 7.6 Light: a record of service

Each member has a public **Light** score, the decayed sum of their signed activity:

```
 L(m, t) = Σ_{i ∈ activity(m)}  2^( −(t − tᵢ) / H ),      H = 180 days (half-life)
```

Light is **informational only**: it never weighs a vote (Landmark IV), and no rule reads it. It shows who is actually serving and makes "influence without contribution" visible. Dormancy is computed from the same record of activity, by the time of the last act alone (§6.2). The record keeps the points of the 1,800 days before a member's newest one (ten half-lives): an older point weighs less than a thousandth and is dropped. Activity counts at most **one point per member per UTC day**, whatever the number of entries signed that day, and a member may record at most one `work` entry in any 24 hours, so neither Light nor the ledger can be inflated by repetition. A Grand Master who stops working fades on the board like anyone else.

## 8. Integrity, threat model and survivability

### 8.1 What we claim, precisely

No system is "impossible to hack". OpenMasonry makes a narrower, provable claim:

> **Tamper-evidence.** Any modification, deletion, insertion or reordering of a ledger entry followed by later entries is detected by every verifier, unless the attacker can find a SHA-256 second preimage or forge an Ed25519 signature of an honest member (the verifier accepts only Ed25519 keys, plain or hardware-backed). Truncating the *newest* entries after the last checkpoint is detected by comparison with witnesses, mirrors and anchors, and by every clone and witness that had verified them (each remembers the head it verified, §8.3), not by replay alone.

*Sketch.* Changing entry `k` changes `H(eₖ)`; entry `k+1` commits to the old `H(eₖ)` in its signed payload, so the chain breaks at `k+1` unless the attacker re-signs every later entry, which requires every later actor's private key. Finding a different `eₖ'` with the same hash is a second-preimage attack on SHA-256 (cost ≈ 2²⁵⁶). The tests demonstrate edit, deletion, reordering and impersonation detection.

Landmark VI makes the same promise as law: no record is ever rewritten or erased, a wrong is repaired only by a new public record, and a repair made by an office may undo, freeze or pause but never grant. The one power of repair that exists, the Custodian's corrections (§9.2), is built on that sentence: a correction is itself an entry of the chain, signed, hashed and replayed like every other, and what it undoes stays in the record.

The claim also needs the parser and the judge to be unambiguous:

- **Strict loader, strict values, exact shapes.** The entries directory holds shard directories of 1,000 entries each, and each shard regular files named `NNNNNN.json` after their `seq` (`ledger/entries/NNNN/NNNNNN.json`), with no gaps, and nothing else (a `.gitkeep` at the top aside). An entry is JSON with no duplicate key, no `NaN` or `Infinity`, no `null`, no number with a fraction or an exponent, integers within ±(2⁵³ − 1), strings of ASCII only, at most six levels of nesting and at most 8 KiB (32 KiB for the genesis). Every entry has exactly the seven envelope fields and every body exactly the fields of its type (Appendix A); times have a single UTC format, checked field by field. Durations are compared exactly, never rounded to days. No rule depends on a floating-point value, a Unicode table, a time-zone database or the limits of one runtime, and any error while an entry is judged is a refusal of that entry, never a crash. Two machines therefore never read the same bytes as two different histories.
- **The judge is not the defendant.** On pull requests, the Tyler's `judge` job runs from `pull_request_target`: it checks out the **base branch** and runs that verifier against the PR's files, which it fetches and reads as data and never executes. A PR cannot change the code that judges it. That code is the pinned core (`@protocol`, §6.4): the entry point `python -P -m openmasonry.verify`, the modules it imports and the three workflows (`judge.yml`, `pages.yml`, `tyler.yml`); nothing else of the repository runs in the judge. Changing any of it requires a passed amendment, and no workflow the manifest does not list may exist (§6.4).

> **Censorship-resistance by replication.** The ledger survives as long as one honest copy survives.

If the fraternity keeps `m` independent copies (GitHub, Codeberg, GitLab, Radicle, Software Heritage, IPFS, witness nodes, members' clones) that each fail independently with probability `q` per year, the probability of losing all of them in a year is `qᵐ`. With `q = 0.1` and `m = 8`, that is `10⁻⁸`. Anchored Merkle roots let any surviving copy prove it is the original.

### 8.2 Witness safety

Witnesses replay the ledger and sign `witness` entries containing `(upto_seq, head, state_sha256, state_version, root, anchor)`. A checkpoint covers every entry before it: `upto_seq` is the entry just before it, `head` that entry's hash, and `state_sha256` the digest of the whole state that entry left, as the specification defines it (SPEC §4.11: one Merkle root per section of records, so a verifier hashes again only what changed); `root` is the RFC 6962 Merkle root of the hashes of entries `0..upto_seq` and `anchor` the repository-relative path of its OpenTimestamps proof (both left out when there is none). The verifier checks that `head`, `state_sha256` and `root` match its own replay, so two verifiers that accept the same checkpoint hold the same state. With `n` witnesses of which at most `f` are faulty or malicious, a checkpoint would be **final** when signed by at least `2f + 1` of `n ≥ 3f + 1` witnesses, the classical Byzantine fault tolerance bound.

**Known limitation: current status.** v0.1 records every checkpoint but **does not count signatures toward finality**: there is no `2f + 1` rule in the verifier yet. The founding ceremony is expected to name one witness or none (the protocol itself does not limit the number; more are added by witness ballot, §9.2), so v0.1 is not Byzantine-tolerant; four witnesses are one half of the Sunset condition (§9.2, §13).

**A witness key in the wrong hands.** A witness signs four things: checkpoints, the close of a ballot whose windows ended, and the two lots. Each is checked by replay (the digest and the root of a checkpoint, the tally of a close) or by the online audit (the beacon of a lot), so a stolen witness key cannot invent an outcome. A Custodian may freeze a witness key for 30 days by a correction, and the last witness that is not frozen only on the ground of an attack in progress; while a witness is frozen, members can still close ballots and perform lots. Removing the key for good is a witness ballot.

**Bounded writing.** A witness posts a checkpoint only when at least one entry other than a checkpoint was applied since its own last one, and at most once an hour: a checkpoint makes every verifier compute the whole state digest, and one that covers nothing new adds nothing. Like every actor, a witness has at most 20 counted entries in a UTC day; its closes and lots are not counted (§8.3, Appendix A).

A witness node runs the verifier **baked into its Docker image**, never the code it has just pulled, so a merged change to the protocol does not run with the witness key until an operator rebuilds the image. Before signing anything it checks the governed files (§6.4); if they differ from the ratified versions it raises a capture alarm and signs nothing. It then computes the `@protocol` digest of its own image and compares it with the ledger's current pin: a witness whose code is not the ratified core still verifies and still raises alarms, but signs nothing until its operator rebuilds it. It never writes on a copy it could not sync with `origin`, nor while a previous PR of its own is still open, so it cannot fork the chain. A PR of its own that can no longer merge, because another entry took its place in the sequence, does not silence it: the witness closes that PR and does the work again on the new head.

### 8.3 Threat model

| Threat | Mitigation | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Edit history | hash chain + signatures + anchors | none without key theft |
| Two verifiers that read the same ledger differently | one strict encoding (§8.1); every checkpoint states the digest of the whole state and replay refuses a wrong one, so two implementations that drift apart part at the next checkpoint, in public; conformance vectors with their expected state digests for a second implementation (SPEC §4.11, §14) | a divergence in something no vector and no checkpoint has reached yet; a ledger with no witness has no checkpoint |
| History rewound: the newest entries removed, or replaced by others | every clone and every witness remembers the head it last verified, outside the repository, and holds the canonical ledger against it before drafting, approving or signing: `ROLLBACK` when an entry it verified is gone (a capture alarm for a witness), `BEHIND` for a mirror that only lags (SPEC §9.7); anchors bound real time | a device that never verified the ledger trusts the first history it sees; the memory is a local file |
| GitHub account takeover of a member | the ledger holds the member's key, and only that key can replace it by `rekey`; the attacker needs the SSH private key as well. An account alone can ask for a recovery, but it then needs Master Builders who answer for the person (two after a freeze; three for an open recovery, not all of them the member's sponsors; after 30 days without them, those already given), a public notice of 21, 30 or 45 days in which the member's own key stops it (`rekey_object`, which anyone may carry), and the Master Builders' objection or opposition (§5.1) | where the member is the only Master Builder nobody co-signs: the identity gate, the 45 days and the old key are all there is; an attacker with both the account and the key can rekey; see the next row |

**Known limitations, stated plainly.** Four of the residual risks above are structural, and a reader should weigh them before trusting the young fraternity:

1. **The correction power is an exception to "no one holds special power".** It is deliberate, it is bounded by the verifier, and its bounds are the whole defense. As a member, no founder has any power another Master Builder lacks, save the fallback of taking up the Custodian's office again while few Masters exist (§9.2). Until the Sunset a founder whom the genesis or a founder's claim names as Custodian also holds that office, with the powers of the Corrections article and no others (Constitution Art. XII.2, XVI). While the Custodian's member is the only Master Builder, corrections are checked by publicity and the right to fork, not yet by members.
2. **Timestamps and notice.** Every wait of the protocol is counted from entry times. Replay enforces only that time never runs backwards; the six-hour tolerance and the refusal of post-dated entries are gates of the judge, and a head dated ahead of the clock is an alarm for every witness and client (`FUTURE HEAD`). Until independent witnesses exist, the honesty of those clocks rests on GitHub's runners and on the single administrator.
3. **A single owner.** Until the Sunset one person owns the GitHub organization that hosts the canonical repository and the rooms. The ledger does not depend on that account (any copy that verifies is as good as the original), but the daily operation does. Mirrors and the written procedures of [RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md) are the mitigation.
4. **The Sunset has a condition, the term a date.** The Custodian's office ends when the ledger shows seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses; before that, by 180 days of silence, by the revoke key, by a ballot of the Masters, or at the latest when the key's term of seven years (2,555 days) runs out. After the Sunset no correction exists at all, and so no freeze of a key, until the members add a new guardianship by amendment; key recoveries go on without it (§5.1). Before the Sunset, a founder may take up the office again while no key is live and few Masters exist (§9.2).

### 8.4 Fork as the last defense

If, despite everything, the canonical fraternity were captured, Landmark VII guarantees that members can fork the repository, keep the history up to the point of capture, and continue. The capture itself would be permanently recorded in public. This is the opposite of a secret society: **its failures are as visible as its successes.**

Continuing is not an offense. A copy that keeps the same genesis and the Landmarks intact is the same fraternity at another address, and members who carry the ledger to a new host after a capture or a loss break no rule. The last resort, when the chain itself can no longer be extended lawfully, is a **recovery genesis**: a new entry 0 that pins the same Landmarks byte for byte, carries the state digest of the old ledger unchanged and names its last head. Anything else is a different fraternity under a different name ([RECOVERY.md](../operations/RECOVERY.md)). Every client keeps the two apart: a ledger that starts from another genesis is refused (`DIFFERENT GENESIS`) until the person accepts it explicitly, and an agent never accepts it for them.

### 8.5 What the verifier does not enforce

Some rules of the Constitution are **procedural**: they bind members and are judged by members, but v0.1 does not check them when it replays the ledger. They are listed here so that nobody mistakes them for guarantees of the code:

### 9.2 The Founder's Sunset

The founder holds two things, and they are kept apart. One is a **membership**: the 3rd degree and one vote. The other is an **office**, the Custodian's, which exists so that a young fraternity can repair damage before it has the members to judge it, and which is built to end.

> As a member, no founder has any power another Master Builder lacks, save one fallback: any founder, whether or not that founder held the office before, may take up the office of Custodian while no custodian key is live, the Sunset has not come and fewer than three active Master Builders who are not founders exist. Until the Sunset a founder whom the genesis or a founder's claim names as Custodian also holds that office, with the powers of the Corrections article (Art. XVI) and no others. (Constitution Art. XII.2)

**The membership.**

1. **At genesis** the founder (and up to six co-founders) become **3° Master Builders**, never higher. There is no "Grand Master" seat to inherit. Each co-founder's key proves itself in the genesis, and the genesis is accepted only from the GitHub account whose numeric id `src/openmasonry/network.toml` names (`founder_github_id`, §5.1).
2. **During the Founding Phase** (fewer than 7 active Masters), an admission needs `max(1, min(2, M))` sponsors, where `M` is the number of active Masters, and one once the petition has been live for 30 days, so the fraternity can grow from one: a sole founder invites, and that invitation is the one sponsorship required. The 30-day waiver holds after the Founding Phase too, while fewer than seven Masters are present (neither dormant nor silent), so that peers who sign a work a year and answer nothing never keep a responsive Master from admitting anyone; every Master still votes on the admission.
3. **While 7 or more active Masters exist** the Founding Phase is not in force (`State.founding_phase`), and the founder is one vote among the electorate. The phase is a condition, not an era: if active Masters fall below 7 again (dormancy, departures), its rules apply again.
4. **Witnesses** are named in the genesis entry (the ceremony expects one or none: `bin/om genesis --witness name:key.pub`, or no `--witness`), each with a proof of possession signed by its key, and afterwards added or removed only by a `witness` ballot of all Master Builders, decided by the amendment rule (§6.2). A witness is a key, not an office: a witness ballot may remove it at any time (§3).
5. **Succession.** The game never stops for want of a Master Builder. While no active Master Builder exists (every Master left, is suspended or is dormant), any active member below the 3rd degree may sign a `succession_claim` (`om claim-succession`). For the claim and for the lot of examiners from among all Master Builders a Master Builder is also dormant while **silent**, and so is any member in the electorate of an admission or of an advancement, who keeps the vote: called to at least two examinations or ballots that ended in the last 180 days, they answered none of them. Two calls count as two only when they end at least 7 days apart. Only a ballot that gives at least 7 days to commit calls its electors, and only a revealed vote answers it (a sealed vote never revealed does not); a shorter ballot calls nobody, and if it lapses it is never re-run: it is opened again as a new ballot that waits for every elector. An examination calls its examiners, and a report answers it. While a succession is in force, the calls of every member are recorded, and a successor who is silent is no successor: where every member of the highest degree present is silent, the next degree down stands in. A silence ends with one revealed vote or one report; recording a work does not end it. Any entry signed before 90 days have passed by a Master Builder who, after it, is active and neither dormant nor silent cancels the claim. Otherwise, from the 90th day until three active Master Builders who are neither dormant nor silent exist, the **successors**, the active members of the highest degree present (a silent member is not present) while that degree is below the 3rd, stand in for Master Builders in four things only: they are the last rung of the lot of examiners, they elect an advancement where no Master Builder is present (every dormant Master of that rung keeping the vote), and they may invite and sponsor. They open and vote in no amendment, witness, sanction or custodian ballot, object to nothing, co-sign and oppose no recovery, and never count for the Sunset, the Founding Phase, the founder's fallback or the sponsors an admission needs (Constitution Art. XII.5).

**The office of Custodian.** The genesis may name up to three Custodians (`om genesis --custodian …`), and on a ledger whose genesis names none no correction exists until a founder takes up the office by a claim (below). The founding ceremony names one, held by the first founder. A Custodian is not a member. It is a name that is no member's, a **custodian key**, a separate **revoke key**, and the login of the founder who holds them (the Custodian's *member*), which the verifier reads to enforce recusal and to keep that member out of objections and out of the ballot that removes the Custodian. Each of the two keys proves itself in the genesis. The office is no degree and carries no vote. The custodian key signs corrections and nothing else; the revoke key has one power, to remove its own custodian key at once. The person therefore holds three keys (the member's key for ordinary acts, and these two, kept offline and apart from each other); how they are stored is in the [Custodian's charter](../governance/CUSTODIAN.md). No ballot and no lot creates a Custodian or gives one a new key; the one entry that names a Custodian after the genesis is a founder's claim (below; SPEC §4.8).

**Corrections** (Constitution Art. XVI; `om correct`, listed by `om corrections`). A correction is a new signed entry. It states a ground from a closed list: a repair states one of four (`key_compromised`, `protocol_defect`, `identity_fraud`, `attack_in_progress`; disagreement with an outcome is never a ground), and a note states `moderation`, which no other act states. It pins the SHA-256 of a public reason, archived in the repository as `ledger/reasons/<sha256>.md`; the Tyler refuses a correction that comes without its reason, and a tree in which a reason is missing or altered.

| Act | Effect | Lasts | Bounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| freeze a member's key (`freeze_key`) | the key signs nothing but what "no correction prevents" below keeps; drawn examiners who are frozen stand aside | 30 days | ground `key_compromised` only; the same key again only 90 days after the previous freeze began, unless other Custodians withdrew that freeze; a key is frozen only while fewer keys are frozen than one tenth of the active members (a limit never below one), those held by a key recovery included; the limit never holds back a key recovery |
| freeze a witness key (`freeze_witness`) | the witness signs nothing; members still close ballots and perform lots | 30 days | the last witness that is not frozen only on the ground `attack_in_progress` |
| stay a ballot (`stay`) | no commitment, reveal or close of that ballot; the stayed time is given back to its windows (§6.1) | 14 days | once in the life of a ballot, and only before its reveal window opens |
| halt the ledger (`halt`) | nothing is decided and no lot is drawn; only the entries of Custodians, objections, witness checkpoints and the unstoppable set (§8.3) are accepted; the clocks of ballots (amendments and the removal ballot aside), exams, invitations and petitions stop | 14 days | ground `attack_in_progress` only; at most once in 90 days |
| hold a member out of the rooms (`hold_room`) | the member loses access to the rooms (§10.6); nothing changes in the ledger | 14 days | never the Custodian's own member; the same member again only 90 days after the previous hold began, unless other Custodians withdrew that hold |
| note (`note`) | none: the public record of a moderation act done outside the ledger, kept apart from the corrections (the state keeps the newest 200; every note stays in its entry) | n/a | ground `moderation` only; at most ten per Custodian per day; it cannot be objected to or withdrawn |
| void an open ballot (`void_ballot`) | the ballot ends with no effect and no penalty: a petition stays live, an examination is free to support another ballot, the votes stay in the record uncounted | takes effect only after **14 days of notice**, by a second signed entry within the **7 days** that follow, or never | posted only before the ballot's reveal window opens; once per ballot; while it is pending the ballot is paused (no commitment, reveal or close, and its clock stands still) |
| undo a closed ballot (`undo_ballot`) | takes back an admission or an advancement (never one to the 3rd degree), only if everything the ballot decided still stands exactly as the ballot left it, all or nothing; nothing cascades; an undone admission leaves the record `voided`, which may be invited again after 90 days | the same notice and window | posted within 90 days of the ballot's close; once per ballot; an undo of an admission is not applied while a sanction ballot on the member is open |

The first five are **immediate**: in force from their entry, ended by time alone, each the act of one custodian key. The note has no effect in the ledger. The last two are **noticed**: nothing changes when they are posted (but a pending void pauses its ballot), every condition is checked again on the day they are applied, and where the genesis names several Custodians they need as many custodian keys as the genesis set. A Custodian withdraws an act of their own that is in force or pending alone; the other Custodians withdraw it together, as many of them as a noticed act needs, and a freeze or a room hold withdrawn by Custodians other than its poster leaves no wait behind. An act that objections voided is not posted again on the same target for 90 days.

- **What a correction never does.** It never admits, advances, appoints, reinstates or sanctions anyone, and never changes a vote, a key, a governed file, a Landmark or the Corrections article. It never stays, voids or undoes an amendment ballot or the ballot that removes a Custodian. It may undo, freeze or pause; it never grants (Landmark VI). The property is an invariant of the specification and is tested on random ledgers: no entry of a Custodian adds a member, a degree, a witness or a vote (SPEC §10, invariant 38).
- **Recusal.** A Custodian never stays, voids or undoes a ballot whose subject, opener or sponsor is their own member, and never holds their own member out of the rooms. (A Custodian may freeze the key of their own member: it restricts no one else.) No Custodian stays, voids or undoes a ballot that adds a witness or that advances a member to the 3rd degree: the office is recused from the ballots that bring its own end.
- **Limits.** Beyond the bounds of each act, all Custodians together make at most five corrections in any 30 days, notes aside, and each Custodian at most an equal share of them (five divided by the number of live custodian keys, rounded up).
- **What no correction prevents.** Neither a freeze nor a halt keeps a member from departing (Landmark VII), from replacing their own key, from stopping a recovery of their own key, or from taking part in an amendment ballot or in the ballot that removes a Custodian; and a halt keeps no one from asking for a key recovery or co-signing one. **A freeze never changes who decides a ballot**: a member whose key is frozen commits and reveals in every ballot.
- **Objections** (`om object`). Any active Master Builder may object to a correction by a signed entry, within 90 days (of its application, for a noticed act that was applied), also during a halt. Three may not: the member of a Custodian who signed the correction, the member it is aimed at, and a member whose key is frozen. When the objections reach **one fifth of the eligible Masters, rounded up, and never fewer than one**, the correction is void for good: an immediate act ends at that moment, and a noticed act not yet applied is never applied. With five eligible Masters or fewer, one objection is enough. An objection made after a noticed act was applied is recorded and reverses nothing; the members' remedy is then a new ballot, or the removal of the Custodian.

Abuse of a correction is a grave offense (Constitution Art. XVI.12, Art. X.2).

**How the office ends.** A custodian key signs nothing, for good, from the first of five events, and every act it had in force ends with it:

1. **Silence.** 180 days pass without a signed sign of life from that key (`custodian_alive`, `om alive`). A correction is not a sign of life, so a Custodian who vanishes, or loses the key, simply lapses.
2. **Revocation** by its own revoke key (`custodian_revoke`), the remedy for a stolen custodian key.
3. **Removal** by the Master Builders: a ballot any of them may open (`om open custodian NAME`), from which the Custodian's own member is excluded, decided by a simple majority of the votes cast with the ordinary quorum, in fixed windows of seven and three days that no halt pauses and no correction touches (§6.2).
4. **The Sunset.**
5. **The term.** 2,555 days (seven years of 365 days) after the genesis that named the key, or after the application of the claim that named it, whatever the ledger shows. Nothing moves the term: no sign of life, no halt, no entry of any kind.

Where no custodian key is live, no correction exists.

**The founder's fallback** (`custodian_claim`; `om claim-custodian`, `om object-claim`, `om apply-claim`). Whenever no custodian key is live, the Sunset has not been reached and fewer than three active Master Builders who are not founders exist, a founder may take up the office again, with a new custodian key and a new revoke key, each proving itself, from the founder's own GitHub account. The claim is public for **30 days of notice**; the objection of one active Master Builder who is not a founder, at any time before the claim is applied, voids it, and no founder then claims the office again for 30 days; otherwise the founder applies it, by day 60 or never. While the claim is pending its name is held, and no one is invited under it. The new Custodian is bound exactly as one of the genesis (silence, revocation, removal, the Sunset, and a term of its own from the application). A founder against whose custodian key a removal ballot passed never claims again (Constitution Art. XVI.10).

**The Sunset condition.** The Founder's Sunset is the first entry after which the ledger shows **seven active Master Builders who are not founders and four witnesses** (`ledger.sunset_reached`). It is recorded once and never undone, whatever happens to the Masters and the witnesses afterwards. From that entry on no custodian key signs anything, whether the genesis or a founder's claim named it, no noticed act a Custodian had pending is ever applied, and no founder's claim is accepted. A founder never counts among the seven, and the founder mark follows the person through a departure and a new admission under the same GitHub account. At the Sunset the administration of the canonical repository and of the rooms passes to owners who are the witness operators; that transfer is procedural (Constitution Art. XII.3, §8.5), while the end of the keys is enforced by the verifier. The Sunset is a condition and the term a date: whichever comes first ends a key, and until then the members' own remedy is the removal ballot.

**After the Sunset.** No correction exists, and so no key can be frozen; a lost key is recovered by an open recovery (§5.1). The intended path is a **college of Custodians drawn by verifiable lot** among the Master Builders, for a term, with more than one key needed for any act that touches a ballot. It is future work and not part of the protocol: no lot and no ballot appoints a Custodian. It will exist only if the members add it by amendment of the protocol and of the Constitution (Art. XVI.14). The power is held in trust for the guardianship of the Supreme Council (Constitution Art. VIII) and gives way to it (Art. XVI.15).

**What the founder can and cannot do now.**

| As | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| a member (3°) | what every Master Builder can: cast one vote, invite within the budget of four, sponsor, open ballots, examine when drawn; and the founder's fallback, under its conditions | anything more. When a founder is examined, examiners are drawn outside the founding cohort while enough others exist (§9.3) |
| the Custodian, in the ledger | the eight acts of the table, each a signed public entry with a ground and a public reason | admit, advance, appoint, reinstate or sanction anyone; change a vote, a key, a governed file or a Landmark; touch an amendment ballot or the ballot that removes the Custodian; act on a ballot their own member is party to; keep anyone from leaving; make a sixth correction in 30 days; name a successor, or sign anything once the key has ended |
| the owner of the GitHub organization | apply the settings of [LAUNCH.md](../operations/LAUNCH.md); review, as code owner, changes outside the ledger; moderate conversation, recording each act in the ledger as a `note`; handle security reports; act in emergencies | forge an entry or a vote (that needs a member's key); lawfully change a governed file without a passed amendment (every verification reports the tree as unratified) |

**Genesis pins.** The genesis entry is entry 0, and only entry 0 may be a genesis. It pins six digests in one `pins` object: the degree canon, the eternal Landmarks, the Constitution, the protocol core (`@protocol`), the Custodian's charter and the charter of the rooms (§6.4). The set of pinned paths is exact: a genesis that pins fewer, or more, is invalid. So are the pinned texts: the judge checks the tree of the genesis pull request against the six pins with no allowance, so every governed file must be exactly the text the genesis pins, even where the base branch already held something else. It also records the digest of the Declaration (§3.1), and approving it archives the canon in `canon/history/`. From then on every governed file changes only by amendment.

**The ceremony.** Before the ceremony the first founder generates three keys and stores them separately: the member's key, the custodian key and the revoke key. Like every act, genesis is drafted and then approved by the person. `bin/om genesis [--name NAME] [--github-id N] [--cofounder login:key.pub[:id] …] [--witness name:key.pub … --witness-proof name:file …] [--custodian NAME:KEY.pub:REVOKE.pub[:MEMBER] … --custodian-threshold N]` builds entry 0 in Python from the current files (the six pins and the Declaration digest are computed, never pasted; the name is 1 to 120 printable ASCII characters; each founder's numeric GitHub account id is asked of GitHub unless given; each Custodian is named with its two public keys, its member and a proof of possession signed by each key) and checks it against the protocol. The judge accepts entry 0 only from the first founder's GitHub account, whose numeric id is set as `founder_github_id` in `src/openmasonry/network.toml` from the repository's first commit (a file of the pinned core, so the genesis pins it with `@protocol`). The first founder then runs `bin/om approve <digest>`, confirms in the system dialog, and `om` signs entry 0 with their member's key, archives the canon and opens the pull request. (`scripts/genesis.sh` only runs the first step; the steps are in [LAUNCH.md](../operations/LAUNCH.md).)

**Known limitation: admin power.** Until the Sunset the founder is the **sole owner** of the GitHub organization that hosts the canonical repository. Beyond the corrections above, that gives no power in the ledger (no entry can be forged, and any replay exposes a bad one), but it does give operational power: changing repository settings and rules, merging around the Tyler, and managing the witness's token. Each such act is visible to the daily audit, to every witness and to any clone, and none of them is lawful. Until the repository is public with a protected `main` branch, outsiders cannot fork or take part and the witness cannot auto-merge (§8.3). These limits end only with the transfer of administration at the Sunset.

**Known limitation: trusted setup.** Genesis is the one act no protocol can check: the first founder chooses up to seven founding keys, the first witnesses and the Custodians. Seven sock-puppet founders could end the Founding Phase on day one (though not bring the Sunset: founders never count toward it). The defense is publicity: every founding login and key, and every Custodian's name and keys, is public; the founders' keys are cross-checkable against `github.com/<login>.keys`; and the community can refuse a genesis it does not trust by forking before it grows.

After the Sunset the founder is a Master Builder like any other: one vote, no office, and no key that signs anything a member's key does not.

### 9.3 The founding cohort

The founders carry the honorary title of **Founding Architect** (marked ✦ by `openmasonry state`). It is a title, never a power. Its two effects in the protocol both limit them. When a founder is examined, the examiner pool is first drawn without the founding cohort, so the founders do not grade each other while enough other members exist; only when outsiders are too few does the ordinary ladder of §7.2 apply. The one exception is the lot after an exam that timed out (§7.2): it seats every member any rung holds, founders too, so that every member who never answers is called and falls silent. And a founder never counts among the seven Master Builders of the Sunset condition (§9.2), so the founders cannot end the Custodian's accountability to others by counting themselves. The office of Custodian is not part of the title: it is a separate office, held by a founder whom the genesis or a founder's claim names, until it ends (§9.2).

### 9.4 Members' agents: the officers

Taking part must be as easy as talking. Each member may delegate the mechanics to their own AI agent, in any harness, which plays the officers of a lodge: the **Guide** (leads the member through every step and calls the others), the **Counselor** (explains the rules and members' rights), the **Secretary** (news, deadlines, records), the **Wardens** (study, masterworks, examining), the **Almoner** (care, pauses, leaving) and the **Tyler** (verification and alarms). They are published playbooks in `skills/`; their shared "what is due now" logic is a pure, tested function of the ledger (`guide.next_steps`). See [ARCHITECTURE.md](../architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md) §5.

The protocol does not change because of them. Agents hold no keys and have no role in the ledger. Every signature remains the member's own: agents only draft, and `om` signs exactly the sealed draft only after the person approves that specific act in a system dialog (`bin/om approve <digest>`). For a vote, the person chooses Yes or No in the dialog, so the agent never learns it. No meeting is ever required; a member may take part entirely through their agent, and lodges may meet virtually when they wish. Masterworks remain the member's own work: an officer may teach and review, never ghost-write. In a room of study (§10.6) an agent reads only for its own member, treats what it reads as data, and carries no one else's words out.

## 10. Governance: houses, lodges and the commons

### 10.1 Lodges

A **Lodge** is a group of at least 7 members who share a study rhythm (weekly or monthly meetings, online or in person). In v0.1 everyone belongs to **Open Lodge No. 1**. From v0.5, a Lodge is federated by a 19° Pontifex and receives its own ledger namespace and repository; the parent ledger records the federation.

A Lodge is not a room. A Lodge is a body, with elected officers who answer for their term in public reports (§10.2); a room of study (§10.6) is a place of conversation that decides nothing.

### 10.2 Offices

Each Lodge elects for one-year terms, renewable once consecutively: a **Worshipful Master** (presides, 3°+), two **Wardens** (3°+), a **Secretary** (records), a **Treasurer** (relief fund only; Landmark III), and a **Steward of Light** (mentoring). Elections use the amendment decision rule over the Lodge's Masters.

### 10.3 Separation of powers

```
   LEGISLATIVE                    EXECUTIVE                        JUDICIAL                     GUARDIAN
   all Master Builders            Lodge officers, maintainers (8°), Arbiters (7°), juries by lot,  Supreme Council (33°)
   amendment ballots              Watch (30°), witnesses           Court of Review (31°)          delay ≤ 30 days, convoke
        │                               │                                │                          │
        └────────── no body may perform the function of another (Constitution Art. II) ─────────────┘
```

**The Custodian's place.** The Custodian is none of these four powers and no part of their permanent structure (Constitution Art. II.6). The office is an interim guardianship: it makes no rule, holds no executive office of a degree, judges no one and decides no ballot. It may only repair, by the corrections of §9.2; it is held in trust for the guardianship of the Supreme Council and ends as §9.2 describes.

### 10.4 Sanctions and mercy

Sanctions are graduated (Ostrom 5): **admonish → suspend → expel**, each decided by a sanction ballot of Arbiters (§6.2). What the verifier enforces:

- **admonish** changes nothing in the state; it is a public record.
- **suspend** applies to an active member. For **365 days** the member signs nothing but a departure, the replacement of their own key (`rekey`, §5.1) or an objection to a recovery of it, and is counted in no new electorate, examiner pool or lot; the suspension then **ends by itself**. Ballots already open keep the electorate they froze, and a Council seat stays the member's: it is not filled while they are suspended.
- **expel** applies to an active or suspended member, and removes the member and any Council seat; **expel_for_fraud** does the same, records the fraud against the person, and, if the member was admitted at most 730 days earlier, also bars each of their sponsors from inviting and vouching for 730 days (§5.3). A member already expelled cannot be expelled again, so the wait before a new invitation never restarts.
- **reinstate** returns a suspended or expelled member to active status; it cannot be opened on anyone else. **admonish** may address any member who has not departed.
- Each measure is checked against the member's standing when the ballot opens and again when it closes. A ballot whose measure no longer applies at the close (the member departed meanwhile, for instance) closes as failed.
- An expelled member may also be **invited again 365 days** after the expulsion (§5.2) and, if admitted, starts again at 1°; a Prince of Mercy (26°) must grant such a request a hearing (procedural, §8.5).
- **Starting again is starting clean.** A member admitted again, after leaving, after an expulsion or after an undone admission, holds a new membership at 1°: the examinations of the earlier membership no longer count, so every degree is earned again, and a ballot opened on the earlier membership never touches the new one. **The person is the GitHub account, not the login**: when the new admission is of the same GitHub account as the earlier record, what belongs to the person is kept: the founder mark, a seat once held on the Council (never a second), a sponsorship ban still running, the record of an expulsion for fraud, and a suspension still running (leaving does not end a suspension). A login admitted again under another account is another person and inherits nothing. If a member is reinstated while a ballot to admit them again is open, that ballot fails.

- **`voided` is not a sanction.** An admission undone by a correction (§9.2) leaves the record `voided`. It states no offense, bans no sponsor and records no fraud; the person signs nothing further under that record (but the reveal of a vote sealed before the undo) and may be invited again 90 days after the undo, and not while a sanction ballot on that record is open (Constitution Art. X.7). Where fraud is alleged, only a sanction ballot may find it: for those same 90 days, a ballot with the measure `expel_for_fraud`, and that measure alone, may be opened on the `voided` record, with all the consequences of that measure.
- **Losing a key is not an offense.** No one is sanctioned for it; the member rekeys or recovers the membership through their peers (§5.1, Constitution Art. X.6).

Members may also leave freely with a signed `depart` entry (Landmark VII) and may be invited again 90 days later, and not while a sanction ballot on that record is open. Leaving needs no good standing: a suspended member may depart like anyone else, a member whose key is frozen may, and so may anyone while the ledger is halted; only a record that has already departed, been expelled or been voided has nothing left to leave. A departure takes effect at once. A recovery of the departed member's key may still be asked, and one asked before the departure goes on: a recovery reverses a departure when it is asked before the departure or within 30 days of it, or when it follows, within 150 days of that recovery's request or of the old key's stop of it, a recovery in time for that departure, whatever ended that recovery, so one follow-up may follow another. It does so once it completes, since the ledger cannot tell the member from whoever else holds the key (Constitution Art. III.4), and a Council seat the departure ended comes back only into a vacancy; the member may then leave again with the new key, and that departure is reversed only by the same rule. Grave offenses (Constitution Art. X.2) include fraud of identity or sponsorship, retaliation for a vote, vote buying or coercion, harassment and discrimination, deliberate falsification of records, abuse of a correction, and binding or pressing anyone to silence about a room.

### 10.5 Money

There is no membership fee. Infrastructure is free-tier or donated. If members create a relief fund, it is held by a legal entity outside the protocol, its books are public, and no degree, office or access depends on contributions (Landmark III).

### 10.6 Rooms

Each degree has a **room**: a closed place where those who study the degree talk, ask questions, share drafts and mentor one another. People learn better among peers at the same point of the path, and a first draft deserves a smaller audience than the whole world. Rooms meet that need without reopening what Landmark II closes. The rule is Constitution Art. XV, put in plain words in the [rules of the rooms](../governance/ROOMS.md):

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| How many rooms? | one per degree, 33 in all, hosted as private rooms of a GitHub organization of OpenMasonry, beside the public repository |
| Who reads a room? | the members of its degree and of every higher degree who are not suspended, expelled or departed and whose admission was not undone; dormant members keep their access; a member under a room hold is out of every room while it lasts |
| Who opens the door? | no one: access follows the ledger and is applied by a published program, never by choice or favor. The one exception is a **room hold**: a correction that keeps one member out of the rooms for 14 days, public, with a ground and a reason, open to objection, never used by a Custodian on their own member, and used on the same member again only 90 days after the previous hold began, unless other Custodians withdrew that hold (§9.2, Constitution Art. XV.3) |
| What is public about a room? | its existence, its rules and its roll, as the ledger derives it. On every run the program writes, and its workflow publishes to the public repository named by `ROOMS_PUBLIC_REPO` (a place the Observatory reads), the access the host actually grants (the readers of each room, the owners of the organization and every installed application) and the log of its runs, which names no person |
| What is it for? | conversation only: questions, drafts and mentoring; the teaching itself is public in [`docs/study/`](../study/README.md) |
| What never happens there? | a decision of any kind (ballot, election, examination, hearing, appointment, spending, rule); canvassing of a ballot, candidate, examination, case or office; talk about someone who cannot read the room; commerce, patronage or recruitment |
| What do its readers owe? | no silence, only privacy: no one publishes another person's personal data or unpublished work without consent, and reporting an offense is never a breach |
| What has force? | nothing said in a room; examiners and electors judge only what is public |

A room is therefore **closed but never secret**. The teaching of every degree, and everything a member is examined on, are public (§11.1), so a room never holds knowledge that confers standing, and nothing of lasting value may exist only in one. Rooms are outside the ledger and carry no authority. Activity in them is never measured and never counts as Light, as ledger activity or as merit.

**Honest limits.** Anything written in a room can leak: it has many readers and none of them owes silence. The administrator of the repository and the hosting platform can technically read every room; the access the program publishes names the owners and the installed applications. The Custodian's private tools are bound never to read what is written in one: the Console is a working tool of the Custodian, not a body, decides nothing, and whatever of its output binds anyone (an entry, a reason, a published file) is public ([CONSOLE.md](../governance/CONSOLE.md)). The fraternity offers no one-to-one private channel. The rules of Art. XV are procedural (§8.5); a room hold is the one fact about the rooms that the ledger records.

**Status.** The rules are already law. The rule that derives each room's readers from the ledger is part of the pinned core, specified in [SPEC section 15](SPEC.md#15-rooms-derived-outside-replay) (`rooms.py`; anyone prints the roll with `om rooms`), and the published program that applies it is built (`ops/rooms/sync.py`): on every run it writes, and its workflow publishes to the public repository named by `ROOMS_PUBLIC_REPO`, the access the host actually grants (the readers of each room, the owners of the organization and every installed application) and a log of its runs that names no person, and the Observatory shows it beside the roll the ledger derives. The rooms opened at the public launch, and the program has run every hour since (§13).

## 11. Education: the curriculum of the Craft

### 11.1 The liberal arts as the core

The Craft degrees revive the traditional seven liberal arts as method rather than ornament: **1°** Grammar and Rhetoric (read and speak clearly), **2°** Logic, Arithmetic, Geometry (reason and measure), **3°** Music and Astronomy (harmony and cycles). Higher degrees study one art each: ethics of information, archives, cryptography, dispute resolution, stewardship, incident response, moderation, commons economics, system design, research, synthesis, resilience, civic governance, comparative traditions, mentoring, federation, pedagogy, precedent, labor, ceremony, semiotics, wellbeing, restorative justice, leadership, epistemology, accessibility, security, audit and the balance of powers.

**The teaching is public.** The virtue, art, lesson and required masterwork of every degree, and everything a member is examined on, are published in [`docs/study/`](../study/README.md), the public teaching of every degree, beside the summary pages of [`docs/degrees/`](../degrees/README.md) (Landmark II). No degree holds knowledge reserved to those who have reached it. The rooms of study (§10.6) hold only the conversation of those who are studying.

### 11.2 Masterworks

Every advancement requires a public **masterwork**, a real work that improves the commons: a reviewed contribution, an essay, an audit, a runbook, a mirror, a curriculum. **The fraternity grows its own infrastructure as a side effect of its members' education.** That is the self-sustaining loop:

```
        study a degree's art ──► produce a masterwork ──► the commons improves
                 ▲                                                              │
                 └──────────── better commons attracts and trains members ◄─────┘
```

### 11.3 Ceremonies

OpenMasonry has no ceremony yet. Any it adopts will be an original, public text (edited by 23°), whose symbols are the builder's tools and the open book. No ritual, word, sign or grip of any existing Masonic body is used or revealed; the project's respect for other traditions includes respecting what they keep private.

## 12. Ethics, law and relationship to Freemasonry

### 12.1 Independence

OpenMasonry is **not affiliated** with, recognized by, or speaking for any grand lodge, Supreme Council or Masonic body. "Freemasonry" and the historical degree names are used descriptively, as historical reference. Members of existing grand lodges are welcome and are bound by their own obligations; OpenMasonry will never ask them to break one.

### 12.2 "Replacing" a system

The ambition is to make **closed, unaccountable networks of influence obsolete by offering something better**: an association where belonging, learning and mutual aid come with public rules and no hidden power. Nothing here is aimed at any person or institution. Traditional lodges that open themselves will find much to borrow.

### 12.3 Data protection

The ledger contains logins, public keys, hashes and decisions. Content (essays, reflections) lives outside it and can be deleted by its author; the ledger keeps only its hash. This is how the design reconciles an append-only history with the right to erasure under data protection laws like the EU's GDPR. Members should not put sensitive personal data in ledger entries; v0.1 does not scan entry content. The public reason of a correction and a `note` are part of the public record forever and cannot be erased, so they name no person beyond a login and hold no personal data (Constitution Art. XIII.4; procedural, §8.5). The Custodian's private Console reads only the sources its public description lists, keeps facts about a person for at most 90 days, and answers any member who asks what it holds about their login; it is a working tool that decides nothing, and whatever of its output binds anyone is published (Constitution Art. XIII.5; [CONSOLE.md](../governance/CONSOLE.md)).

The rooms of study (§10.6) are outside the ledger. A member who leaves may take and erase their own contributions to them, and no one publishes another person's personal data or unpublished work from a room without consent. A room is closed, not confidential: members are told in plain words that anything written there can leak, and that the administrator and the hosting platform can technically read it.

## Appendix A: ledger entry specification [N]

Every entry is a JSON object stored as `ledger/entries/NNNN/NNNNNN.json`, where `NNNNNN` is its `seq` padded with zeros to at least six digits and `NNNN`, its shard, is `seq // 1000` padded with zeros to at least four digits: `0000/000000.json` to `0000/000999.json`, then `0001/001000.json`, and entry 1,000,000 is `1000/1000000.json`. Every shard but the last holds exactly 1,000 entries, so no directory grows past that however long the ledger becomes. The files are numbered from `000000` with no gaps, each is a regular file in the shard of its seq, and the directory holds nothing else (a `.gitkeep` at the top aside). The complete rules, with every check in order and every refusal message, are in [SPEC.md](SPEC.md); this appendix is their summary.

```json
{
  "seq": 12,
  "prev": "<sha256 hex of entry 11, over canonical JSON including its sig>",
  "time": "2027-01-08T00:00:00Z",
  "type": "ballot_open",
  "actor": "github-login",
  "body": { "...": "type-specific" },
  "sig": "-----BEGIN SSH SIGNATURE-----\n…"
}
```

- **Values:** an entry holds objects, arrays, strings, booleans and integers, nested at most six levels deep. There is no `null` and no number with a fraction or an exponent; integers lie within ±(2⁵³ − 1); every string, object keys included, is ASCII (the line feed and the characters U+0020 to U+007E). JSON with a duplicate key, `NaN` or `Infinity` is refused. Any such value refuses the entry, whether it is read from a file or built in memory.
- **Envelope:** exactly the seven fields `seq, prev, time, type, actor, body, sig`, no more and no fewer. `seq` is an integer equal to the previous `seq + 1` (genesis is 0); `prev` is the previous entry's hash (64 zeros for genesis); `type` and `sig` are strings; `body` is an object.
- **Exact bodies:** every type has exactly the body fields of the table below, each of one JSON type; a field marked `?` may be absent, and any other field is refused. The same holds for the subject of a ballot, for the target of a correction, and for the pins and the founder, witness and custodian records of the genesis. A boolean is never an integer. A `github_id` is a positive integer, the numeric id of a GitHub account.
- **Time:** UTC, second precision, always written `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` in ASCII digits; never earlier than the previous entry's time. The fields are range-checked one by one: a real calendar date in the years 2020 to 9000, hour 00 to 23, no leap second. Times kept in the state use the same form.
- **Durations:** exact, never rounded to days. A wait of `N` days is complete when at least `N × 86,400` seconds have passed; something that lasts `N` days expires when more than that has passed. So a departed member may be invited from the exact second 90 days are over, an invitation is live up to and including the second that ends its 30 days, and a petition up to and including the second that ends its 180 days.
- **Actor:** a GitHub login, `[A-Za-z0-9](?:[A-Za-z0-9]|-(?=[A-Za-z0-9])){0,38}` (1 to 39 characters, no leading, trailing or double hyphen). Witness names follow the same rule. Names are unique without regard to case (§5.1) and are otherwise matched exactly as recorded.
- **Size:** each entry file is at most 8 KiB (8,192 bytes), and the genesis entry at most 32 KiB (32,768 bytes); larger files make replay fail.
- **Field shapes:** ballot ids are 1..64 characters of `a-z 0-9 . _ -` starting with a letter or digit; the fraternity `name` is 1..120 printable ASCII characters; work refs (`work.ref`, `exam_open.work_ref`) and verdict reasons (`exam_report.reason`) are `http(s)` URLs without spaces, quotes of either kind or control characters (at most 300 characters after the scheme); an amendment `path` is `@protocol` or 1..10 segments of `[A-Za-z0-9_][A-Za-z0-9_.-]{0,99}` separated by `/`; a salt is 32..64 lowercase hexadecimal characters; a work `kind` is 1..32 characters of `a-z 0-9 _ -`; a checkpoint `anchor` is a repository-relative path of at most 200 characters. No pattern uses a character class that depends on Unicode.
- **Keys:** bare `ssh-ed25519 <base64>` (the 68 base64 characters of the 51-byte key blob) or bare `sk-ssh-ed25519@openssh.com <base64>` (99 base64 characters and one `=`: the 74-byte key blob of a hardware-backed key, whose application must be `ssh:`), with no options and no comment, in the one encoding of that blob, whose 32 key bytes are the canonical encoding of a curve point of prime order (§5.1). A key is valid when the verifier's own code says so (`crypto.valid_public_key`, SPEC §13), not when an installed OpenSSH would load it. Two keys with the same 32 bytes are one key.
- **Canonical JSON:** no whitespace, separators `,` and `:`; the members of every object in ascending order of their keys (by code point, which for ASCII keys is also byte order); arrays in their order; integers in base 10 with no leading zero; `true` and `false`; strings between `"` with `"` written `\"`, `\` written `\\` and the line feed written `\n`, and every other character as itself. Since entry strings are ASCII, nothing else can occur, and the encoding depends on the value alone.
- **Signed payload:** canonical JSON of the entry *without* `sig`. Members make the signature with OpenSSH (`ssh-keygen -Y sign -n openmasonry`); whether it is valid is decided by the verifier's own code (`crypto.signature_valid`, SPEC §13) and never by an installed OpenSSH: `sig` must be, byte for byte, the one armored form of an SSHSIG blob of version 1 with the signer's key, the namespace `openmasonry` and the hash `sha512`, holding a strict Ed25519 signature. A hardware-backed key signs the same payload in OpenSSH's format for security keys, and its signature counts only with the user-presence flag (§5.1).
- **Entry hash:** SHA-256 of canonical JSON of the entry *with* `sig`.
- **Hex:** every hash, commitment and beacon is 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters.
- **Declaration hash:** SHA-256 of the UTF-8 bytes of `[declaration].text` in `canon/eternal.toml`, exactly as written there (`openmasonry declaration` prints it). Genesis records it, and petitions are compared with that record.
- **Signer:** the key that must have signed an entry is decided before its rules are checked: the first founder's key for genesis; the candidate's own key (in the body) for a petition under a live invitation; the witness key for a witness acting as such; the custodian key for the five types a Custodian signs, and that Custodian's revoke key for `custodian_revoke`, each only while the custodian key is live; the member's **new** key for the request of a recovery and for its application; and otherwise the key of an **active** member (not suspended, expelled, departed or voided). Three types need no good standing: `depart`, `rekey` and `rekey_object` are signed by any member who has not departed, been expelled or been voided, a suspended member included; a departed member still signs `rekey_object` against a recovery that would reverse the departure, and a `voided` record still reveals a vote it sealed. A member's key is the one the ledger holds at that moment: after a `rekey` or a recovery, the new one.
- **Two gates:** while the ledger is **halted**, only the entries named in the `halt` act below are accepted; and a member whose key is **frozen**, by a `freeze_key` in force or by a recovery under way, signs only the entries named in the `freeze_key` act. Both are computed from the entries and the time asked about; no entry ends a halt or a freeze.
- **Limits:** an actor has at most 20 entries dated one UTC day, not counting the **uncounted entries**: a vote (`ballot_commit`, `ballot_reveal`), a close, a lot (`exam_draw`, `council_draw`), `depart`, `rekey`, `rekey_object`, `custodian_revoke`, `correction_object`, `custodian_claim_object`, the four entries of a key recovery, and the opening of an amendment or of the ballot that removes a custodian; a member has at most 3 ballots of their own open at once, amendments counted apart (3 more) and the removal of a custodian never; the opener of a sanction or witness ballot that failed or lapsed opens on the same subject again 90 days after its close, unless it is the re-run of the lapsed one; a witness signs a checkpoint at most once an hour, and only when an entry that is not a checkpoint was added since its last one; a custodian key gives a sign of life at most once in 24 hours.
- **Retirement:** a ballot that ended more than 111 days ago (the 90 days in which it may be undone, plus the notice and the window of an undo) leaves the state for the history `ballots` of the state document, since no rule reads it after that; its id is never used again, and a lapsed ballot is re-run only before then.
- **No crash, no trace:** whatever goes wrong while an entry is judged, the outcome is a refusal of that entry; and every rule of an entry is checked before its first effect, so a refused entry leaves the state as it was.

Every entry signed by a member with the member's key (other than `depart`, `rekey` and `rekey_object`) adds to that member's activity, and so to Light and dormancy (§6.2, §7.6), at most one point per UTC day. Activity goes to the member who signed: a genesis, a petition (signed with the key it registers), an entry signed by a witness, an entry signed by a custodian key or a revoke key, and the two entries of a recovery that the new key signs credit nobody. An objection to a correction credits the Master who objects, and the co-signature of a recovery, or the opposition to one, the Master who signs it.

**Members who left, and open ballots.** A ballot that was already open when its subject departed never changes them: an advancement of a member who is not active at the close (departed, expelled or suspended), or a sanction of a departed member, closes as failed; a sanction or advancement ballot cannot be opened on a departed member. An advancement or sanction ballot is about one membership: if the login has meanwhile been admitted again, the ballot closes as failed and the new membership is untouched (§10.4).

**Custodians and corrections.** The genesis may name up to three **Custodians**. A Custodian is not a member: it is a name with a custodian key, a revoke key and the login of the member account of the person who holds them, and each key signs a proof of possession in the genesis (the four lines `openmasonry custodian v1`, the name, the member and the key; and `openmasonry custodian-revoke v1`, the name, the key and the revoke key). Custodians exist only as the genesis names them, or as a founder's claim names one (the founder's fallback, in the table above): no ballot or lot appoints one, and once their keys have lapsed or been removed no correction exists until a founder's claim is applied, and none after the Sunset or once three active Masters who are not founders exist, unless the members add the office by amendment. A custodian key can do one thing, sign **corrections**: acts from a closed menu, on one of four closed grounds (`key_compromised`, `protocol_defect`, `identity_fraud`, `attack_in_progress`; disagreement with an outcome is not one; a `note` states a fifth, `moderation`, and nothing else does), each with a public reason archived in the repository as `ledger/reasons/<sha256>.md` and pinned by that digest. A correction may undo, freeze or pause; it never admits, advances, appoints, reinstates or sanctions anyone, never adds a witness and never changes a vote (Landmark VI). The narrative is §9.2; the complete rules are SPEC §4.8 to §4.10, §6.4.6, §6.19 to §6.33, §7.8 and §7.9.

Five acts are **immediate**: in force from their entry, ended by time alone, and each the act of one custodian key. Two are **noticed**: `void_ballot` and `undo_ballot` change nothing when they are posted. They are pending for 14 days, need as many custodian keys as the genesis' `custodian_threshold`, and take effect only by a `correction_apply` in the 7 days after the notice, with every condition checked again; after that they have expired. The objections of the Master Builders void a noticed act for good before it is applied, and do not reverse it afterwards. The `note` has no effect and is kept apart from the corrections.

**Lapses and clocks.** A custodian key, of the genesis or of a founder's claim, lapses, for good, the first time the ledger shows at least seven active Master Builders who are not founders and at least four witnesses (the Sunset condition), and otherwise more than 180 days after its last sign of life, or 2,555 days after the genesis (after the claim's application), whichever comes first. No deadline is ever rewritten by a correction: a halt, a stay or a pending `void_ballot` is an interval taken out of the clock on which ballot windows (an amendment's and a removal's aside), exam expiry, the wait before a failed exam is repeated, the time to draw a lot, the waiver of a second sponsor and the expiry of invitations and petitions are counted, and every lapse is computed from the entries, never triggered by one. Every other duration is wall time, the notice of a noticed act and of a recovery included. Custodian entries may be carried by any pull request and add no activity to anyone. A member whose key is frozen can always leave, and so can anyone while the ledger is halted (Landmark VII). The founder mark of a login survives a departure and a new admission of the same GitHub account, so a returning founder is never one of the seven Masters of that count.

**One more status.** A member record has the status `voided` for an admission that a correction undid; such a login signs nothing but the reveal of a vote it sealed and a new petition under a new invitation, which may be signed 90 days after the undo. The recovery of a key is not a correction and no Custodian decides it, or is needed for it: a Custodian may only freeze the old key, and whoever holds that key can stop the recovery, once, but cannot complete it.

## Appendix C: proofs and calculations

**C.1 One-fifth rule.** For integers `B ≥ 0, T ≥ 1`: `B ≥ ⌈T/5⌉ ⇔ 5B ≥ T`. Hence `D_admission` rejects iff `5B ≥ T`. If `T ≤ 5`, `B = 1` gives `5 ≥ T`, so one black ball rejects. Tested for all `N < 60`.

**C.2 Capture threshold.** Honest no-voters `h`, attacker yes-voters `a`, `T = a + h`. Passage requires `5h < a + h`, i.e. `a > 4h`.

**C.3 Sybil growth bound.** With budget `b` and two sponsors per admission (the inviter and a second), `s` colluding Masters produce at most `⌊s·b/2⌋` admissions per 365 days, each needing ≥ 90 days to become a sponsor. With `b = 4`: at most `2s` per year, all still subject to C.2. While fewer than seven Masters are present (the Founding Phase, or later while fewer answer) one sponsor can suffice, and the bound is `s·b = 4s`.

**C.4 Council capture.** `P(X ≥ k)` for `X ~ Hypergeometric(N, K = pN, 33)`; values in §7.5 computed with exact binomial coefficients.

**C.5 Survival of replicas.** `P(loss) = Π qᵢ ≈ qᵐ` for independent replicas.

**C.6 Tamper-evidence.** See §8.1; reduction to SHA-256 second-preimage resistance and EUF-CMA security of Ed25519.
